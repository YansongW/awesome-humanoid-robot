## 概述
Tom Burick曾创办White Box Robotics公司，开发了模块化通用机器人平台914 PC-Bot，售出约200台至17个国家。2008年金融危机导致公司于2010年关闭后，他转向教学，先在自闭症青年职业培训项目工作，后于2019年加入PS Academy Arizona。Burick本人患有计算障碍，他将此视为“超能力”，认为其增强了自己的3D空间推理能力。他通过ENIAC复刻等大型项目，让学生利用高度专注和精确重复等特质进行学习，项目共使用近300平方米厚纸板、1600根热熔胶棒和7加仑黑漆，安装了18000个模拟真空管。

## 核心内容
### 从机器人专家到教师
Tom Burick自幼受电视剧《迷失太空》中的机器人角色启发，对机器人技术产生终身热爱。15岁时，他建造了一台150磅重的钢制消防机器人，并因此获得IEEE等机构奖项。他通过联系Carnegie Mellon University的学生和Saint Vincent College的讲师自学，最终选择不读大学，直接投身现实世界的问题解决。

### 创业经历：White Box Robotics
2000年，Burick创立White Box Robotics，灵感来自组装“白盒”PC时发现机器人领域缺乏类似标准化产品。他开发了模块化通用平台914 PC-Bot，其底盘像“乐高盒子”一样可灵活组合：拼接两个躯干可加倍有效载荷，更换驱动系统或传感器头部。他为此申请了实用和设计专利，并与加拿大国防机器人公司Frontline Robotics合并后投产。公司共售出约200台机器人至17个国家，但于2010年底因2008年金融危机倒闭。

### 神经多样性作为超能力
Burick本人患有计算障碍，这使传统数学学习更具挑战，但也赋予他强大的3D空间推理能力。他形容自己“脑中24小时运行着一个CAD程序”，并认为计算障碍是他机器人事业成功的关键。在教学中，他鼓励自闭症学生将神经多样性视为“超能力”，强调需要比他人多一份坚韧，但最终会获得独特的才能和优势。

### ENIAC复刻项目
为纪念ENIAC诞生80周年，Burick带领学生从2025-26学年开始建造全尺寸复刻品。ENIAC由40个大型金属面板组成U形布局，包含20个相同设计的累加器，以及三个用于存储数值常数的函数表和两台穿孔卡机。项目挑战在于重复面板的对称性——任何微小偏差都会累积放大。最终成果使用了近300平方米厚纸板、1600根热熔胶棒和7加仑黑漆，安装了18000个模拟真空管，精确匹配了ENIAC在1950年代被拆解前的原貌。此前，学生们还建造过一辆8英尺长的可驾驶Tesla Cybertruck（配备400瓦立体声系统和低音炮）。Burick计划继续此类项目，可能复刻阿波罗登月任务的相关设备。

Tom Burick has always considered himself a builder. Over the years he’s designed robots, constructed a vintage teardrop trailer , and most recently, led a group of students in building a full-scale replica of a pivotal 1940s computer. Burick is a technology instructor at PS Academy in Gilbert, Ariz., a middle and high school for students with autism and other specialized learning needs. At the start of the 2025–26 school year, he began a project with his students to build a full-scale replica of the Electronic Numerical Integrator and Computer, or ENIAC, for the 80th anniversary of the historic computer’s construction . ENIAC was one of the world’s first programmable electronic computers. When it was built, it was about one thousand times as fast as other machines. Before becoming a teacher, Burick owned a robotics company for a decade in the 2000s. But when a financial downturn forced him to close the business, he turned to teaching. “I had so many amazing people help me when I was young [who] really gave me their time and resources, and really changed the trajectory of my life,” Burick says. “I thought I need to pay that forward.” Becoming a Roboticist As a young child in Latrobe, Pa., Burick watched the television show Lost in Space , which includes a robot character who protects the family. “He was the young boy’s best friend, and I was so captivated by that. I remember thinking to myself, I want that in my life. And that started that lifelong love affair with robotics and technology.” He started building toy robots out of anything he could find, and in junior high school, he began adding electronics. “By early high school, I was building full-fledged autonomous, microprocessor-controlled machines,” he says. At age 15, he built a 150-pound steel firefighting robot, for which he won awards from IEEE and other organizations. Burick kept building robots and reached out for help from local colleges and universities. He first got in touch with a student at Carnegie Mellon University , who invited him to visit campus. “My parents drove me down the next weekend, and he gave me a tour of the robotics lab. ## Content
Tom Burick has always considered himself a builder. Over the years he’s designed robots, constructed a vintage teardrop trailer, and most recently, led a group of students in building a full-scale replica of a pivotal 1940s computer. Burick is a technology instructor at PS Academy in Gilbert, Ariz., a middle and high school for students with autism and other specialized learning needs. At the start of the 2025–26 school year, he began a project with his students to build a full-scale replica of the Electronic Numerical Integrator and Computer, or ENIAC, for the 80th anniversary of the historic computer’s construction. ENIAC was one of the world’s first programmable electronic computers. When it was built, it was about one thousand times as fast as other machines. Before becoming a teacher, Burick owned a robotics company for a decade in the 2000s. But when a financial downturn forced him to close the business, he turned to teaching. “I had so many amazing people help me when I was young [who] really gave me their time and resources, and really changed the trajectory of my life,” Burick says. “I thought I need to pay that forward.” Becoming a Roboticist As a young child in Latrobe, Pa., Burick watched the television show *Lost in Space*, which includes a robot character who protects the family. “He was the young boy’s best friend, and I was so captivated by that. I remember thinking to myself, I want that in my life. And that started that lifelong love affair with robotics and technology.” He started building toy robots out of anything he could find, and in junior high school, he began adding electronics. “By early high school, I was building full-fledged autonomous, microprocessor-controlled machines,” he says. At age 15, he built a 150-pound steel firefighting robot, for which he won awards from IEEE and other organizations. Burick kept building robots and reached out for help from local colleges and universities. He first got in touch with a student at Carnegie Mellon University, who invited him to visit campus. “My parents drove me down the next weekend, and he gave me a tour of the robotics lab. I was mesmerized. He sent me home with college textbooks and piles of metal and gears and wires,” Burick says. He would read the textbook a page at a time, reading it again and again until he felt he had an understanding of it. Then, to help fill gaps in his understanding, he got in touch with a robotics instructor at Saint Vincent College, in his hometown of Latrobe, who let him sit in on classes. Each of these adults, he says, “helped change the trajectory of my life.” Toward the end of high school, Burick realized that college wouldn’t be the right environment for him. “I was drawn to real-world problem-solving rather than structured coursework and I chose to continue along that path,” he says. Additionally, Burick has dyscalculia, which makes traditional mathematics more challenging for him. “It pushed me to develop alternative methods of engineering.” The ENIAC replica Burick’s students built precisely matches what the original computer would have looked like before it was disassembled in the 1950s. Robert Gamboa When he graduated, he worked in several tech jobs before starting his own company. In 2000, he opened a computer retail store and adjacent robotics business, White Box Robotics. The idea for the company came when Burick was building a “white box” PC from standard, off-the-shelf components, and realized there was no comparable product for robotics. So, he started developing a modular, general-purpose platform that applied white box PC standards to mobile robots. “The robot’s chassis was like a box of Legos,” he says. He filed utility and design patents for the platform, called the 914 PC-Bot, and after merging with a Canadian defense robotics company called Frontline Robotics, started production. They sold about 200 robots in 17 countries, Burick says. Then the 2008 financial crisis hit. White Box Robotics held on for a couple of years, shuttering in late 2010. “I got to live my life’s dream for 10 years,” he says. After closing White Box, “there was some soul searching” about what to do next. He recalled the impact his own mentors had, and decided to pay it forward by teaching. ## 핵심 내용
### 로봇 전문가에서 교사로
Tom Burick은 어릴 적 TV 드라마 《로스트 인 스페이스》의 로봇 캐릭터에서 영감을 받아 로봇 공학에 평생의 열정을 갖게 되었다. 15세 때 그는 150파운드 무게의 강철 소방 로봇을 제작했고, 이로 인해 IEEE 등 기관의 상을 받았다. 그는 Carnegie Mellon University의 학생들과 Saint Vincent College의 강사에게 연락해 독학했으며, 결국 대학에 진학하지 않고 현실 세계의 문제 해결에 직접 뛰어들기로 선택했다.

### 창업 경험: White Box Robotics
2000년, Burick은 '화이트 박스' PC를 조립하면서 로봇 분야에는 유사한 표준화 제품이 없다는 점에서 영감을 얻어 White Box Robotics를 설립했다. 그는 모듈식 범용 플랫폼 914 PC-Bot을 개발했으며, 그 섀시는 '레고 박스'처럼 유연하게 조합할 수 있었다: 두 개의 몸체를 연결하면 유효 탑재량이 두 배가 되고, 구동 시스템이나 센서 헤드를 교체할 수 있었다. 그는 이를 위해 실용 및 디자인 특허를 출원했고, 캐나다 국방 로봇 회사인 Frontline Robotics와 합병한 후 양산에 들어갔다. 회사는 약 200대의 로봇을 17개국에 판매했지만, 2008년 금융 위기로 2010년 말에 문을 닫았다.

### 신경 다양성을 초능력으로
Burick 본인은 계산 장애를 앓고 있어 전통적인 수학 학습이 더 어려웠지만, 그 대신 강력한 3D 공간 추론 능력을 갖게 되었다. 그는 자신의 머릿속에 '24시간 CAD 프로그램이 돌아간다'고 표현하며, 계산 장애가 자신의 로봇 사업 성공의 핵심이었다고 본다. 수업에서 그는 자폐 학생들이 신경 다양성을 '초능력'으로 여기도록 격려하며, 다른 사람들보다 한층 더 끈기가 필요하지만 결국 독특한 재능과 강점을 얻게 될 것이라고 강조한다.

### ENIAC 복제 프로젝트
ENIAC 탄생 80주년을 기념하기 위해, Burick은 2025-26학년도부터 학생들과 함께 실물 크기 복제품을 제작하기 시작했다. ENIAC은 40개의 대형 금속 패널로 U자형 배치를 이루며, 동일한 설계의 20개 누산기와 숫자 상수를 저장하는 세 개의 함수 테이블, 그리고 두 대의 천공 카드 기계로 구성되어 있다. 이 프로젝트의 도전 과제는 반복되는 패널의 대칭성에 있었다—아주 작은 오차도 누적되어 커지기 때문이다. 최종 결과물은 약 300제곱미터의 두꺼운 판지, 1600개의 핫멜트 글루 스틱, 7갤런의 검은 페인트를 사용했고 18000개의 모의 진공관을 설치하여, 1950년대에 해체되기 전 ENIAC의 원래 모습을 정확히 재현했다. 이전에 학생들은 8피트 길이의 운전 가능한 Tesla Cybertruck(400와트 스테레오 시스템과 서브우퍼 장착)도 제작한 바 있다. Burick은 이러한 프로젝트를 계속할 계획이며, 아폴로 달 착륙 임무 관련 장비를 복제할 가능성도 있다.
