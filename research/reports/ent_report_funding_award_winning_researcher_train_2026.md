## 概述
Yen-Ling Kuo的职业生涯始于台湾，在台湾大学获得学士和硕士学位后，曾在Google担任软件工程师，参与Shop the Look等AI购物项目。这段经历促使她回到MIT攻读计算机科学博士，师从Boris Katz，并深入参与MIT Center for Brains, Minds, and Machines的跨学科研究。她的研究聚焦于将认知科学中的“心智理论”引入机器人系统，使机器人不仅能理解直接指令，还能解读人类的目光、动作等无声信号。2023年加入弗吉尼亚大学后，她领导Link Lab团队开发了Diff-DAgger方法，该方法通过将扩散损失重新用作实时置信度检查，让机器人能在遇到陌生情况时自我诊断并主动请求人类干预，从而显著提升学习效率和任务成功率。

## 核心内容
### 核心贡献：Diff-DAgger方法
- **问题背景**：传统机器人学习依赖人类示范或DAgger方法（人类实时纠正），但多模型投票策略在复杂模型下难以训练，且模型分歧不一定代表不确定性。
- **创新点**：Kuo团队提出的Diff-DAgger方法基于扩散策略（diffusion policy），将训练过程中的扩散损失（diffusion loss）信号重新用作实时置信度检查。机器人执行任务时，通过统计检验将当前信号与训练数据中的值进行比较：信号异常升高表示遇到陌生情况，触发人类干预；信号平稳则机器人自主决策。
- **关键成果**：失败预测率提升39%，任务完成率提高20%，任务完成速度提升近8倍。

### 实验设置与验证
- 论文发表于IEEE RAS会议，实验涉及机器人操作任务（如抓取、切割等），在环境变化（物体位置、角度改变）场景下测试。
- 与传统的DAgger和robot-gated DAgger方法对比，Diff-DAgger在减少人类干预次数和提升任务成功率上均表现更优。

### 研究延伸与资助
- **NSF Career Award**：获得美国国家科学基金会66.5万美元五年资助，用于构建基于心智理论的人机交互计算模型。
- **Toyota Research Institute Young Faculty Researcher Award**：研究如何让自动驾驶汽车推理与道路和驾驶员的交互。
- 未来目标：开发能通过“接地交互”（grounded interactions）融入人类社交空间的鲁棒机器人。

### 个人背景与IEEE参与
- 学术路径：台湾大学（学士、硕士）→ MIT Media Lab访问学生 → Google软件工程师（2012-2016）→ MIT CSAIL博士（2022，辅修认知科学）→ 弗吉尼亚大学助理教授（2023至今）。
- IEEE角色：2018年首次向IROS投稿，现为IEEE RAS活跃志愿者、论文审稿人及会议演讲者/小组成员，致力于与学生分享经验。

Theory of mind spurs innovation Theory of mind isn’t new, having originated with primatologists studying chimpanzees in the late 1970s. The theory recognizes that others have their own thoughts, beliefs, and perspectives. It’s a skill that allows humans to infer someone’s mental state and predict their behavior without verbal communication. “It’s like when college roommates are moving into their dorm. They may not talk too much, but they work together naturally to coordinate their activities and accomplish goals,” Kuo says. “They can infer and mentally interpret each other’s behaviors and signals to make decisions and complete tasks without words.” She brought her theory of mind research to the University of Virginia when she joined as an assistant professor in 2023. Kuo conducts her research in UVA Engineering’s multidisciplinary cyberphysical Link Lab . Her broad focus is on developing computational models that help robots interpret both direct data and silent signals, from language and movements to a person’s gaze. If successful, it could give robots the same sort of physical and theory of mind reasoning capabilities that power physical and social interactions among humans. “There are no computational frameworks yet available that will translate this kind of understanding into a robot efficiently,” she says. She adds that the process to get there begins with improving how robots learn to perform tasks. The evolution of robot learning Historically, one way robots learned was to mimic humans. A researcher would manually guide a robot through a task, like cutting an apple, and it would repeat the movements. The robot was successful until the environment changed, such as when its hand was in a different position or the apple was at a different angle. The robot was then faced with a situation for which it hadn’t been trained. Without any data available to help it correct course, the robot would start making small errors that eventually led to a full system crash. To solve the problem, researchers developed the dataset aggregation (DAgger) method. As a robot performed a task, a researcher was on standby to provide real-time corrections during unexpected scenarios. The correction data was continuously added to the robot’s model, teaching it how to recover from mistakes. To reduce the human monitoring effort, robot-gated DAgger was created to enable bots to query humans when the machines became uncertain. The most popular approach to make the query decision is to train multiple models to consider when determining a course of action. If the models all agree, the robot proceeds. If they don’t agree, the robot is likely to get stuck and ask for help. Although the multiple model approach was widely adopted, it has limitations. Practically speaking, as models become more complex, it is hard or impossible to train multiple copies. A more fundamental issue is that disagreement among models doesn’t always imply uncertainty; it could just mean there are different ways to accomplish a task. The Diff-DAgger solution That is the gap Kuo’s research team closed with the novel Diff-DAgger research. The approach builds on diffusion policy, a technique that helps robots account for different ways a task can be performed. The new method repurposes diffusion loss, the signal a robot uses to improve its model during training, as a real-time confidence check. During task execution, the robot computes the signal and compares it against values from its training data using a statistical test. The signal spikes when the robot faces an unfamiliar situation and is uncertain how to proceed. The signal stays silent when the robot’s current action is close to what it learned before. The spike represents the robot’s ability to self-diagnose and predict an imminent failure. Human intervention is triggered only when the signal spikes. No spike means the robot can be left to complete its decision-making process on its own. Kuo’s team achieved significant results : Failure prediction rates were improved by 39 percent. Task completion rates were increased by 20 percent, and tasks were completed nearly eight times faster. As service robots and self-driving vehicles become more available, such works are likely to make interactions between humans and robots more intuitive and useful. ## 개요
Yen-Ling Kuo의 경력은 대만에서 시작되었으며, 국립대만대학에서 학사 및 석사 학위를 취득한 후 Google에서 소프트웨어 엔지니어로 근무하며 Shop the Look과 같은 AI 쇼핑 프로젝트에 참여했습니다. 이러한 경험은 그녀가 MIT로 돌아가 컴퓨터 과학 박사 과정을 밟게 된 계기가 되었으며, Boris Katz의 지도 아래 MIT Center for Brains, Minds, and Machines의 학제 간 연구에 깊이 참여했습니다. 그녀의 연구는 인지과학의 '마음 이론(心智理论)'을 로봇 시스템에 도입하여, 로봇이 직접적인 명령뿐만 아니라 인간의 시선, 동작 등 무언의 신호도 해석할 수 있도록 하는 데 초점을 맞추고 있습니다. 2023년 버지니아 대학에 합류한 후, 그녀는 Link Lab 팀을 이끌어 Diff-DAgger 방법을 개발했습니다. 이 방법은 확산 손실(diffusion loss)을 실시간 신뢰도 검사로 재사용하여, 로봇이 낯선 상황에 직면했을 때 스스로 진단하고 인간의 개입을 능동적으로 요청할 수 있게 함으로써 학습 효율과 작업 성공률을 크게 향상시킵니다.

## 핵심 내용
### 핵심 기여: Diff-DAgger 방법
- **문제 배경**: 전통적인 로봇 학습은 인간의 시연이나 DAgger 방법(인간의 실시간 교정)에 의존하지만, 다중 모델 투표 전략은 복잡한 모델에서 훈련이 어렵고, 모델 간 불일치가 반드시 불확실성을 의미하지는 않습니다.
- **혁신 포인트**: Kuo 팀이 제안한 Diff-DAgger 방법은 확산 정책(diffusion policy)을 기반으로, 훈련 과정의 확산 손실(diffusion loss) 신호를 실시간 신뢰도 검사로 재사용합니다. 로봇이 작업을 수행할 때 통계적 검정을 통해 현재 신호를 훈련 데이터의 값과 비교합니다. 신호가 비정상적으로 상승하면 낯선 상황에 직면했음을 의미하여 인간의 개입을 유발하고, 신호가 안정적이면 로봇이 자율적으로 결정을 내립니다.
- **주요 성과**: 실패 예측률 39% 향상, 작업 완료율 20% 향상, 작업 완료 속도 약 8배 향상.
