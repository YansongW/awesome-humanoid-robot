Overview

1X Technologies NEO is a humanoid robot developed by 1X Technologies for home environments, featuring tendon-driven actuators and biomimetic pneumatic muscle actuators, with an emphasis on safe human-robot interaction. What it truly changes is not the robot's locomotion capabilities, but the feasibility of humanoid robots entering the unstructured environment of the home—redefining the safety boundaries of home robots through soft structure and low-noise design.

Content

What It Is: Precise Definition

1X Technologies NEO belongs to the robotic system category, with the English name 1X Technologies NEO. It is a general-purpose humanoid robot designed for home scenarios, developed by the Norwegian company 1X Technologies. NEO's core design philosophy is "Soft and Safe," achieving human-friendly physical interaction through tendon-driven actuators, deformable 3D lattice structures wrapping internal components, and a Knit Suit.

NEO's positioning is not high-load operations in industrial settings, but rather automation of daily tasks in home environments—including washing dishes, cleaning bathrooms, doing laundry, and other household chores. It employs Redwood AI—1X's general-purpose AI model—for task learning and repetitive execution, and supports "Expert Mode," where remote specialists from 1X provide supervised guidance, helping NEO learn new skills while completing tasks.

Why It Exists: Pain Points and Historical Positioning

Since their inception, humanoid robots have faced a fundamental contradiction: industrial scenarios demand high stiffness, high load capacity, and high precision, while home scenarios demand safety, compliance, and low noise. Traditional hydraulic actuation solutions (such as Boston Dynamics' early Atlas) offer high dynamic performance but suffer from issues like loud noise, oil leakage, and complex maintenance, making them unsuitable for home environments.

NEO's creation is a direct response to this contradiction. It chooses a technical path different from mainstream humanoid robots (such as Tesla Optimus and Unitree H1): abandoning high-stiffness joints in favor of a hybrid approach combining tendon-driven actuation and pneumatic muscles. The historical significance of this choice lies in making "safe human-robot interaction" the first design principle, rather than an afterthought—not avoiding collision risks through sensors and algorithms, but making collisions inherently harmless at the physical level.

From an industry chain perspective, NEO belongs to the midstream robotic system segment of the value chain, currently in the validation_markets stage—validating real demand and product feasibility for home scenarios through early users (Early Access).

Principle Breakdown

① Tendon-Driven Actuation: Moving Actuators "Out" of Joints

NEO adopts a tendon-driven actuator approach. Unlike traditional direct-drive or gear-reducer joint motors, tendon-driven systems place motors and reducers in locations away from the joints, such as the robot's torso or forearm, transmitting power to distal joints through high-strength tendons (flexible cables similar to biological tendons). This layout offers two key advantages:

  • Reduced end-effector inertia: Fingers and joints do not carry motor weight, making movements lighter and collision impacts smaller;
  • Inherent compliance: Tendons themselves have elasticity, allowing passive energy absorption during collisions rather than rigidly transmitting impact.

The trade-off is significantly increased complexity in tendon tension calibration, wear compensation, and maintenance—consistent with the technical challenges faced by Shadow Robot Hand.

② Pneumatic Muscles: Another Possibility for Soft Actuation

NEO's design incorporates the concept of pneumatic muscles—flexible artificial muscles driven by air pressure. The working principle of pneumatic muscles is: when the internal air bladder inflates, the geometric constraints of the braided shell cause the muscle to contract along its axis, generating tensile force. Its force-length relationship is nonlinear, naturally exhibiting compliance characteristics similar to biological muscles:

\[ F = P \cdot \frac{3L^2 - b^2}{4\pi n^2} \]

where \(P\) is the inflation pressure, \(L\) is the current muscle length, \(b\) is the braided fiber length, and \(n\) is the number of fiber winding turns. This nonlinear characteristic enables pneumatic muscles to achieve compliant interaction without complex force control algorithms (engineering judgment, based on general principles of pneumatic muscles).

③ Multi-Layer Safety Architecture

NEO's safety design is systematic rather than based on a single technology:

Who Uses It: Application Cases

NEO is currently in the Early Access phase, targeting home consumers. Its typical application scenarios include:

  • Household automation: Users assign NEO a chore list (e.g., cleaning bathrooms, washing dishes), and NEO autonomously completes them within the set time;
  • Expert Mode: For tasks NEO is unfamiliar with, users can schedule remote supervision from 1X specialists, allowing NEO to learn while completing the task;
  • Companion interaction: NEO supports natural language conversation, can answer questions, provide personalized suggestions, and share jokes and stories;
  • Remote operation: Users can control NEO from anywhere in the world via a mobile app or VR devices.

NEO's "Expert Mode" is a noteworthy business model innovation—it combines teleoperation with autonomous learning, ensuring task completion quality while accumulating training data for the robot.

Limitations and Boundaries

  1. Technical maturity: NEO is still in the Early Access phase; its long-term reliability and durability have not been validated through large-scale home deployments (actual deployment data to be confirmed).
  2. Load capacity: The tendon-driven and pneumatic muscle approach excels in compliance, but load capacity and precision are necessarily lower than rigid actuation solutions—a trade-off determined by physical principles.
  3. Maintenance complexity: Tendon tension calibration and wear compensation are core challenges in long-term use, and ordinary home users cannot easily perform maintenance themselves.
  4. Price barrier: The $200 deposit is only a reservation intent; the full unit price has not been disclosed (to be confirmed).
  5. AI capability boundaries: The generality of Redwood AI still requires validation in real-world scenarios; the frequency of switching between "full autonomy" and "Expert Mode" is a key metric for assessing its intelligence level.

Common Misconceptions

  1. "NEO is a soft robot"—Inaccurate. NEO uses a soft outer shell (3D lattice + Knit Suit) and compliant actuators, but its skeleton and transmission system remain rigid structures. The accurate description is "a rigid robot with soft features."
  2. "Pneumatic muscle = pneumatic artificial muscle"—Conceptual confusion. Pneumatic muscle and pneumatic artificial muscle (PAM) are often used interchangeably in academic literature, but NEO actually employs a hybrid approach with tendon-driven actuation as the primary method and pneumatics as supplementary (engineering judgment, based on official claims).
  3. "Safety = softness"—One-sided. NEO's safety is a system-level design: physical compliance + low inertia + transparent status indication (Emotive Ear Rings) work together; "softness" in a single dimension is insufficient to guarantee safety.
  4. "Home robot = general-purpose robot"—NEO's goal is "boring household chores," not a general-purpose home assistant. Its capability boundaries are jointly determined by the AI model and actuators, and it cannot replace humans in all household tasks in the short term.
