Summary

We introduce SAM 3D Body (3DB), a promptable model for single-image full-body 3D human mesh recovery (HMR) that demonstrates state-of-the-art performance, with strong generalization and consistent accuracy in diverse in-the-wild conditions. Institutions per source list: Meta Superintelligence Labs.

Overview

3DB (SAM 3D Body) is the first full-body human mesh recovery model proposed by Meta based on the Momentum Human Rig (MHR) parametric mesh representation. It employs a promptable encoder-decoder architecture, achieving robust full-body pose estimation through independent body/hand decoders and a multi-stage data engine (7 million high-quality annotated images). The core contribution lies in introducing the SAM family's user-guided inference paradigm to the HMR task and systematically addressing the bottlenecks of data quality and diversity.

What It Changes

Existing HMR methods lack robustness on real-world images, frequently failing especially under challenging poses, severe occlusion, or unusual viewpoints, which directly limits their application in practical scenarios such as robotics and biomechanics. The root cause lies in the quality of supervision signals—existing methods rely on noisy monocular pseudo-ground-truth, while laboratory-collected datasets have low diversity and poor pseudo-annotation mesh quality, creating a vicious cycle of "data quality-model performance."

What 3DB truly changes is the HMR training paradigm: instead of passively accepting the limitations of existing datasets, it proactively builds a data engine to mine, annotate, and process hard examples, elevating data quality from "low-fidelity approximations of pseudo-ground-truth" to "high-fidelity annotations via multi-view geometry + differentiable optimization + human review." Simultaneously, it is the first to introduce the SAM family's promptable interaction mechanism to HMR, allowing users to guide inference via 2D keypoints or masks—an innovation not only in interaction but also a practical means to resolve target ambiguity in multi-person occlusion scenarios.

Method Breakdown

Architecture Design

  • Dual-decoder structure: Shared image encoder (ViT-H 632M or DINOv3 840M), independent body decoder and hand decoder, alleviating conflicts between body and hand pose optimization objectives
  • Query token mechanism: Four token types—MHR+camera token, 2D keypoint prompt token, auxiliary 2D/3D keypoint tokens, hand location token—concatenated to form the complete query set T = [Tpose, Tprompt, Tkeypoint2D, Tkeypoint3D, Thand]
  • Hand fusion inference: When hands are detected, the wrist position (hand decoder) and elbow position (body decoder) prompt the body decoder to generate a refined full-body estimate

Data Engine (Core Innovation)

  1. Data quality: 595 dense 2D keypoints, minimizing a composite fitting loss Lfit = Σj λjLj via differentiable optimization (including 2D keypoint loss, initialization anchor regularization, pose/shape priors); multi-view scenarios extended to Lmulti = Σk λkLk (including 3D keypoint loss and temporal smoothness loss)
  2. Data volume: Curating 7 million images from multi-view capture (Goliath 500+ views, Re:Interhand 170 views), licensed image libraries, and synthetic data
  3. Data diversity: VLM-driven mining strategy that automatically generates/updates rules to identify challenging scenarios such as occlusion, unusual poses, interactions, and extreme scales; rules iteratively updated based on failure analysis of the current model (visualized keypoint errors + human annotation)

Training Loss

Ltrain = Σi λiLi, including 2D/3D keypoint losses (L1 with learnable per-joint uncertainty), parameter loss (L2 regression + joint limit penalties), and hand detection loss (GIoU+L1). The 3D keypoint loss employs a warm-up schedule to gradually increase its weight.

Key Innovations

  1. MHR parametric representation: Decouples skeletal pose and body shape, providing richer control and interpretability compared to the SMPL family; this is the first HMR model to use this representation
  2. Promptable inference mechanism: Introduces the SAM family's user-guided paradigm to HMR, supporting 2D keypoints, masks, and camera information as conditional inputs. Ablations show that 2 keypoint prompts improve COCO PCK@0.05 from 86.7 to 93.0, and mask conditioning reduces PVE on Hi4D from 91.4 to 58.3
  3. VLM-driven data engine: Not a static dataset but a dynamic system that automatically iterates and updates mining rules based on model failure analysis—an "active learning" innovation at the data level that directly addresses the data bottleneck in HMR

Experiments and Results

Experiments employ a controlled setup: on standard benchmarks (3DPW, EMDB, RICH, COCO, LSPET), the 3DB-H and 3DB-DINOv3 variants are compared against existing methods such as HMR2.0b, CameraHMR, PromptHMR, and NLF-L+fit; on new-domain datasets (EE4D-Phy, EE4D-Proc, Harmony4D, Goliath, Synthetic, SA1B-Hard), leave-one-out training ensures fair comparison; hand evaluation is conducted on FreiHand; additionally, 2D/3D classification performance analysis, keypoint prompt ablations, mask-conditioned inference ablations, and a large-scale user preference study are included.

With full training data, metrics further improve across domains (e.g., EE4D-Phy PVE 37.0, Harmony4D PVE 41.0, Goliath PVE 34.5, Synthetic PVE 55.2, SA1B-Hard Avg-PCK 76.6). In 3D classification analysis, 3DB significantly outperforms CameraHMR (213.66, 230.51) and PromptHMR (186.35, 186.57) on hard categories such as pose_3d:very_hard (PVE 114.20) and truncation:severe (PVE 126.53). Keypoint prompt ablations show that increasing prompts from 0 to 2 improves COCO PCK@0.05 from 86.7 to 93.0 and reduces EMDB MPJPE from 63.3 to 58.9; at noise scale 0.01 performance is nearly unchanged (90.2 / 60.3), while at 0.1 it drops to 80.9 / 67.8. Mask-conditioned inference on Hi4D reduces PVE from 91.4 to 58.3 and MPJPE from 76.4 to 47.0 (calculated from table values 91.4→58.3, 76.4→47.0, with PVE improvement of 33.1 and MPJPE improvement of 29.4), and SA1B-MP Avg-PCK rises from 67.9 to 72.3. In the user preference study, 3DB's win rates against HMR2.0b, CameraHMR, NLF, PromptHMR, SMPLer-X, and SMPLest-X are 96.2%, 95.0%, 83.8%, 97.5%, 98.8%, and 100.0%, respectively.

  • Comprehensive lead on standard benchmarks: 3DB surpasses all single-image methods on 3DPW and EMDB, with a notable improvement on EMDB PA-MPJPE of 38.5 vs. the best baseline CameraHMR at 43.3 (calculated from table values 43.3→38.5); on RICH, although slightly behind NLF-L+fit which was trained on that dataset, 3DB did not use RICH training data, demonstrating stronger generalization. 2D evaluation achieves SOTA on both COCO and LSPET, with LSPET 68.9 significantly improving over HMR2.0b's 53.3 (calculated from table values 53.3→68.9).
  • Outstanding new-domain generalization: Under the leave-one-out setting, existing baselines alternately rank second across new domains, reflecting their overfitting to narrow data distributions; 3DB leads substantially across all five new domains, with further improvements after full training, indicating that the hard-example distribution mined by the data engine effectively enhances adaptation to unseen domains.
  • Hand accuracy comparable to specialized models: Although 3DB was not trained on FreiHand, its PA-MPVPE of 6.3 approaches specialized hand methods WiLoR at 5.1 and MaskHand at 5.4, and F@15 of 0.988 is extremely close to WiLoR's 0.993 (calculated from table values 0.988→0.993), validating the effectiveness of the unified hand/body decoder design.
  • Robustness validation on hard scenarios: In 3D classification, 3DB shows the largest advantage over baselines on pose_3d:very_hard and truncation:severe categories; mask conditioning brings substantial gains in multi-person interaction scenarios (Hi4D); keypoint prompt ablations demonstrate effective use of 2D prompts with robustness to annotation noise—together supporting 3DB's reliability under extreme poses, severe truncation, and multi-person occlusion.

Boundaries and Limitations

  • Processes each person individually, without modeling multi-person or human-object interactions, limiting accurate interpretation of relative positions and physical interactions
  • Hand accuracy does not exceed specialized methods (HaMeR, WiLoR, etc.) due to not using FreiHand training data
  • Annotation quality from single-view mesh fitting is limited by depth ambiguity and natural occlusion, with lower fidelity
  • MHR inadequately models shapes for atypical age groups such as children
  • Does not surpass NLF on RICH (because NLF uses RICH training data while 3DB does not), with unequal training data in baseline comparisons

Engineering Insights

  • Reproduction priority: First verify the data engine's VLM mining rules and the 595-point dense keypoint detector—these are the core sources of performance differences, but the paper does not provide rule details or detector training code
  • Hand module pitfalls: The hand decoder relies on hand samples with truncated wrists for training; during inference, the hand decoder should be disabled for hand-occluded samples (based on predicted uncertainty) to avoid introducing noise
  • Prompt mechanism usage: 2D keypoint prompts are robust to noise (stable performance at noise scale <0.05), but degrade significantly beyond 0.1 (COCO PCK drops from 86.7 to 80.9); in practice, detector outputs are recommended over manual annotations
  • Mask conditioning: Provides substantial gains in multi-person interaction scenarios (Hi4D, PVE -33.1), but requires segmentation models like SAM2, adding inference pipeline complexity
  • Camera intrinsics: Inference relies on MoGe-2 for FOV estimation, using ground-truth intrinsics for 3D datasets; FOV estimation errors in wild scenarios directly affect absolute error metrics (MPJPE/PVE)
