Overview

Tuopu Group is a leading Chinese supplier of automotive NVH, chassis, and lightweight structural components, and is extending its precision machining, die-casting, and electromechanical integration capabilities into the humanoid robot structural components and actuator domain. As a Tier 1 supplier in the actuator assembly and structural components direction, it undertakes actuator assembly outsourcing from robot OEMs, making it a representative enterprise in the "migration of automotive parts manufacturing processes to humanoid robots."

Content

What It Is: Precise Definition

Tuopu Group (Tuopu Group, SH: 601689) is a publicly listed company headquartered in Ningbo, Zhejiang Province, China, founded in 1983. In the traditional automotive sector, it is a leading Tier 1 supplier of automotive parts, primarily producing shock absorbers, interior trim, chassis systems, thermal management, automotive electronics, and lightweight body components, with customers covering mainstream domestic and international new energy vehicle manufacturers.

In the humanoid robot industry, Tuopu Group is positioned as a Tier 1 actuator/structural assembly supplier. It is not a robot startup starting from scratch, but rather leverages its existing large-scale die-casting, precision machining, and assembly capabilities to develop robotic electromechanical actuators, joint module housings, and lightweight structural components. Its core role is to integrate screw grinding, assembly, and factory testing into turnkey products, delivering "motor + screw + sensor + housing" linear actuator assemblies to robot OEMs.

Why It Exists: Pain Points and Historical Positioning

Between the blueprint and mass production of humanoid robots lies an entire manufacturing process system. The core contradiction faced by robot OEMs is: the manufacturing learning curve for joint modules is extremely steep, while their own resources must focus on the "brain + cerebellum" (AI and motion control) and whole-machine integration.

This contradiction is particularly acute during the production ramp-up phase. Mass production ramp-up follows the following pattern:

\[ Q(t) = Q_{max}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right) \]

where \(\tau\) is the ramp-up time constant, determined jointly by production line commissioning, worker proficiency, and supply chain completeness. Generally speaking, for entirely new product categories (with no inheritable process heritage), \(\tau\) is measured in quarters; production lines involving automotive Tier 1 suppliers see significantly shorter \(\tau\)—this is one reason why automotive-grade suppliers such as Tesla, Tuopu Group, and Sanhua Intelligent Controls are highly valued by robot OEMs.

Tuopu Group's historical positioning lies precisely here: it replicates the "OEM—Tier 1—Tier 2" layered model validated in the automotive industry into the humanoid robot industry. Robot OEMs retain the right to define key interfaces (communication protocols, torque-position characteristics, fail-safe behavior), while outsourcing the manufacturing learning curve of joint modules to Tier 1 suppliers with automotive mass-production experience. For robot OEMs, this model is a manufacturing strategy of "using outsourced assemblies to simplify assembly and clarify responsibility boundaries."

Principle Breakdown

① Integrated Die-Casting: From Dozens of Welded Parts to One or Two Castings

The "giga casting" approach validated in the automotive industry is penetrating humanoid robots: integrating a torso frame originally composed of dozens of stamped/machined parts welded together into one or two large castings can significantly reduce part count, assembly time, and cumulative tolerances. Tuopu Group possesses large-scale die-casting and precision manufacturing capabilities, which is one reason it has been included in the Tesla Optimus supply chain layout.

Integrated die-casting involves three key process concepts:

  • Giga casting: A process using ultra-large die-casting machines to form multiple structural parts into a single large casting in one operation.
  • Near-net-shape: A process where the formed part requires only minimal machining to reach final dimensions.
  • Heat-treatment-free alloys: Alloys designed so that castings meet strength requirements in the as-cast state, avoiding heat-treatment deformation and suitable for large integrated parts.

② Linear Actuator Assembly: Turnkey Manufacturing Strategy

The precision grinding of thread raceways in planetary roller screws and the consistency sorting of rollers are the core of the process, with currently limited global production capacity. Tuopu Group is developing integrated manufacturing of linear actuator assemblies (motor + screw + sensor + housing), combining screw grinding, assembly, and factory testing into a turnkey product.

The engineering implications of this strategy are threefold:

  • Robot OEMs focus on the "brain + cerebellum" (AI and motion control) and whole-machine integration, outsourcing the manufacturing learning curve of joint modules to Tier 1 suppliers with automotive mass-production experience;
  • Tier 1 suppliers further integrate upstream Tier 2 suppliers such as reducers (Leaderdrive, Shuanghuan), motors (MOONS', StepperOnline, etc.), and screws (Nanjing Craft, Best Precision);
  • Robot OEMs retain the right to define key interfaces (communication protocols, torque-position characteristics, fail-safe behavior), i.e., the extension of the ICD.

Horizontal Comparison

In the humanoid robot Tier 1 supplier ecosystem, Tuopu Group and Sanhua Intelligent Controls are the two most frequently mentioned cross-industry suppliers:

Comparison Dimension Tuopu Group Sanhua Intelligent Controls
Traditional core business Automotive NVH, chassis, lightweight structural components New energy vehicle thermal management
Robot entry direction Actuator assemblies and structural components Electromechanical actuators
Core process capabilities Large-scale die-casting, precision machining, assembly integration Electromechanical actuator integration
Cross-industry logic Migration of structural component processes Migration of thermal management and electromechanical integration
Role in supply chain Tier 1 actuator/structural assembly Tier 1 electromechanical actuator

Both embody the industrial logic of "migration of automotive parts manufacturing processes to humanoid robots." Unlike suppliers focused on single components such as Harmonic Drive (harmonic reducers), Leaderdrive (harmonic reducers), Nabtesco (RV reducers), and Kollmorgen (frameless motors), Tuopu and Sanhua provide assembly-level delivery capability—this is precisely the premise that makes the "outsourced assemblies for simplified assembly" strategy viable.

Who Uses It: Application Cases

① Tesla Optimus Supply Chain

In the Tesla Optimus supply chain layout, Tuopu Group serves as an actuator assembly and structural component supplier, and Sanhua Intelligent Controls as an electromechanical actuator partner, both possessing large-scale die-casting and precision manufacturing capabilities. This is a typical implementation case of the "migration of automotive parts manufacturing processes to humanoid robots" industrial logic.

② OEM—Tier 1—Tier 2 Layered Structure

The humanoid robot industry is replicating the layered structure of the automotive industry. Clear Tier 1 supplier entities have emerged in the knowledge graph: Tuopu Group (actuator assembly direction) and Sanhua Intelligent Controls (electromechanical actuator direction), both crossing over from new energy vehicle thermal management and structural components to undertake actuator assembly outsourcing from robot OEMs. Robot OEMs include Tesla, Figure, Unitree, UBTech, and AgiBot.

③ Production Ramp-Up Accelerator

During the mass production ramp-up phase, production lines involving automotive Tier 1 suppliers see significantly shorter \(\tau\). As an automotive-grade supplier, Tuopu Group's value lies not only in manufacturing capability itself, but also in migrating quality methodology systems accumulated in the automotive industry—such as FAI, PPAP, PFMEA, and supplier certification—to robot manufacturing, thereby shortening the ramp-up time constant.

Limitations and Boundaries

① Process Migration Adaptability Issues

Automotive structural components and robot structural components differ significantly in precision requirements, batch sizes, and material selection. The mass production scale of automotive chassis parts can reach hundreds of thousands of units per year, while the demand for humanoid robots in the early stages is far below this level. Tuopu Group's large-scale die-casting capability only demonstrates cost advantages at the scale of tens of thousands of units; below the thousand-unit scale, CNC precision machining may be more economical. This batch-process matching issue requires independent verification.

② The Double-Edged Sword of Responsibility Boundaries

While outsourcing assemblies simplifies the robot OEM's assembly and responsibility boundaries, it also means the OEM loses control over the internal processes of joint modules. If the Tier 1 supplier's process capabilities or quality systems are inadequate, the OEM faces "black box" risks. As a cross-industry supplier, Tuopu Group's accumulated process validation data in the robotics field is not yet as deep as its automotive experience.

③ Process Barriers in Key Components

The precision grinding of thread raceways in planetary roller screws and the consistency sorting of rollers are the core of the process, with currently limited global production capacity. Although Tuopu Group is developing linear actuator assemblies, the process barriers of screws as a core component cannot be overcome in the short term. It needs to integrate upstream Tier 2 suppliers (such as Nanjing Craft and Best Precision), and the Tier 2's process capabilities directly determine the ultimate precision ceiling of the assembly.

Common Misconceptions

  1. "Tuopu Group is a robot company"—Wrong. Its core business remains automotive parts; the robot business is an extension of existing process capabilities. Its core competitiveness comes from large-scale die-casting, precision machining, and assembly integration capabilities accumulated in the automotive field, not from robot-specific technologies.

  2. "Tier 1 is simply contract manufacturing"—Wrong. Tuopu Group delivers turnkey assembly products, including the complete process chain of screw grinding, assembly, and factory testing. Robot OEMs purchase finished products with "clear responsibility boundaries," not consignment processing services.

  3. "Integrated die-casting is suitable for all production scales"—Wrong. The advantages of integrated die-casting only fully manifest at the scale of tens of thousands of units. During the prototype and small-batch stages, CNC precision machining or small-tonnage die-casting may be more economical. Process selection must match the batch size range.

  4. "Tier 1 participation guarantees smooth ramp-up"—Wrong. Tier 1 suppliers can shorten the ramp-up time constant \(\tau\), but cannot eliminate design defects. The main contradiction during the PVT stage remains the exposure of design defects and ECN traceability; Tier 1 process capabilities cannot replace design maturity.
