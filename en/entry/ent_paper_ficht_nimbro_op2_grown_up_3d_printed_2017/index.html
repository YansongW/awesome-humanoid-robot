Summary

This paper introduces NimbRo-OP2, an affordable, fully open-source, 3D-printed adult-sized humanoid robot platform that stands approximately 135 cm tall and weighs 18 kg, and describes the mechanical design, electronics, and ROS-based software that enabled it to win the RoboCup 2017 Humanoid League AdultSize Soccer competition and Technical Challenge.

Sources

  • NimbRo-OP2: Grown-up 3D Printed Open Humanoid Platform for Research

Overview

NimbRo-OP2 is an adult-sized (1345 mm) open-source humanoid robot research platform developed by the team at the University of Bonn. It features a fully 3D-printed exoskeleton structure (SLS/PA12) and 34 uniformly specified Dynamixel MX-106R actuators, with both hardware and software open-sourced. Its core contribution lies in achieving a complete research capability—including onboard computing, unassisted walking, and soccer competition—at a cost far lower than traditional adult-sized platforms (at least an order of magnitude cheaper), and its victory at RoboCup 2017 AdultSize validated the platform's practicality.

What It Changed

Humanoid robotics research has long faced an awkward gap: small platforms (Nao, DARwIn-OP) are cheap, safe, and easy to maintain, but scale effects prevent them from truly reflecting the dynamics and interaction problems of larger robots; meanwhile, adult-sized platforms (Asimo, HRP, Atlas, etc.) offer full performance but cost two orders of magnitude more, are often closed-source, difficult to maintain, and pose safety risks. What NimbRo-OP2 truly changed is filling this gap using 3D printing and commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) actuator strategies—it proved that an adult-sized platform with sufficient torque and complete onboard computing can achieve openness and maintainability close to that of small platforms.

The significance of this change lies not in a single technical breakthrough, but in the redefinition of a research niche. Previously, "open-source humanoid" was almost synonymous with "small humanoid." NimbRo-OP2 pushed the standard of open-source hardware and software to the 1.3-meter level, enabling research teams to validate control algorithms, gait planning, and human-robot interaction schemes at true scale without enormous budgets. It also addressed the common skepticism about "insufficient stiffness of 3D-printed structures"—through exoskeleton design, variable wall thickness, and rib reinforcement, it achieved structural performance at 17.5 kg self-weight sufficient for kicking and impact resistance (pendulum tests).

Method Breakdown

Mechanical Architecture: Exoskeleton and Unified Actuators

  • The entire frame is fully 3D-printed (SLS, PA12, 0.1 mm layer thickness), with the exoskeleton serving both load-bearing and aesthetic functions, with no internal metal frame. Structural strength is achieved through variable wall thickness, ribs, and geometric reinforcement features, rather than relying on the material itself.
  • All 34 joints uniformly use Dynamixel MX-106R (stall torque 10.0 Nm, no-load speed 55 rpm, encoder 4096 ticks/rev). A single actuator type greatly simplifies spare parts and maintenance.

Kinematic Design: Serial and Parallel Hybrid

  • Upper body uses serial chains: neck with 2 DOF (yaw + pitch), arms with a "pitch-roll-pitch" configuration, and shoulders using igus® axial thrust bearings for self-lubrication and reduced pitch-axis stress.
  • Legs employ three joint types:
  • Hip yaw: single actuator + external gear, with igus® PRT slewing ring bearing, hollow bore for cable routing.
  • Hip/ankle roll: dual actuators in master-slave connection driving external gear sets.
  • Thigh/shank parallel kinematics: one actuator per mounting point, master-slave connected for synchronization.
  • The knee consists of 8 actuators and 3 plastic parts, with 4 servos on the front and back each independently accessible, and ventilation holes reserved at critical locations (DC fan optional).

Modular Design

  • Thigh and shank parts are horizontal/vertical mirror images of each other; 8 parts require only 2 molds to repair both legs. Upper arms and covers are shared across shoulder and elbow joints. This design directly reduces manufacturing and spare parts costs.

Electronics and Power Architecture

  • Core computing is an Intel NUC (NUC7I7BNH, Core i7-7567U, 3.5–4.0 GHz, 4 GB RAM), with a Robotis CM740 (STM32F103RE) as the sub-controller.
  • Key design decision: BTS555 smart high-side power switch separates actuator power from control component power, solving the current limit and surge issues of the igus® platform's power board; the Robotis power board is retained only for control components.

Software and Perception

  • Based on ROS middleware with a modular plugin mechanism; behavior control, motion modules, and vision can be independently replaced.
  • Vision: Logitech C905 (720p, 150° wide angle), capturing 1600×1200@30 Hz, downscaled to 640×480 in ROS, HSV color space, Newton-Raphson lookup table for distortion removal, Nelder-Mead for calibrating camera head pose.
  • Attitude feedback: CM740 onboard 6-axis IMU (L3G4200D gyro + LIS331DLH accelerometer), 3D nonlinear passive complementary filter outputting globally stable fused angles.

Control Strategy

  • Feedforward position control: modifies joint trajectories based on input trajectories and expected loads, using the Rigid Body Dynamics Library for whole-body inverse dynamics, accounting for battery voltage, joint friction, and model inertia. Each servo uses only proportional control, with effort values from 0–1 interpolating proportional gains to adjust compliance.
  • Gait generation: open-loop pattern based on desired step frequency + fused angle feedback stabilization. URDF joint name aliases automatically establish transformations between virtual serial chains and real parallel chains (copy, negate, sum, difference), with external gear scaling applied bidirectionally for commands and feedback.
  • Motion playback: nonlinear keyframe interpolator generating smooth trajectories between position, velocity, and torque keyframes, supporting joint PID based on attitude feedback.

Key Innovations

  1. Cost reconstruction via fully 3D-printed exoskeleton + unified actuators: replacing traditional metal machining with SLS/PA12 printed structures and mixed actuator models with a single model (MX-106R) compresses the cost of an adult-sized platform to 2× that of a comparable (90 cm igus®) platform and 4× that of the small DARwIn-OP, but at least an order of magnitude lower than similarly sized platforms. This directly challenges the industry's default assumption that "adult-sized equals expensive."

  2. Software-transparent transformation between parallel kinematics and serial chains: through the URDF joint name alias mechanism, bidirectional mapping (copy/negate/sum/difference) between virtual serial chains and real parallel chains is automated, with external gear scaling applied. This allows upper-level control algorithms to operate without awareness of underlying mechanism differences, greatly reducing the software complexity introduced by parallel mechanisms—a highly pragmatic and rare innovation in engineering.

  3. Feedforward inverse dynamics + proportional control for compliance adjustment: whole-body inverse dynamics (Rigid Body Dynamics Library) computes expected loads, superimposed onto joint trajectories, while using only proportional control with effort-based gain interpolation. This combination maintains tracking accuracy while retaining compliance adjustment capability, without introducing complex impedance control algorithms—simple and effective engineering implementation.

Experiments and Results

Metric Value
Height / Weight 1345 mm / 17.5 kg
Walking speed ~0.4 m/s
Max ankle roll joint tracking error 4° (slight overcompensation)
Other joint tracking errors < 2°
Kicking distance (size 4 standard soccer ball) > 6 m
Battery life 15–30 minutes (4S LiPo, 14.8 V, 6.6 Ah)
RoboCup 2017 results Soccer champion + Technical Challenge champion
Goal statistics 46 goals in 5 matches, final ended early at 11:1
Design award RoboCup Design Award (Flower Robotics)

Experimental validation focused on the RoboCup 2017 Humanoid League AdultSize competition: 46 goals scored in 5 matches, only 1 goal conceded in the final, and in the Technical Challenge, successfully kicked a moving ball and maintained walking stability after pendulum mass impact. These results demonstrate that the platform's dynamic stability, impact resistance, and motion precision reach practical levels in real competitive scenarios. Joint tracking error data (ankle roll 4°, others <2°) indicate that feedforward control performs well on most joints, with the ankle roll overcompensation issue judged negligible by the authors (artificial turf damping + feedback mechanism as fallback).

Boundaries and Limitations

The paper lacks explicit long-term durability testing data—fatigue life of 3D-printed PA12 structures under prolonged, high-load cycles lacks quantitative validation, a risk point that replicators must assess themselves. Validation outside soccer scenarios is missing: the platform excels under RoboCup rules but does not demonstrate general research capabilities such as complex environment navigation, multi-robot collaboration, or unstructured terrain adaptation. Quantitative performance improvement data compared to the igus® platform is not provided; the cost comparison (2× igus®) is the only available relative metric. Although the 4° ankle roll error is judged negligible, the authors acknowledge that increasing effort values can improve tracking but reduce compliance and shorten servo lifespan—implying an unquantified trade-off between precision and durability.

Engineering Insights

Before replication or selection, it is recommended to prioritize the following three points: First, the structural strength of the SLS-printed PA12 exoskeleton depends on wall thickness and rib design; when directly using the public CAD files (github.com/NimbRo/nimbro-op2), the printing service provider's SLS process parameters (0.1 mm layer thickness) and material batch consistency must be verified; otherwise, structural failure risk is uncontrollable. Second, the BTS555 power switch's power separation scheme is key to solving surge currents; if switching to another power switch, power-on sequencing and current margins must be revalidated—this is the most likely hardware pitfall. Third, the transformation between parallel kinematics and serial chains relies on URDF joint name alias conventions; if mechanisms or joint names are modified, alias mappings must be updated synchronously, otherwise command and feedback directions/scales will be incorrect. On the software side, the ROS plugin mechanism (github.com/AIS-Bonn/humanoid_op_ros) allows independent replacement of behavior and motion modules; downstream teams are advised to first run Gazebo simulations (the paper mentions verified lying-to-standing and jumping actions) before deploying on the real robot. For cost estimation, note: the platform costs approximately 2× the 90 cm igus® platform, but additional considerations include 3D printing iteration and trial-error costs, as well as servo lifespan degradation (high effort values accelerate wear).
