Overview

Sanhua Intelligent Controls is a global leader in refrigeration and air conditioning control components and new energy vehicle thermal management. Leveraging its precision valve, pump, and actuator technologies, it is extending into humanoid robot electromechanical actuators and thermal management. It is a typical Tier 1 supplier in the humanoid robot supply chain, taking on actuator assembly outsourcing from OEMs and transferring automotive-grade mass production experience to robot manufacturing.

Content

What It Is: Precise Definition

Sanhua Intelligent Controls is a publicly listed company headquartered in Shaoxing, Zhejiang Province, China (SZ: 002050). Founded in 1994, it is part of Sanhua Holding Group Co., Ltd. Its core capabilities focus on three areas: fluid control, precision electromagnetic actuation, and thermal management. Products cover electronic expansion valves, four-way reversing valves, solenoid valves, microchannel heat exchangers, electronic water pumps, oil coolers, and integrated thermal management modules for new energy vehicles.

In the context of the humanoid robot industry, Sanhua Intelligent Controls is defined as a Tier 1 electromechanical actuator supplier—that is, it delivers highly integrated actuator assemblies (motor + screw + sensor + housing) directly to OEMs, rather than individual components. Alongside Tuopu Group, it is regarded as a representative company for "transferring automotive parts manufacturing processes to humanoid robots."

What it truly changes is not the performance of a single valve, but the interface of responsibility between OEMs and suppliers—shifting from "buying parts and assembling them yourself" to "buying assemblies and installing them directly."

Why It Exists: Pain Points and Historical Positioning

As humanoid robots move from prototypes to mass production at the tens of thousands scale, a core contradiction emerges: OEMs excel at the "brain + cerebellum" (AI and motion control) but are not skilled at climbing the manufacturing learning curve. Joint modules involve precision grinding, heat treatment, sorting, assembly, and end-of-line testing—process barriers that require years of accumulation.

Sanhua's historical positioning happens to fill this gap:

  • It has accumulated over 30 years of precision valve manufacturing experience in refrigeration and thermal management, mastering high-volume, high-consistency production management.
  • It entered through new energy vehicle thermal management, establishing automotive-grade quality systems (IATF 16949, PPAP, PFMEA, etc.).
  • It possesses large-scale die-casting and precision manufacturing capabilities, enabling it to undertake integrated die-cast structural components and electromechanical actuator assemblies.

In the context of production ramp-up, production lines involving automotive Tier 1 suppliers exhibit a significantly shortened ramp-up time constant τ. This is the fundamental reason why automotive-grade suppliers such as Tesla, Tuopu Group, and Sanhua are highly valued by OEMs.

Principle Breakdown

① Manufacturing Process Transfer: From Automotive Thermal Management to Robot Actuators

Sanhua's core process logic is "process transfer." Automotive thermal management components (electronic expansion valves, electronic water pumps) and robot actuators share a large number of underlying processes at the manufacturing level:

Process Capability Automotive Thermal Management Application Robot Actuator Application
Precision valve machining Electronic expansion valves Thermal management valves
Electromagnetic actuation Solenoid valves, water pump motors Servo motor stator windings
Die casting and near-net shaping Housings, integrated modules Joint housings, structural components
Assembly and testing Integrated thermal management modules Linear/rotary actuator assemblies

② Integrated Die Casting and Large Structural Components

The "giga casting" approach validated in the automotive industry is now penetrating humanoid robots: consolidating a torso skeleton originally welded from dozens of stamped/machined parts into one or two large die-cast components significantly reduces part count, assembly time, and cumulative tolerances. Sanhua possesses large-scale die-casting capabilities, which is one reason it has been included in the Tesla Optimus supply chain layout.

③ Turnkey Model for Linear Actuator Assemblies

The precision grinding of planetary roller screw thread raceways and the consistency sorting of rollers are the core processes, with limited global capacity currently. Sanhua is developing integrated manufacturing for linear actuator assemblies, combining screw grinding, assembly, and end-of-line testing into a turnkey product. For OEMs, this is analogous to integrated joint modules—a manufacturing strategy of "outsourcing assemblies in exchange for simplified assembly and clear responsibility interfaces."

Who Uses It · Application Cases

① Tesla Optimus Supply Chain

In the Tesla Optimus supply chain layout, Sanhua Intelligent Controls is explicitly mentioned as an electromechanical actuator partner, alongside Tuopu Group (actuator assembly and structural component supplier). This reflects the industrial logic of "transferring automotive parts manufacturing processes to humanoid robots."

② OEM–Tier 1–Tier 2 Layered Structure

The humanoid robot industry is replicating the automotive industry's layered model:

  • OEMs: Tesla, Figure, Unitree, UBTech, AgiBot, etc., focusing on the "brain + cerebellum" and whole-machine integration.
  • Tier 1: Sanhua Intelligent Controls, Tuopu Group, etc., taking on actuator assembly outsourcing.
  • Tier 2: Leader Harmonious Drive, Shuanghuan (reducers), Moons', maxon, Kollmorgen, Stepper (motors), GSA, Nanjing Craft, Best Precision (screws), etc.

As a Tier 1 supplier, Sanhua further integrates upward with Tier 2 suppliers of reducers, motors, and screws, forming a vertically integrated supply chain.

③ Production Ramp-Up Scenarios

During the ramp-up from PVT small-batch to steady-state mass production, the value of automotive-grade suppliers like Sanhua lies in the significantly shortened ramp-up time constant τ. Their production lines possess mature SOP iteration, error-proofing mechanisms, and supply chain readiness management capabilities, enabling rapid scaling from hundreds of units per month to thousands or even tens of thousands per month.

Limitations and Boundaries

① Core Actuator Processes Are Not Its Native Capability

Sanhua's strength lies in fluid control and thermal management. Core processes such as precision grinding of planetary roller screw thread raceways and flexspline machining for harmonic reducers are not part of its historical expertise. It functions more as an assembly integrator, procuring key components upstream and completing integration and testing. Process barriers such as screw grinding and roller sorting still depend on Tier 2 suppliers.

② Robot Business Share Is Still Small

Sanhua's revenue is still primarily driven by refrigeration and air conditioning control components and new energy vehicle thermal management. The robot actuator business is in the early deployment stage, with specific revenue share not disclosed and requiring independent verification.

③ Technology Roadmap Uncertainty

The actuator technology roadmap for humanoid robots has not yet converged—whether linear actuators will use planetary roller screws or ball screws, and whether rotary actuators will use harmonic reducers or planetary reducers, is still evolving. Sanhua's capacity investments face route-selection risks.

Common Misconceptions

  1. "Sanhua Intelligent Controls is a robot component manufacturer"—Inaccurate. It is a Tier 1 assembly supplier delivering integrated actuator assemblies, not individual parts. Its value lies in "outsourcing assemblies in exchange for simplified assembly and clear responsibility interfaces."

  2. "Sanhua's robot business is already mature"—Caution is needed. Its robot actuator business is still in the deployment stage, and core process barriers (screw grinding, reducer machining) still rely on upstream Tier 2 suppliers.

  3. "Sanhua and Tuopu Group have identical businesses"—Not entirely. Sanhua's entry point is electromechanical actuators and thermal management, while Tuopu's is actuator assemblies and structural components. There are differences in process focus.

  4. "Automotive-grade suppliers will definitely make good robot components"—Not necessarily. Automotive experience offers advantages in quality systems, mass manufacturing, and supply chain management, but robot actuators have different requirements for precision, weight, and power consumption compared to automotive parts, requiring re-adaptation.
