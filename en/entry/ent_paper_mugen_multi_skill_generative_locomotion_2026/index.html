Summary

This paper presents MuGen, a data-driven framework for learning and deploying multi-skill locomotion on humanoid robots. MuGen enables a robot to perform expressive motions like humans under the guidance of example motion sequences. Institutions per source list: 北京大学.

Overview

MuGen proposes a multi-skill generative motion controller for humanoid robots, employing a teacher-student architecture with a vector quantization (VQ) codebook as the skill space, unifying motion generation and physical control within a differentiable world model for joint optimization. The method achieves robust tracking of unseen motions (SR 0.9535 vs baseline 0.7279) and real-world deployment on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot. The core contribution lies in bridging the gap between privileged information and onboard sensing through discrete skill embeddings.

What It Changes

The core pain point of existing RL motion control methods is that each new task requires redesigning reward functions and tuning algorithms, while reference-motion-tracking approaches suffer from a structural flaw of "disconnected generation and control"—kinematically feasible trajectories may not be dynamically reproducible. What MuGen truly changes is not a specific reward term or network layer, but rather extracting "skills" themselves from implicit policy parameters and materializing them into a discrete, reusable VQ codebook. This means motion skills become, for the first time, transferable and composable explicit assets, rather than implicit knowledge bound within specific policy weights.

More critically, the authors leverage a world model to transform policy optimization from model-free sample estimation into a differentiable gradient backpropagation path, enabling the teacher policy to directly "imagine" action consequences within physical simulation, rather than relying on high-variance policy gradients. This design choice fundamentally alters the training paradigm: the optimization of skill embeddings is no longer constrained by sample efficiency but can directly backpropagate in latent space, thereby aligning the structure of the skill space with robot dynamics constraints.

Method Breakdown

Overall Architecture

Teacher-student framework with four core components: encoder ℰ, decoder 𝒟, VQ bottleneck ℬ, and world model 𝒲. The teacher policy is trained using privileged information (global state + reference motion), while the student policy relies solely on proprioceptive inputs for deployment.

World Model (Differentiable Dynamics)

  • Input: current state sₜ and action aₜ, predicts state change Δs, integrated with current state to obtain sₜ₊₁
  • Loss: ℒ_𝒲 = ∑ γᵗ‖W_𝒲(sₜ₊₁^sim − sₜ₊₁^𝒲)‖₁, weights W_W = [2,1,10,5] (position, rotation, velocity, angular velocity)
  • Key design: teacher policy and world model are jointly optimized via alternating updates; the world model is frozen during the distillation phase

Skill Embedding (VQ-VAE)

  • Encoder ℰ(s,m) → continuous embedding z → VQ bottleneck quantizes to nearest codebook entry ẑ
  • Decoder 𝒟(ẑ,s) → action distribution a ∼ 𝒟(·|ẑ,s)
  • Teacher loss: ℒ_T = ∑ γᵗ(‖W_𝒯(sₜ₊₁^𝒲 − mₜ₊₁)‖₁ + β₁‖sg(ℰ(sₜ,mₜ)) − ẑₜ‖₂ + β₂‖aₜ‖₂ + β₃‖aₜ − aₜ₋₁‖₂)
  • Hyperparameters: β₁=0.05 (commitment loss), β₂=0.01 (action smoothing), β₃=0.001 (action norm penalty)
  • Codebook updates use EMA strategy, updated only during teacher training phase

Policy Distillation

  • Student loss: ℒ_S = ∑ γᵗ(‖aₜ^S − aₜ^T‖₂ + β₄ 𝔼[‖ℰ^S(sₜ,gₜ) − ℰ^T(sₜ^full,mₜ)‖₂]), β₄=1
  • Dual-channel transfer: action-level BC + feature-level alignment (ensuring student embeddings are compatible with the frozen teacher codebook)

Three-Phase Training Schedule

  1. Pre-training (epoch < ms1): train only teacher + world model, build codebook skill set
  2. Warm-up (ms1 < epoch < ms2): optimize all modules, but only teacher rollouts
  3. Distillation (ms2 < epoch < ms3): teacher frozen, teacher selection probability P = 1 − (e−ms2)/(ms3−ms2) linearly annealed to zero, with intra-trajectory mixed policies (distinct from DAgger's trajectory-level aggregation)

Generative Capability (Post-training)

A prior encoder ℰ̂(z|s) accepts only proprioceptive inputs, outputs codebook index distribution, trained via cross-entropy. At inference, combining ℰ̂ + codebook + student decoder generates actions without requiring reference motion.

Key Innovations

  1. Differentiable world model-driven skill learning: Introduces MBRL into motion skill embedding learning, with policy gradients backpropagated through the world model rather than sample estimation, significantly improving sample efficiency and allowing the skill space to be directly shaped by dynamics constraints. This is a fundamental breakthrough over the existing two-stage paradigm of "first learn kinematic trajectories, then learn tracking policies."

  2. VQ codebook as skill transfer medium: The discrete codebook simultaneously serves three purposes—structured representation of teacher skills, shared interface for teacher-student knowledge transfer, and prior space for generative tasks. Discreteness naturally provides compositionality and interpretability, while avoiding posterior collapse commonly seen in continuous latent spaces.

  3. Intra-trajectory mixed policy distillation: Randomly mixes teacher and student actions within a single rollout with linear annealing, rather than DAgger-style trajectory-level aggregation. This allows the teacher to intervene immediately to stabilize the robot when the student makes errors, significantly reducing instability in early distillation—particularly critical for high-DOF humanoid robots.

Experiments and Results

Experiments are conducted in the Isaac Gym simulation environment. The policy generates target joint positions at 30 Hz, with a low-level PD controller executing at 500 Hz; evaluation uses 1024 parallel environments, each initialized with different domain randomization parameters. The comparison setup includes three ablation baselines: vanilla MLP policy without history input (directly mapping input states and reference motion to actions), VAE-based policy without history input (continuous latent skill space), and VQ-based policy without history input (discrete latent skill space). Evaluation metrics are Survival Rate (SR, proportion of environments completing without falling), Mean Joint Rotation Error (MJRE, average local rotation error per joint against reference across successful rollouts, in radians), and Mean linear Velocity Error (MVE, average deviation of root velocity from reference). Training data comes from the LaFAN1 motion dataset (retargeted version provided by Unitree), selecting a locomotion-only subset totaling approximately 1 hour; ablation experiments use approximately 200 seconds of walking motion as training set (6105 frames / 203.5 seconds) and approximately 90 seconds of walking motion as test set (2589 frames / 86.3 seconds).

  • MuGen significantly outperforms all baselines on unseen motions: On unseen motions, MuGen achieves an SR of 0.9535, compared to 0.6070 for the vanilla MLP baseline, 0.5151 for VAE-based, and 0.7279 for VQ-based; in MJRE, MuGen achieves 0.0927, lower than all three baselines (0.0996, 0.1094, 0.1108). Notably, vanilla MLP has the highest SR on seen data (0.9082) but drops substantially on unseen data (0.6070, a decrease of approximately 33% calculated from the table values 0.9082→0.6070), indicating overfitting to the training set; whereas MuGen's SR remains nearly flat between seen and unseen (0.8820→0.9535, actually increasing rather than decreasing per table values), validating the enhanced generalization capability of discrete skill embeddings.
  • There exists an optimal range for temporal context length: At #HT=0, #HS=5, unseen SR reaches its highest at 0.9535 with MJRE of 0.0927; however, increasing history frames to #HT=5, #HS=5 causes seen SR to plummet to 0.2006, unseen SR to 0.2615, and MJRE to rise to 0.1170. This indicates that moderate history frames (#HS=5) improve tracking accuracy and survival rate, but excessive history frames introduce noise and exceed the policy network's representational capacity, leading to performance collapse.
  • Discrete skill space outperforms continuous latent variable space: The VQ-based method (unseen SR 0.7279) significantly outperforms the VAE-based method (unseen SR 0.5151), while MuGen further elevates unseen SR to 0.9535 while maintaining a high seen SR of 0.8820. This trend holds for MJRE as well (VQ-based 0.1108 vs VAE-based 0.1094 vs MuGen 0.0927), demonstrating that the structured skill representation provided by discrete codebooks is more conducive to robust tracking across motion patterns than continuous latent variables.

Boundaries and Limitations

  • Difficulty standing still: The VQ codebook is primarily trained on motion-rich sequences, resulting in under-represented quantized embeddings for static/quasi-static poses, causing the policy to oscillate between similar embeddings (foot dragging, pacing in place). This is an inherent drawback of discretization—codebook capacity is occupied by dynamic motions, leaving static patterns undersampled.
  • Limited world model accuracy: The simple MLP does not explicitly model environmental interactions; gradient directions are effective but precision is insufficient, ultimately limiting the teacher policy's precise tracking performance. The paper does not provide quantitative analysis of world model prediction error.
  • Sim-to-Real relies purely on domain randomization: No real-world rollout is used to fine-tune the world model or re-distill, making stable deployment still challenging. The paper does not mention training steps, network architecture details, codebook size, or other critical reproduction parameters.
  • Insufficient validation of generative capability: The generation quality of the prior encoder is only qualitatively described, lacking quantitative evaluation (e.g., diversity of generated motions, physical feasibility metrics).

Engineering Insights

  • Prioritize checking codebook capacity and static pose coverage: When encountering standing instability during reproduction, examine the proportion of static/quasi-static frames in the training data and consider oversampling low-frequency poses during codebook updates.
  • Strictly adhere to the "student history frames=5, teacher history frames=0" configuration: Ablations show that teacher history frames are detrimental to generalization (SR drops to 0.2 at #HT=5), which is counterintuitive but clearly supported by data—do not arbitrarily increase teacher history length.
  • Ensure intra-trajectory mixed policy during distillation: Linearly anneal P from 1 to 0, rather than DAgger-style trajectory-level switching. This significantly reduces early-distillation instability and is particularly critical for high-DOF humanoid robots.
  • World model weight settings W_W directly impact teacher performance: Velocity/angular velocity weights (10 and 5) are much higher than position/rotation (2 and 1), indicating that dynamics prediction precision takes priority over kinematics—maintain this proportional relationship when tuning.
  • Note initial pose interpolation during deployment: The G1's default standing pose differs significantly from the first frame of reference motion; direct execution may damage hardware. Linear interpolation transition is a necessary engineering safeguard that must not be omitted.
