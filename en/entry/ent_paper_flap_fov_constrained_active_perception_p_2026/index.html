Summary

Safe and efficient trajectory planning in unknown, cluttered 3D environments constitutes a critical bottleneck for deploying Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) in real-world applications. Institutions per source list: 浙江大学工业控制技术国家重点实验室、湖州研究院.

Overview

FLAP is an active perception planning framework proposed by the Zhejiang University team for unknown 3D environments, embedding FOV constraints directly into trajectory optimization rather than treating them as a standalone module. Its core contribution lies in achieving an adaptive trade-off between safety and efficiency through dual ESDF maps, differentiable visibility penalties, and active perception sub-trajectory segments, while maintaining task completion capability even under extremely narrow vertical FOVs.

What It Changes

The fundamental contradiction faced by existing UAV planning methods in unknown cluttered 3D environments is: the sensor FOV is limited, while trajectory optimization must guarantee safety. Traditional approaches either make optimistic/pessimistic assumptions about unknown space, rely on conservative heuristics (speed limits, fixed perception patterns), or apply perception constraints only near the known-unknown boundary—these solutions either sacrifice efficiency or cause collisions due to FOV blind spots during vertical maneuvers. More critically, existing methods generally ignore the coupling between UAV volume, full-attitude dynamics, and sensor geometry, as well as the physical fact that acceleration-induced pitch causes the sensor principal axis to deviate from the flight direction.

What FLAP truly changes is transforming "active perception" from an independent module in the planning pipeline (such as next-best-view selection) into a differentiable component within trajectory optimization. This means perception behavior is no longer a passive response to boundary states, but is jointly solved with safety and dynamic feasibility constraints in the same optimization problem. It breaks the serial paradigm of "plan path first, then check visibility," enabling the UAV to actively adjust attitude and position during flight to keep critical unknown regions within the FOV—something traditional dual-trajectory strategies (SUPER) or conservative constraint methods (FM) cannot handle in scenarios with extremely narrow vertical FOV (e.g., 0.2°) or when targets are directly above unknown space.

Method Breakdown

Dual ESDF Maps and Space Partitioning

  • Construct two independent ESDFs: E_s (both unknown and obstacles are unsafe) and E_u (only obstacles are unsafe)
  • Define safety margin d_s, partitioning three spaces: known safe space D_s, safe-or-unknown space D_u, and conditionally traversable space D_a = D_u D_s
  • Front-end uses weighted A*, applying a fixed penalty w_a for entering D_a, encouraging the UAV to approach the target altitude within known space

Trajectory Representation and Optimization Framework

  • Uses the MINCO trajectory class, outputting o(t) = [p^T, ψ]^T (position + yaw), with system order h=3 and 5th-degree polynomials per segment
  • Decouples spatial and temporal parameters via the invertible banded matrix K(T), achieving linear-complexity spatial-temporal deformation
  • Terminal yaw ψ_f and terminal velocity ṗ_f are optimization variables rather than fixed boundaries, avoiding over-conservatism

Visibility Constraint Modeling

  • Constraints expressed in the sensor coordinate frame S: vertical angle C_ver, horizontal angle C_hor, distance C_dmax/C_dmin, sensor position C_vd, minimum viewing angle C_vθ
  • Key design: sensor mounting pose ᴮR_S ∈ SO(3) is explicitly modeled, coupling full-attitude dynamics with sensor geometry
  • Safety criterion C_sd = ‖v‖²/(2a_max) + d_m - ‖p - p_u‖ ≤ 0, where a_max is the conservative deceleration

Active Perception Segments (AP Segments)

  • Insert a sub-trajectory segment S_p ⊂ S_s of fixed duration T_p into the trajectory, with start time t_as = ρ(T_s - T_p), where ρ ∈ (0,1) is transformed into an unconstrained variable via the logit transform
  • Add w_ρ·ρ(ϱ) to the objective function to encourage early observation
  • Apply point penalties S_ap at AP segment endpoints to prevent failure to brake when visibility constraints are satisfied but occlusion causes observation failure

Replanning Trigger Mechanism

  • Monitor the ESDF value at boundary point p_u: trigger when E_s(p_u) changes; also trigger when E_u(p_u) ≤ d_s; declare observation failure if E_s(p_u) remains unchanged by t_as + T_p
  • Set cutoff time T_R equal to expected computation time, switching trajectories at t_c + T_R

Key Innovations

  1. Fully Differentiable Embedding of Perception Constraints: Formulate FOV constraints, safety criteria, and active perception penalties all as differentiable penalty terms, directly integrated into gradient-based optimizers without requiring expensive front-end perception-aware path generators. This is a fundamental difference from existing FOV-related methods (which rely on front-end search).

  2. Activation-Function-Modulated Adaptive Trade-off: Achieve smooth transitions between safety and perception through the continuous gating function L_a(C_sd)—perception constraints are inactive when safety margins are good (L_a=0), fully applied when danger is near (L_a=1), and partially activated in intermediate states. This enables the UAV to autonomously exhibit three-stage behavior (Safety, Coupling, Perception) during flight, rather than being manually preset.

  3. Optimizable AP Segment Start Time: Treat the start time of the active perception segment as an unconstrained optimization variable (via logit transform), allowing the UAV to autonomously decide "when to begin observing unknown regions." This addresses the problem of "triggering observation too late, forcing UAV deceleration" in existing methods, manifesting as spiral ascent rather than direct vertical climb in overhead-obstacle scenarios.

Experiments and Results

Experiments were conducted in both simulation and real-world environments. The simulation section covers four scenarios: horizontal narrow spaces, overhead obstacles, U-shaped mazes, and vertical pipes, with sensor configurations including omnidirectional LiDAR (horizontal FOV 360°) and forward depth cameras (horizontal FOV ~78°, vertical FOV ~64°) as two representative settings; real-world experiments used the Livox Mid-360 LiDAR (horizontal FOV 360°, vertical FOV ~52° upward, ~7° downward) and the Orbbec Gemini 335 depth camera. Baseline methods include SUPER (dual-trajectory strategy), FM (MINCO-based FASTER implementation with explicit tilt angle constraints), RAPTOR (constraining obstacle distance with integrated active yaw planning), and NBV (candidate viewpoint sampling-based active perception). Evaluation metrics are trajectory duration TD (s), trajectory length TL (m), energy E (jerk squared integral, m²/s⁵), mean velocity MV (m/s), success rate (successes out of 10 trials), and active perception/backup trajectory usage ratio P.

The implications of the above results can be summarized as follows:

  • Active perception maintains task feasibility under constrained FOV. When the vertical FOV narrows from 90° to 0.2°, SUPER fails entirely at 30° and below (maximum completion rates of 79.19% and 66.04%), FM cannot generate feasible trajectories at 0.2° due to overly strict tilt angle constraints (F Infeasible), while FLAP still achieves 6 successes out of 10 trials (T 6/10) under the extreme 0.2° condition. This is attributed to FLAP embedding perception constraints directly into trajectory optimization and expressing them in the sensor coordinate frame, enabling the UAV to actively adjust attitude during AP segments to maintain observation of unknown regions, rather than relying on conservative speed limits or backup trajectories.

  • FLAP trades moderate trajectory length for higher overall efficiency. At vertical FOV=30°, FLAP's trajectory length (77.38 m) is longer than FM's (73.56 m), but trajectory duration (51.81 s) is shorter than FM's (55.09 s), and mean velocity (1.49 m/s) is higher than FM's (1.34 m/s) (calculated from table values). The advantage is more pronounced at vertical FOV=10°: FLAP's TD is 69.85 s, approximately 18% shorter than FM's 85.68 s (calculated from table values 69.85→85.68), with MV of 1.26 m/s versus 0.89 m/s. Although detours increase path length, they avoid time losses from excessive deceleration.

  • Explicit yaw planning improves perception smoothness and computational efficiency. In camera experiments, FLAP's SSAY (121.10 rad²/s⁴) is significantly lower than RAPTOR's (701.58 rad²/s⁴), indicating that FLAP's explicit penalty on yaw smoothness effectively suppresses unnecessary attitude jitter; additionally, FLAP's CT of 5.49 ms meets real-time execution requirements, and the number of replanning steps (182) is lower than NBV (231) and FM (563), with the non-fixed-frequency replanning mechanism reducing redundant computation.

Boundaries and Limitations

  • Visibility constraints only model FOV geometry, not occlusion—p_v may remain unobserved during the AP segment even when constraints are satisfied
  • Under extremely narrow vertical FOV (0.2°), highly fragmented unknown regions cause boundary estimation errors, and strict FOV constraints may trigger optimization failure leading to emergency stops (6/10 success rate)
  • The front-end adopts optimistic assumptions about unknown space; when previously occluded obstacles are newly observed, sudden braking may occur, leading to higher energy consumption (FLAP's E=138.01 at 90° FOV is higher than FM's 137.50)
  • No comparison with real-world experiments, no specific computation time values provided, no weight parameter sensitivity analysis, no direct comparison with other active perception methods
  • No specific failure cases or failure rate data mentioned; in the L-shaped obstacle experiment, the UAV did not check the left side, and the authors acknowledge this behavior was not the focus of the experiment

Engineering Insights

When reproducing FLAP, the three key points requiring the most careful verification are: first, the construction and update logic of the dual ESDFs—the difference between E_s and E_u directly determines the definition of D_a, which in turn affects the front-end A* penalty terms and trajectory segmentation; second, the parameters ε_a of the activation function L_a(C_sd) and the distance parameter d_Bs^max, which jointly determine the sensitivity of the safety-perception trade-off—the paper does not provide specific values, requiring self-calibration; third, the logit transform of the AP segment start time parameter ρ and the weight w_ρ, which directly affect whether the UAV can complete attitude adjustment before entering unknown space.

The most common pitfalls lie in: visibility constraints are expressed in the sensor coordinate frame, but the sensor mounting pose ᴮR_S must be correctly coupled with the attitude quaternion in the UAV dynamics model—if acceleration-induced pitch (nose-down) is ignored, the actual FOV coverage will deviate significantly from optimization assumptions. Additionally, the replanning trigger mechanism relies on monitoring the ESDF value at boundary point p_u, requiring synchronization between map update frequency and trajectory execution time; otherwise, trigger opportunities may be missed. For downstream teams, it is recommended to first validate behavior at 30° vertical FOV in simulation (where FLAP performs stably), then gradually reduce FOV to test limits; for real-world deployment, tilted LiDAR configurations (15° forward tilt) are more favorable for vertical maneuver scenarios than horizontal mounting.
