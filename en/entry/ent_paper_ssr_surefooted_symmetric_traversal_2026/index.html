Summary

Extending humanoid traversal to the open world is key to practical deployment in human environments, but remains challenging. The robot must use vision to ensure safe and reliable foot placement on heterogeneous terrain under highly dynamic motion, while producing coordinated, natural whole-body behaviors. Institutions per source list: 浙江大学.

Overview

SSR is a unified single-stage end-to-end reinforcement learning framework for open-world traversal in humanoid robots, proposed by the AgiBot team. It directly learns reliable foothold selection and symmetric, natural whole-body motion from egocentric depth images and proprioception. The core contributions lie in shifting foothold safety assessment from the contact moment to the swing phase (imagined foothold guidance), achieving mirror-equivariant symmetry augmentation in latent space (rather than input-level augmentation), and injecting human motion styles via terrain-specific discriminators. This ultimately achieves near-100% success rates on heterogeneous terrains such as stairs, gaps, and high platforms, along with 1.3 km of continuous outdoor traversal, in both simulation and real-world deployment.

What It Changes

Previous learning-based humanoid traversal methods either relied on multi-stage pipelines (e.g., HPL) that accumulate errors, or performed input-level symmetry augmentation that made training visual recurrent policies prohibitively expensive (InpSym peaked at over 24 GB of memory, requiring 48 GB GPUs). A more fundamental issue is that foothold safety assessment typically occurs only at or after the contact moment, leaving the swing leg's in-air motion without dense early guidance signals, preventing the policy from proactively avoiding edges and overhanging regions before touchdown. SSR fundamentally changes this by converting "safe foothold" from a sparse contact-event reward into dense pre-contact guidance, while moving the symmetry prior from the expensive input space into a compact latent space, enabling training on a single RTX 4090 (memory reduced from 28.8 GB to 23.7 GB). This resolves the previous pain point where symmetry learning coupled with visual policies was "computationally expensive and fragile," allowing symmetric whole-body coordination without relying on extensive mirrored data augmentation.

Method Breakdown

Problem Formulation and Architecture

  • The motion is modeled as a POMDP, optimized with PPO, using an asymmetric actor-critic. The policy observation is o_t = (o_{t-h+1:t}^p, I_t), where proprioception o_t^p ∈ ℝ^72 (base angular velocity, projected gravity, velocity commands, joint positions/velocities, previous actions) and depth image I_t ∈ ℝ^{36×36}; the action a_t ∈ ℝ^21 consists of joint position targets.
  • The critic uses privileged information (ground-truth velocity, contact states, limb positions, height maps around feet/body).
  • The policy consists of a recurrent cross-modal encoder (CNN encoding images + MLP encoding proprioception + GRU fusion), a motion state estimator (predicting base velocity v̂_t), and a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) actor. The encoder has three heads ẑ_t^f, ẑ_t^b, ẑ_t^p that decode foot height maps, base height maps, and next proprioception, respectively (VAE).

Imagined Foothold Guidance

  • During training, a foothold imagination model predicts the expected contact distribution for each foot from privileged state s_t and action a_t: q_{i,t}(p) = N(p; μ̂_{i,t}, (σ̂_{i,t})²I), supervised by Gaussian negative log-likelihood: L_pred ∝ Σ_i (||p_{i,t}^* - μ̂_{i,t}||² / (2σ̂²) + 2log σ̂).
  • Support deficiency ρ(p) is sampled every 2.5 cm on a 22.5 cm × 10 cm foot patch, measuring the proportion of support overlap near the contact point. The foothold reward is r_t^f = exp(-(Σ_i ρ̃_{i,t})² / σ_f), where support feet are evaluated at the current contact point and swing feet are evaluated under the expected deficiency from the imagined distribution (approximated via discrete Gaussian-weighted sampling).
  • Key design: pre-contact guidance is only enabled after early-stage gait stabilization (terrain curriculum exceeding a preset level) to avoid noisy targets interfering; on sloped terrain, ρ̃ = 0 is enforced to avoid suppressing inclined contacts.

Equivariant Latent-Space Symmetry Augmentation

  • Input-level mirror augmentation is costly for visual recurrent policies (requiring re-encoding mirrored images and unrolling GRU hidden states). SSR encodes the raw observation only once, then mirrors the compact actor input o_t^a = [o_t^p, v̂_t, ẑ_t].
  • The encoder enforces mirror equivariance: temporal proprioception is mapped via a fixed rearrangement operator 𝒯 into left/right branches (each ℝ^{42×h}), where the mirror transformation reduces to branch swapping ℳ_c; the CNN uses lifted convolutions (K_l = [k_l; ℳ_2D k_l]) to lift images into paired channels, with subsequent layers using constrained kernels K_nl = [[k_a, k_b]; [ℳ_2D k_b, ℳ_2D k_a]] to preserve symmetric structure.
  • During training, mirrored trajectories τ^g = (M_o o_t^a, M_s s_t, M_a a_t) are appended to the original rollout for PPO updates, without incurring additional forward encoding costs.

Cross-Terrain Motion Priors

  • A separate discriminator D_i is trained for each terrain type (Slope, Stairs, Discrete, Gap, Platform), modeling terrain-specific reference motion styles from five-frame histories ψ_t (63 dimensions per frame), providing AMP-style rewards. Data comes from custom motion capture and AMASS subsets (total durations: Slope 261.3 s, Stairs 295.8 s, Discrete 136.0 s, Gap 174.5 s, Platform 181.6 s).

Key Innovations

  1. Pre-Contact Foothold Guidance: Transforms foothold safety from "assessment at contact" to "dense guidance during swing" by using imagined contact distributions and support deficiency metrics, guiding the foot toward flat regions before touchdown. This is a fundamental improvement over "post-contact feedback" paradigms like HPL/PIM, directly reducing edge contacts and slips.
  2. Latent-Space Equivariant Symmetry Augmentation: By constructing a mirror-equivariant encoder (with a complete proof chain for equivariant MLP/GRU/CNN), symmetry learning moves from the input space to the latent space, reducing training memory by 18% (28.8 → 23.7 GB), enabling training on a single RTX 4090, while preserving the flexibility of alternating left/right foot dominance (NoSym fixes the dominant leg).
  3. Terrain-Specific Multi-Discriminators: Using 5 discriminators to model motion styles for different terrains individually, rather than a shared discriminator, significantly reduces peak foot contact forces (e.g., Stairs Up from 538.5 N to 507.7 N) and average power, preventing style priors from being corrupted by terrain differences.

Symmetry Ablation

  • SSR tracks more accurately under eight-direction velocity commands, with near-mirror-symmetric in-place turning trajectories; NoSym accumulates drift and fixes the dominant leg.
  • SSR vs. InpSym: higher return for the same training time, reaches maximum difficulty earlier, GPU memory 28.8 → 23.7 GB (-18%).

Outdoor and Cross-Platform

  • 1.3 km continuous traversal in an industrial heritage park over 40 minutes, with no resets; zero-shot OOD generalization includes perforated grid flooring, 1.3 m wide open stairs, and spiral staircases.
  • Cross-platform to DEEP Robotics DR02 (1.8 m tall, 70 kg): Stairs Up/Down 15/40 cm success rate 100%, Gap 80 cm success rate 100%, Platform 45 cm success rate 95% (calculated from in-table values 253.0 → 12.6).

Boundaries and Limitations

  • The fixed forward-facing depth camera (50° pitch) limits perception coverage, making omnidirectional traversal (e.g., lateral or backward) difficult; the authors suggest multi-view perception or active viewpoint adjustment.
  • Severe depth perception failures (e.g., specular reflections under strong sunlight) can still distort local geometry, leading to foothold misjudgments; more robust perception embeddings are needed.
  • Only foot-ground interaction is considered; upper-body contacts (e.g., hand support, climbing) are not explored; multi-contact skills and richer human demonstrations could further expand mobility.
  • The paper does not specify: training data volume, hyperparameter sensitivity analysis, or quantitative sim-to-real gap.

Engineering Insights

  • Reproduction Priority: First verify the terrain curriculum (5 terrain types × 20 difficulty levels, 8 m × 4 m areas) and command ranges (Table 4/5), as these are the foundation of training stability. Next, check the equivariant CNN kernel constraint implementation (K_nl = [[k_a, k_b]; [ℳ_2D k_b, ℳ_2D k_a]]), which is the core of symmetry augmentation and the most error-prone part.
  • Most Common Pitfalls: The activation timing of imagined foothold guidance (curriculum level threshold) and the special handling of sloped terrain (ρ̃=0) must be strictly followed; otherwise, early noisy targets can destabilize the policy. The resolution of support deficiency sampling (22.5 cm × 10 cm patch, 2.5 cm intervals) directly affects reward quality.
  • Hardware and Deployment: Depth rendering uses NVIDIA Warp (per-pixel parallel, no BVH reconstruction); real-robot inference uses Jetson AGX Orin + ONNX Runtime at 50 Hz; joint gains (Table 14) need adjustment per platform; cross-platform deployment retains the same training pipeline but requires recollecting motion prior data.
  • Downstream Teams: If the target platform lacks a waist-mounted camera or has a different camera pitch angle, retraining is required (perception distribution shift); if omnidirectional traversal is desired, perception coverage must be addressed first—SSR's fixed forward camera is a hard constraint.
