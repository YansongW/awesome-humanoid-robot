Summary

Learning-based inertial odometry has achieved remarkable progress in pedestrian navigation. However, extending these methods to quadruped robots remains challenging due to their distinct and highly dynamic motion patterns. Institutions per source list: 香港科技大学（广州）智能交通 Thrust、HKUST 新兴交叉学科部.

Overview

X-IONet is a cross-platform inertial odometry framework that relies solely on a single IMU, consisting of a rule-driven expert selection network, a dual-stage attention displacement prediction network, and an EKF state estimator, targeting two heterogeneous platforms: pedestrians and quadruped robots. The authors reduced the average ATE from 2.18m of the strongest baseline to 1.03m (a 52.8% reduction) on their self-collected Unitree Go2 dataset, and also reduced the average ATE by 14.3% on the RoNIN pedestrian dataset, validating the feasibility of cross-platform generalization.

What It Changes

The field of inertial odometry has long been dominated by "single-platform specialized models": pedestrian models fail on quadruped robots, and vice versa. The root cause is that the highly non-periodic inertial signals generated by quadruped robots' rapid acceleration/deceleration and lateral/backward movements differ greatly from pedestrian gait signals in both frequency-domain and time-domain structure, and the fixed feature extraction methods of existing architectures (such as the RoNIN series and TLIO) cannot adapt to this cross-domain distribution shift. What X-IONet truly changes is making "platform identification" explicit as a front-end routing decision rather than an implicit learning problem—first using a lightweight classifier to determine the current motion mode, then feeding it to the corresponding expert network, thereby decomposing the cross-platform problem into two more easily optimizable sub-problems: "classification + specialized regression."

The significance of this design choice lies in its acknowledgment that a single network struggles to simultaneously model two heterogeneous motion patterns, instead employing an engineered "divide and conquer" strategy to circumvent gradient conflicts and mode confusion in end-to-end joint training. Meanwhile, the dual-stage attention mechanism (temporal self-attention + dimensional self-attention) and the Huber-Gaussian uncertainty loss provide architecture-level adaptations for the multi-scale temporal dependencies and heteroscedastic noise characteristics of inertial signals, rather than simply stacking Transformer modules. This enables X-IONet, while maintaining single-IMU input, to achieve usable accuracy for the first time in pedestrian-quadruped robot cross-platform scenarios.

Method Breakdown

1. Expert Selection Network (Rule-Based Expert Selection)

  • Input: IMU segment 𝒳 ∈ ℝ^{6×T×f} (T=1s window, f=200Hz, 6 channels including 3-axis gyroscope ω and 3-axis accelerometer a)
  • Feature extraction: three stacked 1-D convolutional blocks, each layer being MaxPool(relu(BN(Conv1d(𝒳_n)))), with Kaiming normal initialization
  • Classification head: adaptive average pooling → fully connected layer → softmax output for binary classification logits (class 1=pedestrian, 0=quadruped robot)
  • Routing rule: if-else hard routing, sending the IMU sequence to the corresponding pre-trained expert network

2. Displacement Prediction Network (Dual-Stage Attention Encoder-Decoder)

  • Embedding layer: divides 𝒳 ∈ ℝ^{6×L} into sub-segments along the dimension (length L_seg), with linear projection plus learnable positional encoding: h_{i,j} = H·𝒳^{seg}{i,j} + E^{pos}
  • Dual-stage attention (applied to 2D tensor Y ∈ ℝ^{N×D×d_model}):
  • Stage 1: independent multi-head self-attention per dimension, capturing long-range temporal dependencies
  • Stage 2: cross-dimensional multi-head self-attention, modeling inter-dimension relationships, with a lightweight router to reduce computational cost
  • Encoder: N layers, each layer merging adjacent temporal features (except the first layer) to obtain multi-resolution representations Y^{enc,0}...Y^{enc,N}, where higher layers capture long-term dynamics and lower layers preserve fine-grained details
  • Decoder: N+1 layers, with initial input being learnable positional embeddings E^{dec}; each layer first performs dual-stage attention, then cross-attention with corresponding encoder features, followed by MLP + layer normalization to regress 3D displacement and covariance

3. Uncertainty-Aware Training (Huber-Gaussian Loss)

  • Total loss: ℒ = ℒ_Huber + λ·ℒ_C, λ=1e-4
  • Huber term: δ=0.005, robust to outliers
  • Gaussian likelihood term: ℒ_C = ½(d̂_i - d_i)Σ̂_i^{-1}(d̂_i - d_i)^T + ½ln(det Σ̂_i), enabling uncertainty-aware weighting
  • Compared to TLIO's staged training, this loss simultaneously optimizes error magnitude and uncertainty estimation, resulting in more stable training

4. EKF State Estimation

  • State vector: S = (λ₁,...,λ_n, x), x = (R_i^w, v^w, p^w, b_g, b_a)
  • Propagation: rotation R̂{n+1} = R̂_n·exp_SO3((ω_n - b̂)Δt), velocity v̂{n+1} = v̂_n + g^w·Δt + R̂_n^w(a_n - b̂)Δt, position p̂{n+1} = p̂_n + ½Δt(v̂ + v̂_n), bias random walk
  • Update: measurement function h(X) = R^T(p_j^w - p_i^w) = d̂ij + σ_ij, Jacobian H = R̂^T·⌊p_j^w - p_i^w⌋×, H, with SO(3) group addition for pose updates} = R̂^T; Kalman gain K = P·H^T(HPH^T + Σ̂_ij)^{-1

Key Innovations

  1. Explicit expert routing mechanism: Decomposes the cross-platform problem into "motion mode classification + specialized regression" using a 1-D CNN classifier for hard routing. This is the first scheme to explicitly model platform heterogeneity in inertial odometry, avoiding mode confusion in end-to-end joint training; ablation experiments show ATE degrades by 11.9% when the classifier is removed.

  2. Dual-stage attention + hierarchical encoder-decoder: Temporal self-attention captures long-range dependencies, dimensional self-attention models inter-channel relationships in the IMU, and the hierarchical structure preserves both fine-grained motion details and long-term dynamics. Ablation shows removing dual-stage attention increases ATE by 50.0%, making it the single most contributing component.

  3. Huber-Gaussian joint loss: Unifies robust regression (Huber) with uncertainty modeling (Gaussian likelihood) into a single objective, replacing TLIO's staged training. The 3σ coverage of 99.2% outperforms TLIO's 98.7%, and ablation shows replacing it with MSE increases ATE by 20.3%, demonstrating that uncertainty awareness is crucial for cross-platform generalization.

Boundaries and Limitations

  • Only validated on two platform types—pedestrians and quadruped robots—without covering UAVs, wheeled robots, etc.; the authors explicitly state future work will extend to more platforms
  • No multimodal fusion (e.g., vision, foot contact sensors), relying solely on a single IMU; no direct comparison with methods using additional sensors
  • No validation of long-term deployment stability in mixed indoor/outdoor or long-duration large-scale scenarios
  • The paper does not specify inference frequency, model parameter count, or computational cost, making it difficult to assess real-time resource usage
  • The self-collected Go2 dataset is only 1.5 hours, with limited training data scale, which may affect generalization in Hard scenarios (Hard 01/02 ATE is notably higher than Easy/Medium)

Engineering Insights

  1. Reproduction priority: First verify the classification threshold and routing logic of the expert selection network—this is the "first gate" for cross-platform performance, with ablation showing an 11.9% ATE degradation when removed. It is recommended to evaluate classifier accuracy independently on self-collected data before integrating the full system.

  2. Most common pitfall: IMU sampling rate alignment. The paper resamples the phone IMU from original 100 Hz to 200 Hz and linearly interpolates to align with ground-truth trajectories. Any sampling rate inconsistency or timestamp offset will directly contaminate displacement prediction training data; it is recommended to strictly verify time synchronization during reproduction.

  3. Loss function hyperparameter sensitivity: λ=1e-4 and δ=0.005 are tuned for this data distribution. When transferring to other platforms, it is recommended to first perform a grid search on δ (0.001~0.01) while keeping λ at the 1e-4 scale; otherwise, the Huber term may over-smooth or the Gaussian term may dominate, causing training instability.

  4. EKF initialization: The state vector includes n historical poses λ_i, but the specific value of n is not specified in the paper. It is recommended to start with n=5~10; too small loses smoothness, while too large increases computational burden and may introduce latency.

  5. Hardware requirements: Training was completed on an RTX 4090 with batch size 128 and 100 epochs. If resources are limited, one can first freeze the expert selection network and train only the displacement prediction network, then fine-tune jointly, which can significantly reduce memory requirements.
