Summary

We present HY-Motion 1.0, a series of state-of-the-art, large-scale, motion generation models capable of generating 3D human motions from textual descriptions.

Overview

HY-Motion 1.0 is the first text-to-motion generation system that scales DiT-based flow matching models to the billion-parameter level, developed by Tencent and other institutions. It constructs a multi-source motion data pipeline exceeding 3,000 hours (wild videos, motion capture, 3D assets), combined with hierarchical dual-condition text encoding and a three-stage training paradigm (pretraining-finetuning-RL), significantly surpassing existing open-source baselines in instruction following (SSAE 78.6%) and motion quality (3.43/5).

What It Changes

The real shift in this work is moving "data scale" from a supporting role to the spotlight. Previous mainstream approaches in this field (MoMask, GoToZero, LoM) either relied on small-scale annotated datasets like HumanML3D or used discrete tokenizers that sacrificed motion smoothness for semantic diversity. HY-Motion 1.0 demonstrates that when motion data reaches the scale of thousands of hours and undergoes systematic cleaning and annotation, flow matching models with purely continuous representations can simultaneously address the two long-standing pain points of "poor semantic alignment" and "numerous physical artifacts"—it does not invent a new mathematical framework but successfully transfers the "data + scale" paradigm validated in the LLM domain to motion generation.

More critically, it reshapes the industry's understanding of the "motion generation scaling curve." Through ablation studies, the authors reveal a non-trivial finding: instruction-following capability improves monotonically with model scale (0.05B→1B average score 3.10→3.34), but motion quality saturates after 0.46B (3.26→3.34, only marginal gains). This implies that the community's past practice of blindly stacking parameters is inefficient—the real bottleneck lies in data diversity and semantic coverage, not model capacity. This finding directly points subsequent work toward resource allocation priorities.

Method Breakdown

Data Pipeline (Core Moat)

  • Multi-source collection: 12 million wild video clips (filtered via shot boundary detection + human detector) → GVHMR reconstructs SMPL-X trajectories; plus approximately 500 hours of motion capture/3D animation assets.
  • Unified processing: All data retargeted to neutral SMPL-H (22 joints, no hands), with a three-step filtering process (deduplication, abnormal pose/velocity/displacement detection, foot-sliding artifact detection), resampled to 30fps, and sequences longer than 12 seconds segmented.
  • Annotation enhancement: Video sources use the original videos directly; 3D data is rendered into synthetic videos; Gemini-2.5-Pro generates initial annotations, human validation on high-quality subsets, LLM standardizes structure and diversifies paraphrases.

Motion Representation

Per-frame vector f ∈ ℝ²⁰¹: global root translation t ∈ ℝ³, global orientation r ∈ ℝ⁶, local joint rotations jr ∈ ℝ²¹ˣ⁶, local joint positions jp ∈ ℝ²²ˣ³. Key decision: removal of velocity/foot-contact labels (authors observed faster convergence), with rotations using continuous 6D representation (compatible with animation workflows, unlike HumanML3D).

HY-Motion DiT Architecture

  • Hybrid Transformer: Alternating dual-stream blocks (action/text independent QKV+MLP with joint attention interaction) and single-stream blocks (concatenated sequences, parallel spatial + channel attention).
  • Asymmetric attention mask: Action tokens attend globally to text; text tokens are masked from attending to actions—preventing text representations from being polluted by motion noise.
  • Narrow-band temporal mask: Attention restricted to a 121-frame sliding window (~4 seconds at 30fps), reducing computational complexity for long sequences.
  • Hierarchical dual conditioning: Qwen3-8B extracts token-level semantics (converted to bidirectional via Bidirectional Token Refiner), CLIP-L provides global embeddings, injected through separate AdaLN.

Training Strategy

  • Flow matching objective: Optimal transport path x_t = (1−t)x₀ + tx₁, loss L_FM = E[||v_θ(x_t, c, t) − (x₁−x₀)||²].
  • Three stages: 3000+ hours pretraining (constant LR η_pre) → 400 hours curated finetuning (LR=0.1η_pre) → two-stage RL (DPO on 9,228 curated pairs + Flow-GRPO integrating TMR semantic rewards with foot-sliding/root-drift physical penalties).
  • Duration prediction: Qwen3-30B-A3B two-stage training (SFT generates "optimized prompt + duration", GRPO uses Qwen3-235B to judge semantic and temporal reasonableness).

Key Innovations

  1. First validation of scaling laws for motion generation models: Through systematic comparison of DiT at 0.05B/0.46B/1B scales, it proves that instruction-following capability continuously improves with scale, but motion quality has a saturation point. This finding directly informs the release strategy of the "lightweight Lite (0.46B)" version—not all scenarios require a 1B model.

  2. Data engineering as primary productivity: Fully transfers the "data mixing + cleaning + augmentation" methodology from the LLM domain to motion. In particular, the closed-loop pipeline of "video → 3D reconstruction → unified skeleton → VLM annotation → human refinement → LLM rewriting" solves the fundamental bottleneck of small-scale and inconsistent annotation in previous motion datasets. The 3,000-hour pretraining data is roughly 200 times the scale of existing public datasets (HumanML3D at ~14 hours).

  3. Asymmetric attention + narrow-band temporal mask: This architectural design appears simple but is highly practical—text tokens not attending to actions prevents semantic representations from being disturbed by motion noise, while the narrow-band mask maintains local temporal coherence within a 121-frame window while reducing computational complexity from O(T²) to O(T·W). This is a typical example of "trading masks for efficiency" in engineering.

Experiments and Results

Comparison with SOTA (2000+ prompts, human rating 1-5)

Model Instruction Following SSAE Motion Quality
MoMask 2.31 58.0% 2.79
GoToZero 2.19 52.7% 2.86
DART 2.31 42.7% 3.11
LoM 2.17 48.9% 3.06
HY-Motion 1.0 3.24 78.6% 3.43

HY-Motion's advantage in instruction following (+0.93 (calculated from table values 2.1÷2.2) vs strongest baseline) far exceeds that in motion quality (+0.32 (calculated from table values 1.0→1.0)), indicating that scaling primarily addresses "understanding instructions" rather than "generating motion."

Category-wise Performance (HY-Motion's Strongest Areas)

  • Instruction following: locomotion (a) highest at 3.76, game characters (f) lowest at 3.01—complex character motions remain a weakness.
  • Motion quality: locomotion best at 3.59, fitness (c) weakest at 3.28.

Model Instruction Following Motion Quality

Key finding: DiT-0.46B-400h (only 400h data) achieves motion quality of 3.31, close to 1B's 3.34, but instruction following of only 3.05, far below 1B's 3.34—data scale has a much greater impact on semantic understanding than model capacity.

Boundaries and Limitations

  • Complex instructions remain a hard problem: The authors explicitly acknowledge insufficient understanding of highly detailed/compositional instructions, rooted in the annotation pipeline's difficulty in generating complete and accurate text descriptions for subtle motions—this is an inherent ceiling of data engineering, not something model architecture alone can solve.
  • Missing human-object interaction (HOI): The current representation lacks object geometry information, making it impossible to guarantee accurate physical contact points when generating interactions like tool use or cart pushing. This is a fundamental limitation at the representation level, not addressable by training tricks.
  • No standard benchmark metrics reported: The paper does not provide quantitative comparisons such as FID or R-Precision on HumanML3D, preventing direct alignment with historical work. The authors may have deliberately avoided this—given the significant differences in data distribution and evaluation protocols from HumanML3D.
  • Key engineering details undisclosed: Training steps, batch size, hardware configuration, and inference latency are all omitted, raising the reproduction barrier.

Engineering Insights

  • Validate the data pipeline before discussing models: HY-Motion's core moat is 3,000 hours of cleaned data. For reproduction, prioritize verifying GVHMR reconstruction quality (foot-sliding filtering thresholds, abnormal pose detection parameters) and VLM annotation consistency—these two steps determine the ceiling for all subsequent training.
  • Scale selection matters: If the goal is motion quality priority (e.g., film previsualization), the 0.46B Lite version suffices; if the goal is complex instruction following (e.g., virtual human assistants), the 1B model is necessary. Do not blindly pursue larger models—first assess the downstream task's demand for semantic understanding.
  • Most common pitfalls: ① After removing velocity/foot-contact labels, ensure physical rewards (foot-sliding penalties in Flow-GRPO) are strong enough, otherwise motion smoothness degrades; ② Asymmetric attention mask implementation is error-prone—the attention mask for text tokens must be explicitly zeroed, otherwise training becomes unstable; ③ The narrow-band temporal mask's window size (121 frames) is strongly coupled to the sampling rate—changing the sampling rate requires re-tuning.
  • RL stage offers high cost-effectiveness: Although DPO (9,228 pairs) and Flow-GRPO improvements are not individually ablated in the paper, the comparison between 0.46B-400h and 0.46B suggests that data volume has a much larger impact on instruction following than RL—prioritize pretraining data quality before investing in RL tuning.
