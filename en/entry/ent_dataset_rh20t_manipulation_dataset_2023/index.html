Overview

The RH20T Manipulation Dataset is a large-scale real-world robot manipulation dataset constructed by Cewu Lu's team at Shanghai Jiao Tong University. It contains over 110,000 contact-rich manipulation sequences, each recording visual, force, audio, and action information simultaneously, accompanied by corresponding human demonstration videos. What it truly changes is not the sheer volume of data, but rather enabling "one-shot imitation learning" to move beyond simple pushing and grasping tasks toward complex real-world skills that require multimodal perception to solve.

What It Is: A Precise Definition of the RH20T Manipulation Dataset

The RH20T Manipulation Dataset belongs to the dataset type, with the full English name RH20T Manipulation Dataset. It was released in 2023 by Hao-Shu Fang, Hongjie Fang, Zhenyu Tang, Jirong Liu, Chenxi Wang, Junbo Wang, Haoyi Zhu, Cewu Lu, and others from Shanghai Jiao Tong University. Its domains include Data and Datasets (09_data_datasets) and AI Models and Algorithms (07_ai_models_algorithms), with a value chain level of intelligence.

From an ontological commitment perspective, a dataset belongs to the Information Entity—an object existing in symbolic or data form. The core composition of RH20T is over 110,000 contact-rich real-world robot manipulation sequences, covering diverse skills, scenes, robots, and camera viewpoints. Each sequence includes visual, force, audio, and action information, accompanied by corresponding human demonstration videos. The task sources for the dataset are: 48 tasks selected from RLBench, 29 tasks selected from MetaWorld, and 70 self-developed tasks that "robots frequently encounter and can accomplish."

Why It Exists: Pain Points and Historical Positioning

Before RH20T emerged, the field of robot manipulation learning faced a structural dilemma: data scale and task complexity could not be achieved simultaneously. Mainstream datasets at the time (such as BridgeData V2, DROID, etc.) provided large numbers of demonstrations, but task types were mostly concentrated on simple scenarios like pushing and pick-and-place, relying primarily on visual guidance. However, the real world contains numerous complex skills—insertion, wiping, door opening, bottle cap twisting—whose success depends on precise regulation of contact forces and contact patterns, for which vision alone is far from sufficient.

RH20T's positioning is precisely to fill this gap: it explicitly adopts contact-rich as the core standard for data collection and introduces multimodal signals such as force and audio. Its design goal targets one-shot imitation learning—enabling robots to generalize to new tasks based on a small number of demonstrations. This goal complements the rise of action generation methods such as Diffusion Policy: Diffusion Policy solves the problem of modeling multimodal action distributions, while RH20T provides the real-world data containing contact feedback needed to train such policies.

Principle Breakdown

① Multimodal Synchronous Collection: Complete Recording of Contact Information

Each sequence in RH20T simultaneously records visual, force, audio, and action information. The platform configuration is: each platform includes a robotic arm with a force-torque sensor, a gripper, 1-2 in-hand cameras, 8-10 global RGBD cameras, and 2 microphones. Operators perform intuitive and precise teleoperation through haptic devices and pedals, with all equipment connected to a data collection workstation.

② Data Modalities and Frequency Specifications

The data modalities and collection frequencies of RH20T are shown in the following table:

Modality Size Frequency
RGB Image 1280 × 720 × 3 10 Hz
Depth Image 1280 × 720 10 Hz
Binocular Infrared Image 2 × 1280 × 720 10 Hz
Robot Joint Angles 6 / 7 10 Hz
Robot Joint Torques 6 / 7 10 Hz

Among these, the fingertip tactile sensing modality is only available in Robot Configuration 7 (Robot Cfg. 7). All sensors undergo rigorous calibration to ensure data quality.

③ Teleoperation First: The Philosophy of Collecting Contact-Rich Data

The RH20T team emphasizes that, unlike previous approaches that simplified teleoperation interfaces using 3D mice, VR controllers, or smartphones, they value the critical role of intuitive and precise teleoperation in collecting contact-rich data. The combination of haptic devices and pedals allows operators to perceive contact forces in real time and finely adjust actions—this is a prerequisite for generating high-quality contact-rich demonstrations.

RH20T's differentiated advantage lies in the fact that it is the only large-scale manipulation dataset that incorporates force and audio as standard modalities in every sequence, and it explicitly centers on contact-rich tasks—this forms a complementary positioning with OmniAction's "proactive manipulation" and DROID's "visual robustness."

Who Uses It: Application Cases

The primary users of RH20T include:

  1. One-shot imitation learning researchers: The title of the RH20T paper states its goal directly—"Learning Diverse Skills in One-Shot." Researchers use its pairs to train policies, enabling robots to generalize to new tasks based on a single demonstration.
  2. Multimodal policy learning teams: Since each sequence simultaneously contains visual, force, and audio information, RH20T is an ideal data source for training visual-tactile fusion policies, and even vision-language-action models (VLAs). This aligns closely with directions such as Learning Visuotactile Skills (2024) and Tactile-VLA (2025) in the knowledge graph.
  3. Contact-rich task benchmarking: RH20T's 48 RLBench tasks and 29 MetaWorld tasks provide a bridge aligned with simulation benchmarks, while the 70 self-developed tasks cover real contact scenarios that are difficult to model in simulation.

Limitations and Boundaries

  1. Teleoperation dependency: All RH20T data is collected via teleoperation with haptic devices and pedals, meaning data quality is highly dependent on operator skill. Although the team emphasizes the "intuitive and precise" teleoperation design, operator training costs cannot be ignored when scaling up.
  2. Platform heterogeneity: Data comes from multiple robot configurations (with 6 or 7 joints), and fingertip tactile sensing is only available in Configuration 7. When transferring across platforms, the issue of missing modalities requires additional handling.
  3. Frequency limitations: All modalities are uniformly collected at 10 Hz. For fine manipulations requiring high-frequency force feedback (such as high-speed insertion), 10 Hz may be insufficient to capture transient contact events—this is an engineering judgment that needs to be validated in the context of specific tasks.
  4. Inherent limitations of real-world collection: Unlike simulation data, RH20T cannot provide infinitely diverse scene variations; its coverage is limited by the task design and environment setup at the time of collection.

Common Misconceptions

  1. "RH20T contains millions of demonstration pairs"—Inaccurate. The paper abstract clearly states that the dataset contains "over 110,000 contact-rich manipulation sequences," while "millions of pairs" is an expression of the overall vision in the paper's introduction, not the actual data scale.
  2. "RH20T is a purely visual dataset"—Wrong. One of its core innovations is precisely the introduction of force and audio modalities; every sequence includes visual, force, audio, and action information.
  3. "RH20T is only applicable to single-arm manipulation"—To be confirmed independently. The platform configurations mentioned in the corpus include robotic arms and grippers, but are not explicitly limited to single-arm setups; its task sets (such as RLBench and MetaWorld) include bimanual tasks, but the specific collection configurations require consulting the original paper.
  4. "Contact-rich data does not require teleoperation"—Quite the opposite. The RH20T team explicitly emphasizes that intuitive and precise teleoperation is key to collecting contact-rich data, in contrast to simplified approaches using 3D mice or VR controllers.
