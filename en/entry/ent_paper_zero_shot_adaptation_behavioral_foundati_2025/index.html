Summary

Behavioral Foundation Models (BFMs) proved successful in producing policies for arbitrary tasks in a zero-shot manner, requiring no test-time training or task-specific fine-tuning.

Sources

  • arXiv:2505.13150 Zero-Shot Adaptation of Behavioral Foundation Models to Unseen Dynamics

Belief-FB (BFB)

  • The permutation-invariant transformer encoder \( f_{dyn} \) processes the trajectory \( H = \{(s_t, a_t, s_{t+1})\} \) and outputs a dynamics encoding \( h \in \mathbb{R}^d \).
  • Reparameterized sampling \( z_i = \mu_i + \varepsilon_i \odot \exp(\log \sigma_i) \) makes \( h \) follow a Gaussian prior.
  • Auxiliary loss \( L_{context} = \frac{1}{B \cdot T} \sum \| \hat{s}_{i,t+1} - s_{i,t+1} \|_2^2 \) uses the next-state predictor \( g_{pred} \) to predict the next state.
  • Key design: FB training is separated from \( f_{dyn} \) pretraining (in stages), and conditioning is applied only to the forward network \( F(s, a, [h; z_{FB}]) \), while the backward network \( B \) is shared across contexts—the authors found that conditioning \( B \) produces a smooth Q-function, which harms performance.

Rotation-FB (RFB)

  • Replace the uniform prior with a von Mises-Fisher distribution: z_FB ~ vMF(μ=h, κ), where h is the context direction output by f_dyn.
  • Sampling implementation: First select a base vector (e.g., the North Pole), perturb it with vMF noise, then rotate it to the h direction via Householder reflection, avoiding the high computational cost of direct vMF sampling.
  • κ controls policy diversity: low κ produces overlapping clusters, while high κ makes task vectors more specialized.
  • Theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4): Under the block-separated parameterization, the regret bound depends only on the maximum cone size k_max rather than the total number of tasks k, i.e., ε_k = max_j ε|C_j| ≤ ε_kmax*.
