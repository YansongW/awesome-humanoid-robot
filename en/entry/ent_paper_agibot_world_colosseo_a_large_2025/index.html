Summary

The implementation path of AgiBot World Colosseo is to first encode language instructions, camera images/multi-view observations, ontology states and joint sequences into multimodal representations, and then use VLM semantic planning/routing, latent variables/action tokens, MM-DiT/Transformer action heads to predict whole-body trajectories/action sequences, end effectors/wrist targets, and action chunk/tokens. The key is to break down tasks into routable skills or expert strategies, which are then selected and combined in execution using high-level modules.

Overview

AgiBot World Colosseo is a large-scale real-world robot manipulation platform built by the AgiBot team, comprising 100 homogeneous dual-humanoid robots, a 4,000-square-meter facility, and over 1 million trajectory data entries. Its core contribution lies in systematically addressing the issues of fragmented robot manipulation data and uncontrollable quality through standardized collection pipelines, human-in-the-loop quality control, and the accompanying GO-1 (ViLLA framework) policy model, while demonstrating a power-law scaling relationship between data scale and policy performance.

What It Changes

The robot manipulation field has long been trapped in the dual dilemma of "data hunger" and "data messiness." Existing datasets such as Open X-Embodiment, despite their large scale, are essentially simple concatenations of data from multiple labs—heterogeneous hardware, non-standardized collection protocols, and quality control lacking human feedback force models to expend significant capacity adapting to data distribution differences rather than learning manipulation skills themselves. DROID attempts to solve the scale problem through crowdsourcing, but its fixed single-arm hardware and unverified trajectory quality limit its generalization capability. What AgiBot World truly changes is the paradigm of "data production": it no longer assumes data is readily available and passively collectible, but instead treats data collection itself as a core component requiring engineering design—from hardware homogenization and teleoperation standardization to iterative human-in-the-loop quality loops, each step eliminating systematic noise in the data. This "data factory" approach allows subsequent model training to focus on algorithmic innovation rather than data cleaning, which is the most fundamental difference from all previous datasets.

Method Breakdown

Data Collection Infrastructure

  • Hardware Homogenization: 100 AgiBot G1 humanoid robots, dual 7-DoF arms + mobile base + adjustable waist, modular end effectors (standard gripper or 6-DoF dexterous hand), 8-camera configuration (1 RGB-D + 3 front fisheye + per-wrist RGB-D/fisheye + 2 rear fisheye), synchronized recording of images and proprioception at 30 Hz.
  • Dual-Channel Teleoperation: VR headset (gesture → end-effector pose → inverse kinematics → joint angles) and full-body motion capture (human joints including fingers → robot pose mapping), with the latter supporting individual finger movement and torso posture, providing the data foundation for dexterous manipulation.

Three-Stage Collection Pipeline

  1. Pre-collection Validation: Confirm task feasibility and establish collection standards (e.g., trajectory duration, action规范性).
  2. Skilled Operator Collection: Execute according to standards, verify completeness locally, then upload to the cloud.
  3. Post-processing Annotation: Annotators verify episode-by-episode compliance with standards and attach language annotations.

Key Design Decisions

  • Failure Recovery Data Retention: Approximately 1% of trajectories include segments where operators made errors, recovered, and completed the task, with failure causes and timestamps manually annotated. The authors argue this data is indispensable for policy failure reflection and robustness alignment.
  • Human-in-the-Loop Iteration: Collect small-scale demonstrations → train policy → deploy and evaluate → revise collection protocols based on failure modes. For example, after discovering prolonged pauses at action initiation, a post-processing step was introduced to eliminate idle frames.

GO-1 Model (ViLLA Framework) Three-Stage Training

  • Stage 1 Latent Action Model (LAM): Train an encoder-decoder on internet-scale heterogeneous data (including Ego4D human videos). The encoder is a spatiotemporal transformer with causal temporal masking, and the decoder is a spatial transformer, quantizing via VQ-VAE objective to obtain latent action tokens, with codebook size |C| and k=4 tokens.
  • Stage 2 Latent Planner: Uses InternVL2.5-2B as the VLM backbone (24-layer transformer, fully bidirectional attention), taking multi-view images (head, left wrist, right wrist) and language instructions as input to predict latent action tokens.
  • Stage 3 Action Expert: Diffusion objective models the continuous distribution of low-level actions, decoding action chunks A_t = [a_t, ..., a_{t+H}] with H=30. Conditions include multi-view images, proprioceptive state p_t, and language instructions.

Design Rationale: The latent action space is orders of magnitude smaller than OpenVLA's discrete low-level action space, enabling general-purpose VLMs to efficiently adapt to robot policies; the 2B parameter scale of InternVL2.5-2B was shown in preliminary experiments to balance performance and computational cost.

Key Innovations

  1. Data Quality as a First-Class Citizen: AgiBot World is the first platform to systematically incorporate "human-in-the-loop validation" and "failure recovery data" into dataset design. Previous datasets either relied entirely on automated collection (DROID) or lacked quality feedback mechanisms, whereas the three-stage validation pipeline and failure trajectory annotation here directly improve the data signal-to-noise ratio—ablation experiments show that 528 human-validated trajectories outperform 482 unvalidated ones by 0.18 completion score, proving quality matters more than quantity.
  2. Latent Action Space as a Bridge Between VLM and Robot Policies: GO-1's LAM compresses high-dimensional continuous actions into discrete latent tokens, allowing general-purpose VLMs like InternVL2.5-2B to be used for robot control without architectural modification. This design avoids the semantic gap caused by directly discretizing low-level actions in OpenVLA, representing a significant engineering breakthrough in the "VLM-as-policy" approach.
  3. Scalability Validation of Homogeneous Hardware + Standardized Pipeline: Data collected from 100 homogeneous robots eliminates cross-hardware domain differences, making scaling law research possible—performance follows a power-law relationship with trajectory count (Pearson r=0.97), marking the first clear scaling signal validated on real-world robot manipulation data and providing quantitative evidence for future data scale decisions.

Boundaries and Limitations

  • Missing Simulation Environment: All evaluations are conducted in the real world; the simulation environment is still under development, limiting the efficiency of rapidly reproducible benchmarks and ablation studies.
  • Dexterous Hand Tasks Not Individually Evaluated: Although the hardware supports 6-DoF dexterous hands, the paper does not provide quantitative results for dexterous hand tasks, leaving their actual gains unclear.
  • Multi-Robot Collaboration Not Quantified: The platform supports collaborative tasks, but no evaluation data for multi-robot collaboration is provided.
  • Extremely Low Proportion of Failure Recovery Data: Only approximately 1%, with no systematic exploration of its applicability across different task types or optimal proportions.
  • Not Explicitly Stated in the Paper: Specific GPU models, training duration, inference latency, total model parameter count (beyond the 2B VLM backbone), and other engineering details.

Engineering Insights

  • First Verify Data Quality Protocols: When reproducing or using this dataset, be sure to retain its "human validation" and "failure recovery" annotation fields—ablation experiments prove these contribute significantly to performance (+0.18 completion score); discarding them wastes data value.
  • Focus on Latent Action Space Rather Than Direct VLM Fine-Tuning: The core of GO-1 lies in LAM compressing actions into tokens; directly discretizing low-level actions (as in the OpenVLA approach) loses semantic information. When reproducing, prioritize reproducing LAM's VQ-VAE training rather than jumping directly to Stage 2/3.
  • The Scaling Law Is a Double-Edged Sword: The power-law relationship with r=0.97 implies diminishing returns from doubling data volume, but it also indicates the current data scale has not yet saturated. Downstream teams should decide based on their task complexity whether to continue collecting data or optimize model architecture.
  • Most Likely Pitfall: Trajectory duration distribution varies greatly (30-60 seconds dominant, some exceeding 2 minutes), whereas DROID/OXE mostly range from 5-20 seconds. If mixing training directly, time-scale mismatches need to be addressed; it is recommended to group by task type or ensure temporal alignment when using action chunks (H=30).
  • Hardware Homogeneity Is an Implicit Prerequisite: All conclusions are based on 100 homogeneous robots. If downstream teams use heterogeneous hardware, the dataset's pretrained weights may not transfer directly, requiring additional domain adaptation steps.
