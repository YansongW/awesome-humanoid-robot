Summary

The full-size open-source humanoid reference platform from China's National and Local Co-built Humanoid Robot Innovation Center, 185 cm and over 80 kg with 43 active degrees of freedom including dexterous hands, open-sourced under the OpenAtom Foundation with an MPC plus WBC whole-body control framework deployable in MuJoCo.

Overview

OpenLoong (Open Dragon) / "Qinglong" is a full-size open-source humanoid robot reference platform launched by the National and Local Co-built Humanoid Robot Innovation Center (Shanghai, established 2023-12-28, inaugurated 2024-05-17) and Humanoid Robot (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. On 2024-12-19, it passed the TOC review of the OpenAtom Foundation and was donated to the foundation for incubation and operation. The robot stands over 185 cm tall, weighs over 80 kg, and has 43 active degrees of freedom (including five-finger dexterous hands). The open-source content includes hardware blueprints, a whole-body dynamics control framework based on MPC + WBC (deployable in MuJoCo simulation), and datasets. What it truly changes is not "yet another humanoid robot," but rather making the core technologies (hardware blueprints + control framework + datasets) of full-size humanoid robots available to the entire industry in the form of a reference platform.

Content

What It Is: Precise Definition

OpenLoong is a full-size open-source humanoid robot reference platform project. The term "reference platform" is analogous to a Reference Design in the electronics industry: it is not a product sold to end users, but a validated, complete technical solution that third parties can replicate, modify, and reproduce. Its open-source content covers three levels:

  1. Hardware layer: The OpenLoong-Hardware repository, containing design specifications, STEP models, circuit schematics, and installation/maintenance manuals;
  2. Software layer: The OpenLoong-Dyn-Control repository, a whole-body dynamics control framework based on MPC (Model Predictive Control) + WBC (Whole-Body Control), deployable in MuJoCo simulation;
  3. Data layer: The OpenLoong-Dataset repository, providing robot operation data.

The project is positioned as an industry "reference platform/core technology" and has been called by the media the world's first full-size humanoid full-stack open-source project. The main code repositories use the Apache-2.0 license; the OpenLoong-Hardware and OpenLoong-Dataset repositories use custom/unspecified licenses (marked NOASSERTION on GitHub).

Why It Exists: Pain Points and Historical Positioning

The humanoid robot industry faces a fundamental dilemma: reinventing the wheel. Every startup must start from motor selection, structural design, dynamics modeling, and control framework construction, with R&D cycles spanning years and extremely high costs. The technical barrier for full-size humanoid robots is particularly high—unlike small bipedal or quadrupedal platforms, full-size means higher inertia, greater joint torque requirements, and more complex whole-body dynamic coupling.

OpenLoong's historical positioning is to open-source the "wheel" itself. It is led by the National and Local Co-built Humanoid Robot Innovation Center (a national-level platform) and incubated and operated through the OpenAtom Foundation—meaning it is not a company's commercial project but an industry infrastructure. In 2025-08, the community also launched NanoLoong (a small biped) which is lighter and lower-cost and has been open-sourced, further lowering the entry barrier.

Principle Breakdown

① Whole-Body Dynamics Control: The Hierarchical Architecture of MPC + WBC

The core of OpenLoong-Dyn-Control is the hierarchical combination of MPC (Model Predictive Control) and WBC (Whole-Body Control). MPC operates on a slower timescale (typically 50–100 Hz), predicting the optimal center-of-mass trajectory and footholds over a future time horizon based on a simplified model (such as a linear inverted pendulum or centroidal dynamics); WBC operates on a faster timescale (typically 500–1000 Hz), converting the high-level commands from MPC into joint torques that satisfy whole-body dynamics constraints.

The mathematical essence of WBC is a constrained quadratic programming (QP) problem:

\[ \min_{\ddot{q}, \tau, \lambda} \sum_{i} w_i \| J_i \ddot{q} + \dot{J}_i \dot{q} - \ddot{x}_i^{\text{des}} \|^2 \]
\[ \text{s.t.} \quad M(q)\ddot{q} + C(q,\dot{q}) + G(q) = S^T \tau + J_c^T \lambda \]

The first line minimizes task tracking errors (\(J_i\) is the task Jacobian for the \(i\)-th task, \(\ddot{x}_i^{\text{des}}\) is the desired task acceleration), and the second line is the floating-base dynamics equation (\(M\) is the mass matrix, \(C\) is the Coriolis/centrifugal term, \(G\) is the gravity term, \(S\) is the selection matrix, \(J_c\) is the contact Jacobian, and \(\lambda\) is the contact force). Constraints also include friction cones, joint torque limits, and joint position limits. OpenLoong-Dyn-Control provides three examples—walking, jumping, and blind stepping over obstacles—and has achieved walking and blind obstacle stepping on the physical prototype.

② EtherCAT Bus: The Deterministic Foundation for Joint Communication

OpenLoong uses EtherCAT as its joint communication bus. EtherCAT's core feature is "processing on the fly": slave stations read and write data immediately as the Ethernet frame passes through, without needing to receive the entire frame before forwarding. This ensures that multi-axis joint control achieves deterministic latency comparable to dedicated hardware buses over a single Ethernet cable—for whole-body control with 43 active degrees of freedom, the determinism of communication latency directly determines the stability of the control loop.

OpenLoong's uniqueness lies in the combination of full-stack open source + full-size. Academic open-source platforms (such as ODRI Bolt) are mostly small robots that cannot cover the dynamics challenges of full-size platforms; commercial companies' full-size robots (such as Unitree H1) are not open source. OpenLoong fills the gap between these two.

Who Is Using It · Application Cases

  • Industry replication: As a reference platform, OpenLoong's hardware blueprints can be used directly by third-party manufacturers for production. Media reports state "pricing is ultimately set by manufacturers"—meaning hardware costs are determined by the replicating party.
  • Control algorithm research: OpenLoong-Dyn-Control can be deployed in MuJoCo simulation, providing three examples—walking, jumping, and blind stepping over obstacles—allowing researchers to conduct whole-body control algorithm research without physical hardware.
  • Ecosystem project integration: Ecosystem projects such as dora-rs/dora-openloong have integrated Intel RealSense D435 RGB-D cameras and microphone arrays on the physical robot for perception and interaction research.
  • Education: NanoLoong (a small biped launched in 2025-08) further lowers the learning barrier and is suitable for university teaching.

Limitations and Boundaries

  1. Unknown hardware cost: The open-source reference platform is not sold directly, the total BOM price has not been published, and actual production costs must be estimated independently (engineering judgment: the BOM for a full-size 43-DOF humanoid robot is typically on the order of hundreds of thousands of RMB, but this figure requires independent verification).
  2. Opaque actuator specifications: Specific motor/gearhead models and torque parameters lack a unified specification sheet; reference must be made to the selection documents in the OpenLoong-Hardware repository.
  3. No unified sensor list: The official release does not include a unified sensor list; physical integration solutions depend on ecosystem projects' own component selection.
  4. Low beginner friendliness: The research archive assesses it at 2 / 5—debugging, maintenance, and safety requirements for full-size platforms are far higher than for small platforms.
  5. Inconsistent licensing: The hardware and dataset repositories use custom/unspecified licenses (marked NOASSERTION on GitHub), requiring careful evaluation for commercial use.

Common Misconceptions

  1. "OpenLoong is a robot you can buy"—Wrong. It is a reference platform and is not sold directly; hardware is manufactured and priced by producers. What you get is the blueprint, not the complete machine.
  2. "Open source = low cost"—Wrong. Open source saves R&D costs, not manufacturing costs. The materials and machining costs for a full-size 43-DOF platform remain high.
  3. "MPC + WBC was invented by OpenLoong"—Wrong. Both MPC and WBC are mature academic methods (the QP formulation of WBC dates back to the 2000s). OpenLoong's contribution lies in engineering, modularizing, and open-sourcing them.
  4. "43 degrees of freedom = 43 motors"—Inaccurate. The DOF count includes joints in the five-finger dexterous hands, which may have coupled designs where multiple motors drive one joint or one motor drives multiple joints (engineering judgment; specific structures require reference to the hardware repository).
