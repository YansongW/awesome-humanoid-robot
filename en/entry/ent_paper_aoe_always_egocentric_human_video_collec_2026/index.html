Summary

Embodied foundation models require large-scale, high-quality real-world interaction data for pre-training and scaling. However, existing data collection methods suffer from high infrastructure costs, complex hardware dependencies, and limited interaction scope, making scalable expansion challenging. Institutions per source list: Ant Digital、CAS、浙大、北大、BAAI.

Overview

AoE (Always-on Egocentric) is a large-scale first-person human video collection system designed for embodied intelligence, proposed by the research team. It utilizes a neck-mounted phone holder and smartphone to achieve low-cost, non-invasive real-world interaction data collection. Its core contribution lies in treating "humans themselves as physical embodied agents," leveraging an edge-cloud collaborative architecture and automated annotation pipeline to transform passive video data into training signals usable for robot policy learning, while validating its effectiveness when jointly trained with teleoperation data.

What It Changes

The field of embodied intelligence has long been constrained by a fundamental contradiction: policy learning requires large-scale, fine-grained interaction data, yet existing collection methods are either costly (teleoperation >$50k/user), invasive (AR/VR >500g), or yield low-quality data (passive video). Datasets such as Ego4D and Epic-Kitchens are substantial in scale but lack the atomic action-level annotations required for policy learning, and extracting high-quality segments from noisy streams demands extensive manual curation. What AoE truly changes is the economics of data collection—compressing per-user costs from tens of thousands of dollars to under $20 while maximizing scalability to five stars, making "globally distributed human agents" feasible. This is not merely hardware simplification but a redefinition of the data production paradigm: instead of relying on laboratory environments or specialized equipment, it leverages natural human interactions in daily life to continuously produce first-person videos with physical interaction dynamics at near-zero marginal cost.

Method Breakdown

The AoE system design revolves around the core philosophy of "minimal hardware, software compensation," detailed as follows:

Hardware Layer

  • Neck-mounted phone holder (supporting mechanical, MagSafe, and magnetic interfaces), stabilizing the phone on the chest with a rear ultra-wide-angle lens capturing natural interaction views
  • Auxiliary stabilization strap reduces camera shake during dynamic manipulation
  • Total assembly cost <\(20, compared to teleoperation >\)50k, UMIs \(300-800, wearables >\)2k

On-Device Processing (Privacy-First)

  • Lightweight vision models: hand detection, motion tracking, open-set recognition, triggering recording only upon detecting relevant hand-object interactions
  • All inference and raw data storage remain local, with automatic de-identification of sensitive attributes (faces, text) before upload
  • Continuous non-mutable audio signals alert bystanders during recording, and user manual review and authorization are required before cloud transmission

Cloud Automated Annotation Pipeline (6 Stages)

  1. Camera Calibration: Factory calibration intrinsics obtained via Camera2 API, with deviation from offline target calibration <1% (mean 0.64%, std 0.21%), eliminating device-specific calibration overhead
  2. Atomic Action Segmentation: Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B segments video into semantic atomic segments, with human verification correcting VLM hallucinations
  3. Camera Trajectory Estimation: Lingbot-Depth refines RGBD input, MegaSAM estimates trajectories using robust kernels adjusted for motion blur
  4. Hand Reconstruction: HaWoR performs 3D joint recovery, rescaled via depth estimation, MANO outputs transformed to world coordinates via SLAM poses, with sliding window optimization enforcing kinematic consistency
  5. Data Augmentation: Hand replacement follows Masquerade (GAN removal, 6-DoF robot alignment, photometric re-rendering); background replacement employs depth- and segmentation-conditioned video diffusion
  6. Quality Control: Automatic filtering of kinematic outliers (>3σ joint velocities) and high reprojection errors (>5 pixels), 5% manual inspection, failed samples relegated to a hard negative pool

Edge-Cloud Collaborative Architecture

  • Proximate edge ingestion nodes with intelligent routing reduce transmission latency (from 500ms+ to under 100ms)
  • Declarative workflow orchestration with modular algorithm integration and hot-swappable components
  • Kubernetes cloud-native architecture with HPA real-time monitoring of queue depth and processing latency, partitioned GPU/CPU heterogeneous resources

Policy Training

  • Fine-tuning pretrained GR00T N1.5 with FLARE loss: future latent alignment loss + action flow matching loss for joint training; action flow matching loss only for teleoperation-only training
  • Fine-tuning steps positively correlated with dataset size and task difficulty (100k steps for scarf folding, 60k steps for closing laptop)

Key Innovations

  1. Economic Reconstruction of Data Collection: Compresses per-user hardware costs to <$20 while maintaining four-star data quality (versus one star for passive video). This is the first system to turn "humans as distributed sensors" from concept into engineering reality, enabling global-scale data crowdsourcing.

  2. Privacy-First Architecture Design: On-device inference + local storage + pre-upload de-identification + continuous audio alerts + user manual authorization—a five-layer protection mechanism addressing the most sensitive ethical issues in first-person video collection. The non-mutable audio signal design is particularly ingenious, safeguarding bystander awareness while preventing malicious muting.

  3. Cross-Embodiment Transfer Without Action Labels: Aligning latent representations of future human actions via FLARE loss allows robot policies to learn state transition rules from human videos lacking action labels. This breaks the dependence of teleoperation data on action labels, expanding data sources from laboratories to the real world, with experiments demonstrating that 200 AoE clips significantly improve policy success rates.

Ablation Study (Close Laptop)

  • Sparse data (10 teleoperation clips) baseline completely failed (0% progress)
  • Adding 200 AoE clips guided the policy to 55% SR

Calibration and Reconstruction Accuracy

  • Factory intrinsics vs offline calibration deviation: mean 0.64%, std 0.21%
  • Hand pose AUC >0.90
  • ATE <5mm after 7-DoF alignment (all datasets); ATE-S of 16.2mm on EgoDex
  • Real-to-sim reconstruction average penetration depth <2mm

Distributed System Performance

  • Data transmission latency: reduced from 500ms+ to under 100ms
  • Algorithm integration cycle: shortened from weeks to days
  • Supports thousands of concurrent devices with minute-level response to workload peaks

Boundaries and Limitations

  • Deformable Object Manipulation Failure: Fold Scarf task SR only 10.0%, attributed by authors to hardware latency limitations hindering high-frequency reactive control—indicating AoE data offers limited help for tasks requiring real-time tactile feedback
  • EgoDex Accuracy Limitations: ATE-S of 16.2mm stems from inherent motion blur and misalignment in the dataset; textureless backgrounds further reduce accuracy
  • Non-Invasiveness Not Perfect: ★★★★✩ rather than ★★★★★; the presence of a lightweight holder remains a distracting factor
  • Inference Frequency and Training Configuration Undisclosed: The paper does not mention model inference frequency or specific training hyperparameters, introducing uncertainty in reproduction
  • Peak Demand Resource Allocation: The system cannot dynamically adjust resource allocation under concurrent data streams from thousands of devices, leading to over-provisioning waste and under-provisioning bottlenecks
  • Privacy Protocol Details: Crowdsourcing economic incentive designs such as fee settlement and minimum video duration thresholds are mentioned but their effectiveness is not discussed in the paper

Engineering Insights

  • Verify Calibration Deviation First: Factory intrinsics (Camera2 API) with <1% deviation from offline calibration are the cornerstone of system accuracy; validate device intrinsic quality during reproduction, with radial distortion coefficients needing stability at the 10⁻³ order
  • Quality Control Thresholds Are Critical: Filtering standards of >3σ joint velocity and >5 pixels reprojection error directly impact data quality; validate these thresholds' effects on downstream policies with small samples first
  • The Hardest Pitfall Is Deformable Objects: Fold Scarf's zero improvement indicates AoE data is effective for rigid object manipulation but offers limited help for deformable materials like cloth—if downstream tasks involve such operations, do not expect AoE data to solve them
  • Data Ratio for Joint Training: The 4:1 ratio of 50 Teleop + 200 AoE shows significant gains on simple tasks (+50pp), but only +20pp on complex tasks (Push Bowl & Pour Seeds); adjust the ratio based on task difficulty
  • Fine-Tuning Steps Need Task-Specific Adjustment: 100k steps for scarf folding vs 60k steps for closing laptop indicates overfitting risk varies with task difficulty; caution is needed when fine-tuning simple tasks with large datasets
  • Privacy Compliance Is a Prerequisite for Deployment: The four-layer mechanism of on-device inference + de-identification + audio alerts + user authorization is indispensable; missing any layer may cause data collection to fail ethical review
  • Distributed Architecture Elasticity Is a Double-Edged Sword: HPA and heterogeneous scheduling solve resource efficiency, but dynamic adjustment under peak demand remains a bottleneck; plan capacity in advance for large-scale deployment
