Summary

We introduce Humanoid-GPT, a GPT-style Transformer with causal attention trained on a billion-scale motion corpus for whole-body control. Unlike prior shallow MLP trackers constrained by scarce data and an agility-generalization trade-off, Humanoid-GPT is pre-trained on a 2B-frame retargeted corpus that unifies all major mocap datasets with large-scale in-house recordings. Institutions per source list: 清华大学、银河通用、上海交大、北京大学、上海期智研究院.

Overview

Humanoid-GPT is a general-purpose online humanoid motion tracker based on a GPT-style causal attention Transformer. By scaling training data to 2B frames (over 200x larger than prior work) and pairing it with Harmonic Motion Embedding (HME) data curation and expert distillation pipelines, it achieves zero-shot motion tracking of unseen dance movements. The system runs closed-loop control on the Unitree-G1 at 50 Hz with end-to-end inference latency below 1.5ms, significantly outperforming existing baselines in both simulation and real-world experiments.

What It Changes

Motion tracking has long been dominated by shallow MLPs, with training data stuck at 10^4 trajectories (approximately 7.2M frames). This created a seemingly irreconcilable "agility-generalization" trade-off: the tracking sharpness of in-domain agile motions and the ability to generalize to unseen styles were difficult to achieve simultaneously. The authors' core judgment is that this trade-off is not a fundamental limitation but a symptom of insufficient data scale and mismatched training design—the large-scale scaling path from the language/vision domains has never been seriously attempted in motion control.

What truly changes is the establishment of the "scaling hypothesis" in motion tracking. The authors not only expanded data by two orders of magnitude but, more importantly, demonstrated that when data grows from millions to billions of frames, the choice of model architecture (causal Transformer vs MLP) determines whether performance keeps improving or saturates early. This breaks the default "small data + shallow model" paradigm in motion tracking, reframing the problem as a joint optimization of data curation, architecture matching, and training stability, rather than mere algorithmic tuning.

Method Breakdown

Data Curation and HME Sampling

  • Aggregates AMASS, LAFAN1, MotionMillion, PHUMA, and internally collected data, mapped via motion retargeting to the Unitree-G1's 29-DoF space
  • Filters out sequences with object interactions (chair sitting, swimming, etc.), applies time-warping augmentation (uniform acceleration/deceleration) to expand data approximately 5x, yielding a 2B-frame corpus
  • Trains multiple Periodic Autoencoders to extract per-joint periodic amplitude/frequency, aggregated into HME vectors; K-Means clustering produces approximately 300 motion clusters (1k–2k sequences each)
  • Key design: HME enables diversity-aware, distribution-balanced sampling—insufficient diversity causes overfitting to frequent patterns, while insufficient balance limits the capability ceiling

Motion Expert Training

  • Trains a PPO policy per cluster π: 𝒢 × 𝒮 ↦ 𝒜, with state comprising joint positions/velocities, root angular velocity, projected gravity, and previous actions; outputs are converted to torques via a PD controller
  • Rewards are computed at the body keypoint level: R_kpt(t) = R_pos(t) + R_rot(t) + R_vel(t) + R_penal(t), where R_pos(t) = Σ w_k exp(−α_pos ‖e^pos‖₁), α_pos=1.0, α_rot=2.0, α_vel=0.03; lower-body weights are 1.5, upper-body 0.75
  • After training, only high-fidelity, long-term stable experts are retained (approximately 384 as the optimal balance point)

DAgger Distillation to a Generalist Policy

  • Employs the DAgger framework: iteratively rolls out the student policy and queries expert target actions, training the student to match expert outputs
  • Input token e_t = [s_t; q_t^ref] (proprioception + target reference pose concatenated), with a sequence of length H=32 passed through a time-causal masked Transformer
  • Loss is SmoothL1Loss, Equation (2): â = ⋃ concat_{k∈[-H+1,0]} t_i(s_{t-k}, g_{t-k}), l = ℒ(G_θ(e_{t-H+1:t}), â)
  • At inference, maintains a queue of H historical tokens, taking the last position's output as the control target; batch size must be no smaller than the number of experts to avoid mode collapse

Architecture Selection Rationale

  • Motion tracking is inherently causal (no future observations at test time), aligning GPT-style causal attention with deployment constraints
  • Transformers scale more cleanly with data/model size compared to MLPs and non-causal variants
  • Parallel sequence supervision + autoregressive temporal prediction leverages the Transformer's inherent strengths, implicitly learning position-invariant temporal prediction, enabling stable outputs even at episode start (when history is scarce)

Key Innovations

  1. Data Scale Crossing a Critical Threshold: The 2B-frame training corpus is 20x larger than the largest prior work (SONIC's 100M frames) and over 200x larger than mainstream baselines (7.2M frames). This is not an incremental improvement but the first push of motion tracking into the "data-limited" regime—experiments show diminishing marginal gains from 200M→2B, indicating model capacity becomes the new bottleneck, a phenomenon never observed in the small-data regime.

  2. HME-Driven Distribution-Balanced Sampling: Existing work either samples randomly (uncontrolled diversity) or uniformly (overfitting to frequent patterns). HME extracts harmonic features via periodic autoencoders and clusters them, achieving dual constraints of "diversity-aware + distribution-balanced." The authors explicitly emphasize that both are indispensable—this is an important addition to data curation methodology, not mere data stacking.

  3. Causal Transformer as Control Architecture: While Transformers have been applied in motion generation, using them as the core architecture for an online tracker and systematically validating the synergy of "causality + scaling" is novel. The key insight is that causal attention not only satisfies deployment constraints but also solves the cold-start problem at episode start through implicit temporal prediction—a property absent in both MLPs and TCNs.

Boundaries and Limitations

The paper does not explicitly mention detailed failure case analysis, performance floors for specific motion categories, or discussions of hardware wear or safety in real-world deployment. Acknowledged boundaries include: not incorporating richer modalities such as contact, vision, or language; not extending to interactive or multi-agent scenarios; and not coupling with long-horizon planning or VLA-style instructions.

Notable implicit limitations: All real-world experiments are based on dance motions (highly dynamic but without object interaction), which may not apply to tasks requiring environmental contact (e.g., manipulation, climbing)—such sequences were explicitly filtered during data curation. Additionally, the number of experts (approximately 384) is tightly coupled with the number of clusters; if the target motion distribution deviates significantly from the training distribution, HME clustering coverage may be insufficient.

Engineering Insights

For reproduction or adaptation, the following aspects should be prioritized:

  1. Data pipeline is the biggest engineering risk: The acquisition, retargeting, filtering, and augmentation pipeline for the 2B-frame corpus accounts for a far larger share of total effort than model design. If reproducing the same data scale is infeasible, first verify whether the HME sampling strategy still outperforms random sampling at the 100M-frame scale—this determines the marginal value of data curation.

  2. Expert count and distillation stability: Approximately 384 experts is the balance point among diversity, intra-cluster consistency, and computational cost (128 is too coarse, 1024 too expensive). During DAgger distillation, batch size must be no smaller than the number of experts, otherwise mode collapse is likely—this is the most common pitfall.

  3. History length H=32 is a compromise between computation and performance: Experiments show that 64 frames of history continue to improve performance, but computational cost grows quadratically with sequence length. If the downstream task is latency-sensitive, first verify whether 16 frames are acceptable; if pursuing maximum precision, try 64 frames and accept increased inference latency.

  4. Domain randomization parameters need platform-specific tuning: Parameters such as ground friction 𝒰(0.3, 2.0), external force intervals 𝒰(5.0, 10.0), and torso CoM variation 𝒰(−0.15, 0.15) are tuned for the G1. When porting to other hardware, sensitivity analysis is recommended first, particularly regarding the impact of armature scaling (𝒰(1.0, 1.05)) on sim-to-real gaps.

  5. Deployment stack is open-sourced: ONNX export + TensorRT optimization (causal attention and fused MLP kernels) + C++ streaming pipeline, with 50 Hz closed-loop control. If inference latency exceeds targets during reproduction, first check whether TensorRT has truly fused the causal attention mask—this is the key to the 1.5ms latency.
