Summary

Generating natural and physically plausible character motion remains challenging, particularly for long-horizon control with diverse guidance signals.

Overview

UniPhys is a diffusion-based behavior cloning framework that unifies motion planning and control into a single model, jointly modeling state and action distributions conditioned on text. Through the Diffusion Forcing training paradigm, latent action representation, and flexible denoising schedules, it enables physics-based character control without task-specific fine-tuning, supporting tasks such as text-driven control, velocity control, goal reaching, and obstacle avoidance.

What It Changes

Previous works (such as CLoSD) separated the diffusion planner from the RL tracking controller, leading to domain gaps and degraded motion quality, and required task-specific fine-tuning. UniPhys fundamentally changes this paradigm: instead of treating planning and control as two independent modules, it eliminates the hierarchical gap by jointly modeling state and action distributions. This means downstream tasks no longer require retraining a controller for each new control signal; instead, they adapt directly through test-time guidance losses. This fundamentally changes how physics-based character control is deployed—shifting from "customization at training time" to "guidance at inference time."

Method Breakdown

Behavior Representation

  • Behavior sequence X = x_{1:T}, where x_t = (s^c_t, z_t), with z_t ∈ R^32 being the latent action representation from the PULSE encoder
  • State S^c includes global root trajectory (position, orientation, linear velocity, angular velocity) and local joint features (position, velocity, rotation, angular velocity), with rotations represented in 6D
  • Per-frame feature dimension is 398 (32-dimensional latent action + 366-dimensional state)

Training

  • Follows the Diffusion Forcing paradigm, injecting random noise levels k = k_{1:T} ∈ [K]^T independently for each frame
  • Uses a 12-layer causal transformer decoder with hidden size 768, conditioned on CLIP text embeddings
  • Training loss: L(θ) = E_{k,X^0}[||X^0 - M_θ(X^k, k, c)||^2]
  • During training, sequences are divided into 32-frame segments with stride 8, batch size 1024, and learning rate 1.5 × 10⁻⁴

Inference

  • Receding-horizon policy and autoregressive behavior synthesis, supporting flexible denoising configurations (history length h, prediction horizon H, action execution steps T_a, denoising schedule)
  • Stabilization trick: replace zero values in the noise level matrix with a stabilization noise level n to prevent the model from being overconfident
  • Monte Carlo Guidance (MCG) estimates gradients from multiple samples to reduce variance

Task-Specific Guidance

  • Goal reaching: position loss + orientation loss, H=28, T_a=8
  • Velocity control: velocity magnitude + direction + heading loss, H=28, T_a=8
  • Obstacle avoidance: SDF-based softplus smooth loss, H=28, T_a=8

Key Innovations

  1. Unified Planning and Control: This is the first work to truly unify planning and control into a single diffusion model. Previous methods were either two-stage (planner + controller) or purely kinematic. UniPhys eliminates the hierarchical gap by jointly modeling state and action distributions, enabling direct adaptation to new tasks through loss guidance at test time without fine-tuning.

  2. Per-Frame Independent Noise Injection: Unlike traditional diffusion models that apply a uniform noise level to the entire sequence, UniPhys injects noise levels independently for each frame. This design allows the model to handle flexible noise configurations at inference (such as autoregressive denoising and progressive denoising) and addresses the distribution shift introduced by physics simulators.

  3. Stabilization Trick: During denoising, the noise indicator for fully denoised frames is set to a value greater than zero, adjusting only the noise indicator without adding noise to the state-action predictions. This simple trick significantly improves long-horizon rollout stability, increasing the average episode length from 148.2 to 2320.3 (under the autoregressive denoising configuration).

Boundaries and Limitations

  • Low inference efficiency: The diffusion framework has lower inference efficiency than RL policies, achieving approximately 10 FPS (autoregressive denoising) and 18 FPS (progressive denoising) under text-driven control, which is insufficient for real-time control needs.
  • Balance loss and falls: Balance loss can still occur during highly dynamic actions or when text instruction switching is poorly timed, leading to failed skill transitions and falls; completely avoiding falls is unrealistic.
  • Fall recovery not implemented: Plans to collect expert demonstrations of getting up from the ground and train a dedicated RL policy are in place but not yet completed.
  • Terrain adaptation not implemented: Due to the lack of terrain-specific data, walking on different terrains cannot be immediately achieved under the behavior cloning framework.
  • Dexterous hand control not included: The current method does not include dexterous hand control, limiting human-object interaction tasks.
  • Zero-shot generalization to unseen tasks: The paper does not mention details of zero-shot generalization tests on unseen task types.

Engineering Insights

  • Verify the data pipeline: Dataset construction relies on the PULSE tracking policy to track AMASS motions, storing successfully tracked sequences. When reproducing, confirm PULSE's tracking success rate, as failed sequences are discarded, potentially introducing data bias.
  • Stabilization noise level is a key hyperparameter: Text-driven control uses a stabilization noise level of 3 (1, 2, or 3 is sufficient; increasing to 5 reduces stability). Loss-based guidance requires higher stabilization noise levels and needs task-specific tuning.
  • MCG sample count affects success rate: N=1 yields only a 26% success rate, while N=5 reaches 98%. When computational resources allow, prioritize N=5.
  • Denoising schedule selection: Autoregressive denoising produces more stable rollouts, while progressive denoising improves performance on long-horizon planning tasks. Use autoregressive for text-driven control (H=8) and progressive for goal reaching/velocity control/obstacle avoidance (H=28, T_a=8).
  • Inference efficiency bottleneck: FPS ranges from 9.5 to 23, far below real-time requirements. If downstream applications need real-time control, consider distillation or more efficient sampling strategies.
  • Training cost: Approximately 10 GPU days on a single A100, with 15k iterations. The loss decreases only slightly during training, but policy stability and motion semantic fidelity continue to improve—observe patiently rather than relying solely on the loss curve.
