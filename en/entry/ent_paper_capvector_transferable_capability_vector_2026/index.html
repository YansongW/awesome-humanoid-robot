Summary

This paper proposes a novel approach to address the challenge that pretrained VLA models often fail to effectively improve performance and reduce adaptation costs during standard supervised finetuning (SFT). Institutions per source list: HKUST(GZ)、浙江大学、西湖大学、清华大学、北京智源人工智能研究院等.

Overview

CapVector proposes a method for extracting and merging "capability vectors" in parameter space, transferring the general capability gains brought by auxiliary-objective SFT (such as Spatial Forcing) to a pretrained VLA model. This allows downstream tasks to achieve performance and efficiency close to auxiliary training using only standard SFT, with negligible additional overhead. The core contribution lies in systematically introducing model merging ideas to VLA post-training for the first time, and designing orthogonal regularization to prevent capability forgetting.

What It Changes

Standard SFT of pretrained VLA models often performs mediocrely on complex downstream tasks, while advanced fine-tuning with auxiliary objectives significantly improves performance and convergence speed but incurs 28% additional FLOPs and 17% additional GPU memory due to extra forward passes. What CapVector truly changes is the entrenched notion that "performance gains must be tied to training overhead"—it demonstrates that the general capabilities gained from auxiliary training can be decoupled, extracted, and solidified into model parameters, thereby allowing downstream fine-tuning to return to the simplicity of standard SFT. This effectively transforms "training tricks" into "model attributes," representing a substantial simplification of the post-training paradigm.

Method Breakdown

Capability Vector Extraction (Stage 1)

  • On a small task set D_ext, train two models using standard SFT and auxiliary-objective SFT respectively, obtaining θ_ft and θ_ao.
  • Assuming their task-related vectors are approximately equal (Δ_ft ≈ δ_ao), the capability vector is γ_ao = θ_ao − θ_ft.
  • Construct a meta-model: θ_meta = θ_pt + α·γ_ao, where α is the merging weight (default 1.1, optimal in ablation).

Downstream Fine-tuning (Stage 2)

  • Initialize from θ_meta and fine-tune with standard SFT to obtain θ_ft′ = θ_meta + Δ_ft′.
  • Introduce orthogonal regularization: ℒ_orth = Σ_p Σ_{i,j} |γ_ao^(p){ij} · Δ_ft′^(p)|, enforcing orthogonality between the capability vector and the new task update direction.
  • Total loss: ℒ = ℒ_action + λ·ℒ_orth, with λ defaulting to 1e-4.
  • For LoRA fine-tuning, the orthogonal loss is computed only on matrix A, since A represents the update direction while B serves as the linear weighting coefficient.

Key Design Rationale

  • Orthogonality stems from the nature of fine-tuning: parameter changes encapsulate the model's update direction, and orthogonality prevents interference between the capability vector and the new task vector.
  • Unlike simple model ensembling, CapVector extracts vectors through parameter arithmetic rather than weighted averaging of multiple model outputs.

Key Innovations

  1. Parametric Extraction and Transfer of Capability Vectors: First systematic application of model merging to VLA post-training, extracting general capability gains via parameter subtraction and injecting them into the pretrained model via addition, enabling cross-task and cross-model capability transfer.
  2. Orthogonal Regularization to Prevent Capability Forgetting: Explicitly constrains the capability vector to be orthogonal to the new task update direction during downstream fine-tuning, ensuring transferred capabilities are not overwritten—a key mechanism for long-term performance stability.
  3. Near-Zero-Overhead Alternative to Auxiliary Training: Compared to Spatial Forcing's additional 5.0T FLOPs (28%) and 10.9G GPU memory (17%), CapVector adds only 0.3G FLOPs (<0.002%) and 0.5G GPU memory (<0.8%), obtaining auxiliary training's performance gains almost for free.

Key Conclusions

  • On LIBERO, CapVector achieves 91.7% average success rate at 5k steps, surpassing Spatial Forcing's 87.5%, and reaches 97.1% after convergence (150k steps), outperforming all baselines.
  • In RoboTwin cross-domain transfer, CapVector (D_ext=Spatial) achieves 31.8%, close to Spatial Forcing's 33.1% and far exceeding standard SFT's 6.7%.
  • In real-world long-horizon precision manipulation tasks (sequentially transferring 4 test tubes), success rate improves from 0.36 to 0.53.

Boundaries and Limitations

The paper does not explicitly declare limitations. Inferred from factual points: 1) Only two auxiliary-objective methods (Spatial Forcing and LaRA-VLA) are validated, not covering other auxiliary training strategies; 2) Capability vector extraction depends on task relevance between D_ext and D_down, and transfer effectiveness drops significantly when D_ext has low visual richness (e.g., LIBERO-90, achieving only 9.4% on RoboTwin); 3) Cross-embodiment validation only involves two platforms (ARX Lift 2 and AgileX Cobot), with details of a third platform undisclosed; 4) Applicability of capability vectors to non-visual-dominant tasks (e.g., pure force control or high-frequency feedback tasks) is not analyzed; 5) Capability vector acquisition in RL post-training settings is explicitly left for future work.

Engineering Insights

When reproducing, prioritize verifying three points: α merging weight (default 1.1; deviation may cause 2-4% performance degradation), orthogonal loss weight λ (default 1e-4; too large may suppress new task learning), and D_ext selection (visual richness and task relevance directly affect transfer effectiveness; LIBERO-Spatial is the best default choice). The most common pitfall is computing the orthogonal loss only on matrix A during LoRA fine-tuning—if mistakenly computed on B, it breaks the linear weighting relationship and renders the capability vector ineffective. Engineering recommendations: 1) First extract capability vectors on a small-scale D_ext (e.g., 10 tasks) to validate α sensitivity; 2) Monitor ℒ_orth values during downstream fine-tuning—if it keeps increasing, the capability vector is being overwritten and λ should be increased; 3) For cross-domain transfer, prioritize D_ext with visual distributions close to the target domain, avoiding task sets with high visual discrepancy (e.g., LIBERO-90).
