Summary

World models are widely explored in embodied intelligence, yet they typically predict distinct evolutions of the world and the ego within a single stream, where the world captures persistent instruction-agnostic scene regularities and the ego captures robot-centric instruction-conditioned dynamics. Institutions per source list: CAS、UCAS、上海交大、北大.

  • arXiv:2605.19957 World-Ego Modeling for Long-Horizon Evolution in Hybrid Embodied Tasks

Overview

This paper proposes the World-Ego Modeling (WEM) paradigm, which explicitly decomposes future evolution in long-horizon mixed embodied tasks into two state streams: a persistent scene (world) and an instruction-conditioned self (ego), and constructs the HTEWorld benchmark based on BEHAVIOR-1K. The authors achieve decoupling through semantically masked boundary definitions, state predictors with asymmetric query budgets, and a CP-MoE diffusion generator, significantly surpassing baselines such as PAN-style (58.40) and Cosmos-Predict 2.5-14B (55.41) with a score of 61.48 across 16 EWMScore metrics.

What It Changes

Existing embodied world models cram world evolution and ego behavior into the same latent space stream. This is tolerable in short-horizon tasks, but once entering long-horizon scenarios of hundreds of steps where navigation and manipulation interleave, errors from single-stream prediction accumulate snowball-style through chunk-wise autoregressive rollout. Scene consistency across distant chunks (e.g., object permanence, layout stability) and instruction alignment (e.g., "first grab the cup, then walk to the table") interfere with each other, causing generated content to become both distorted and unfocused over long ranges. What the authors truly change is decomposing the single problem of "predicting the future" into two separable sub-problems: the world state answers "how the scene persists," and the ego state answers "how the robot acts on the scene." This conceptual split allows the model to allocate different capacities, attention ranges, and update frequencies to the two evolutions at the architectural level, rather than relying on implicit learning to distinguish them—which has proven unreliable over long horizons.

More critically, the authors do not stop at the conceptual level. They provide actionable boundary definitions (motion, semantic, and intent perspectives) along with corresponding decoupling implementations (pre-/post-/full decoupling), and construct HTEWorld, the first benchmark that simultaneously evaluates navigation-oriented scene imagination and manipulation-oriented physical simulation. This transforms "world-ego decoupling" from a philosophical proposition into a quantifiable, ablatable, and reproducible engineering solution, offering a clear comparison anchor for subsequent research.

Method Breakdown

Overall Architecture

WEM consists of two stages: - Prediction stage: A vision-language state predictor \( \Phi_\phi \) infers the world state \( \mathbf{S}^w_k \) and ego state \( \mathbf{S}^e_k \) from multimodal history (initial frame \( \mathbf{O}_0 \), historical videos \( \mathbf{V}_{<k} \), action sequence \( a_{\leq k} \)). - Generation stage: A CP-MoE diffusion generator \( \mathcal{D}_\theta \) generates the current chunk video \( \hat{\mathbf{V}}_k \) conditioned on \( \mathbf{S}^w_k, \mathbf{S}^e_k \).

Key Designs of the State Predictor

  • Asymmetric query budget: Among 256 learnable queries, 192 are allocated to the world and 64 to the ego. Rationale: the world needs to encode persistent structures accumulated over long history (layout, object permanence), while the ego only needs to encode instruction-conditioned dynamics for the current step.
  • Role-Conditioned Attention (RCA): World queries attend only to the full visual history and past instructions, masking current instructions and ego queries; ego queries attend to each other, the current instruction, and the most recent \( K \) instruction-video pairs, masking distant history and world queries. This forces the two state streams to be orthogonal in terms of information sources.

Key Designs of the Generator

  • Pre-expert: A shared DiT backbone performs self-attention on noisy latent variables, with two parallel cross-attention streams (text instruction, \( \mathbf{S}^w_k \), \( \mathbf{S}^e_k \)); the outputs of the two streams are summed and passed through an FFN.
  • Semantic head: A lightweight Dense Prediction Transformer extracts multi-scale features from encoder layers {5, 9, 13, 17, 21, 23}, outputting world-ego masks \( \mathbf{M} \) in a coarse-to-fine manner. Mask ground truth comes from simulator instance segmentation labels (robot body, end effector, and currently manipulated object as ego; the rest as world).
  • Routing and de-routing: The mask \( \mathbf{M} \) serves as a routing signal, assigning tokens to either world experts or ego experts; the active token set for each expert is expanded to spatiotemporal neighbors of the assigned region (to avoid seam artifacts); after role-expert computation, outputs are reassembled into a single sequence.
  • Comparison of three decoupling strategies:
  • Pre-decoupling: separation at the input of the post-stage, handled by restricted cross-attention.
  • Post-decoupling: separation at the output of the post-stage, with the mask as a soft fusion of the two expert outputs.
  • Full decoupling (default): combines routing, expert specialization, and de-routing.

Training Objective

Total loss \( \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{mask}} \), where \( \mathcal{L}_{\text{mask}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{Dice}} \), with \( \lambda = 0.3 \) annealed to 20% of its initial value. The flow-matching loss is inherited from the pretrained DiT (Wan2.2-TI2V-5B).

Ablation Variants

  • Equal query budget: 128 queries each for world/ego.
  • Relaxed RCA: allows query groups to access inputs from the other role.
  • Disabled neighbor-expanded routing: only processes mask-assigned tokens.

Key Innovations

  1. Three-perspective definition of the world-ego boundary: The motion perspective (residual optical flow), semantic perspective (instance segmentation masks), and intent perspective (conditioning information sources) provide actionable and complementary boundary criteria for decoupling. The semantic perspective wins by default (EWMScore 61.48 vs. motion 59.36 vs. intent 58.69), indicating that in mixed tasks, object interaction states (whether being manipulated) characterize the world-ego boundary more stably than pixel-level motion patterns.

  2. Asymmetric query budget + Role-Conditioned Attention: This is an architectural-level implementation of the intuition that "the world needs long memory, the ego needs short instructions." The 192/64 allocation and RCA's attention masking enable the world state to stably encode cross-chunk scene constancy, while the ego state remains undisturbed by distant history—ablations show that removing either design hurts performance (60.50/60.64 vs. 61.48).

  3. Neighbor-expanded routing in CP-MoE: Directly routing tokens to role experts via semantic masks produces seam artifacts (discontinuities at boundaries). By expanding the active token set to spatiotemporal neighbors of the assigned region, ablations show this design contributes the most (dropping to 59.57 vs. 61.48, a loss of 1.91 points). This indicates that the benefit of decoupling comes not only from the conceptual split but also from engineering treatment of generation quality at boundaries.

Boundaries and Limitations

  • Simulation environment limitations: Experiments are conducted only in the BEHAVIOR-1K simulator, which cannot capture real-world visual diversity, perceptual noise, object variation, or contact dynamics; sim-to-real generalization is not validated.
  • Boundary construction dependency: The default semantic perspective relies on instance segmentation labels, requiring additional preprocessing for datasets without such annotations; the motion perspective is less robust under large viewpoint changes or contact-rich interactions; the intent perspective yields the weakest separation (58.69).
  • Residual long-horizon degradation: Autoregressive rollout still accumulates errors across chunks, and consistency between distant chunks is harder to maintain than intra-chunk local coherence—RCBD of only 0.31 is evidence.
  • Unexhausted design space: Boundaries could be defined via 3D geometry, affordances, controllability, causal interactions, or task progress; CP-MoE is just one way to implement decoupling; the state predictor uses a frozen VLM which may be suboptimal, and world/ego query capacities are fixed.
  • Downstream applications unexplored: The paper only evaluates video generation quality, not downstream tasks such as policy learning, planning, autonomous driving, or interactive simulation.

Engineering Insights

  • Verify mask quality first: WEM's benefits are highly dependent on the accuracy of semantic masks. When reproducing, ensure consistency between simulator segmentation labels and annotations for the robot body/end effector/manipulated objects; mask noise directly contaminates the routing signal.
  • Neighbor expansion is the biggest lever: Ablations show that removing neighbor-expanded routing causes the largest drop (−1.91), so prioritize correct implementation of this module during reproduction—the spatiotemporal neighbor expansion range for active token sets requires tuning; too small leads to seam artifacts, too large defeats decoupling.
  • Mind baseline inference adaptation: Cosmos-Predict2.5 and WoW-7B differ greatly in chunk conditioning (latent frames vs. pixel frames, 10 frames vs. 41 frames); comparisons must strictly follow the paper's protocol, otherwise fairness is questionable.
  • LoRA is not viable here: The authors explicitly found that LoRA consistently underperforms full fine-tuning, attributed to the domain gap between internet video pretraining and first-person manipulation scenes. If compute is limited, prioritize reducing epochs rather than downgrading to LoRA.
  • For long-horizon evaluation, look at CISR/CPDM: These two metrics best reflect cross-chunk semantic consistency; if your method shows limited gains on these, decoupling has not truly improved long-range memory—small RCBD improvements may only reflect local visual continuity gains.
  • Don't over-interpret WorldArena results: WEM scores below IRASim and CtrlWorld on WorldArena, but that benchmark is not designed for mixed navigation-manipulation tasks; cross-benchmark ranking differences may reflect task distribution mismatch rather than methodological flaws.
