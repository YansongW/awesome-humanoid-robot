Summary

Unsupervised zero-shot reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for pretraining behavioral foundation models (BFMs), enabling agents to solve a wide range of downstream tasks specified via reward functions in a zero-shot fashion, i.e., without additional test-time learning or planning.

ReLA（Residual Latent Adaptation）

  • Core idea: Starting from the latent variable \( z = z_r \) obtained through zero-shot inference, use off-policy actor-critic to perform gradient ascent in the latent space, while freezing the pretrained actor and successor feature networks.
  • Q-function decomposition: Utilize the identity from Proposition 1, \( Q^{π_z}_r(s,a) = ψ(s,a,z)^T z_r + Q^{π_z}_{r−φ^T z_r}(s,a) \), to split the Q-function into a "pretrained predictable part" and a "residual part".
  • Residual critic: Train only a small network \( Q^{residual}(s,a;θ) \) (2-layer MLP, hidden dimension 64) to compensate for reward projection errors, with the target being the TD error \( y_i = r_i + γ(ψ(s'_i, π_z(s'_i), z_r)·z_r + \min(Q^{residual}_{θ1'}, Q^{residual}_{θ2'})) \).
  • Latent actor update: The gradient is computed only with respect to z, \( \nabla_z E_{s∼D_{online}}[ψ(s,π_z(s),z_r)^T z_r + Q^{residual}(s,π_z(s);θ)] \), keeping the pretrained actor parameters completely frozen.
  • Design rationale: The search space is reduced from millions of parameters to a few hundred dimensions (the dimensionality of z), and the residual network only needs to compensate for projection errors, making it much smaller than a full critic, thus avoiding the sample complexity of learning a critic from scratch.

LoLA（Lookahead Latent Adaptation）

  • Core idea: A hybrid on-policy actor-only method that combines fixed-horizon rollouts with the frozen terminal value function estimate from BFM to compute returns.
  • Policy parameterization: The latent policy is a normal distribution \( π_{μ,σ} = N(μ,σ) \), with a trainable mean μ (initialized to z_r) and a fixed diagonal covariance σ.
  • n-step lookahead return: \( R^n(s_0,z) = Σ_{t=0}^{n−1} γ^t r(s_{t+1}) + γ^n ψ(s_{n+1},π_z(s_{n+1}),z)^T z_r \), using pretrained successor features to estimate the terminal value, avoiding full Monte Carlo trajectories.
  • Gradient estimation: Uses a leave-one-out baseline for variance reduction, \( E_{s_0∼ν}[1/k Σ_{i=1}^k (R(s_0,z_i) − 1/(k−1) Σ_{j≠i} R(s_0,z_j)) ∇_μ log π_{μ,σ}(z_i)] \), where \( z_1,…,z_k ∼ π_{μ,σ}(·) \).
  • Practical details: z is normalized to the hypersphere via projected gradient descent; initial states are sampled from a mixture of the environment's initial distribution d_0 and the online replay buffer D_online.
