Summary

The implementation path of LingBot-VLA is to first encode language instructions, camera images/multi-view observations into multimodal representations, and then use VLA multimodal action models and VLM semantic planning/routing prediction to execute action commands. The key point is to preserve the semantic understanding of VLM while adding robot states and action heads to avoid staying only at language planning.

Sources

  • LingBot-VLA project page

Overview

This paper introduces LingBot-VLA, a vision-language-action (VLA) foundation model for real-world bimanual manipulation tasks, developed by the Robbyant team. Its core contribution lies in systematically studying the scaling laws of VLA models on 20,000 hours of real robot data, and significantly surpassing existing methods on the GM-100 benchmark through the Mixture-of-Transformers architecture, Flow Matching action modeling, visual depth distillation, and distributed training optimization.

What It Changes

What this work truly changes is the community's perception of "data scalability" for VLA models. Previously, VLA research mostly remained at the level of validation on small-scale data or simulation environments, lacking rigorous empirical evidence on large-scale real data across multiple platforms under a unified framework. LingBot-VLA demonstrates with 20,000 hours and 9 robot configurations that real robot performance continuously improves with increasing pretraining data scale, with no saturation observed at 20,000 hours—this conclusion directly addresses the core debate of "whether data remains the first-principle bottleneck for VLA."

Second, it changes the standard for VLA training engineering. Existing open-source codebases (such as π_0) are limited by data I/O and communication overhead in multi-node scaling, whereas LingBot-VLA achieves 261 samples per GPU per second on 8 GPUs through FSDP+HSDP hybrid sharding strategies and operator-level optimizations, a 1.5–2.8× speedup. This means large-scale VLA pretraining is no longer a privilege of institutions with supercomputing centers, but a reproducible engineering practice.

Method Breakdown

Model Architecture: Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)

  • Integrates a pretrained VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) with a randomly initialized action expert, performing unified sequence modeling across layers through shared self-attention.
  • Observation context is defined as O_t = [I_t^1, I_t^2, I_t^3, T_t, s_t], containing three-view images, task instructions, and robot state.
  • Action sequence A_t = [a_t, ..., a_{t+T-1}], with action chunk length T=50 during pretraining.

Action Modeling: Flow Matching

  • Training objective is the conditional flow matching loss: L_FM = E ||v_θ(A_{t,s}, O_t, s) - (A_t - ε)||², where intermediate action A_{t,s} = sA_t + (1-s)ε.
  • Compared to discrete tokenization or Gaussian mixtures, Flow Matching provides smoother continuous action distribution modeling, suitable for high-precision manipulation.

Attention Mechanism: Blockwise Causal Masking

  • The sequence is divided into three functional blocks: [image, instruction], [state], [action chunk].
  • Inter-block causal masking with intra-block bidirectional attention prevents future action information from leaking into observation representations.

Spatial Awareness: Visual Depth Distillation

  • Aligns the VLM's learnable queries Q_t with LingBot-Depth's depth tokens D_t, minimizing L_distill = E|Proj(Q_t) - D_t|.
  • This design directly injects geometric information, addressing VLA's inherent deficiency in precise depth reasoning.

Distributed and Operator Optimization

  • FSDP (ZeRO) shards optimizer states, parameters, and gradients; draws on HSDP to construct dedicated "shard groups" for the action expert to reduce communication overhead.
  • Mixed precision: float32 for reductions, bfloat16 for storage and communication.
  • FlexAttention optimizes sparse attention, torch.compile fuses operators to reduce kernel launch overhead.

Data Annotation Pipeline

  • Human annotators segment multi-view videos into atomic actions, removing static frames.
  • Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B is used to automatically annotate task and subtask instructions.

Key Innovations

  1. First large-scale real-world VLA scaling empirical study: Systematically demonstrates on 20,000 hours and 9 bimanual configurations that performance continuously improves with data scale without saturation. This provides the strongest evidence to date that "data scale is the core lever for VLA," rather than relying solely on architectural innovation.

  2. Visual depth distillation as a general spatial awareness module: Depth information is injected into VLM queries via distillation, rather than simple concatenation or input channel modification. This design achieves an average SR improvement of 4.28% over π_0.5 across three real-world platforms, and ablation studies show the gains are more pronounced in randomized scenarios (+9.92%), indicating depth information is critical for generalization.

  3. Distributed training system optimization tailored for VLA: The HSDP shard group design targets the heterogeneous communication patterns between the action expert and VLM in the MoT architecture, combined with FlexAttention and torch.compile, achieving 1.5–2.8× throughput improvement. This is the first public work that deeply couples system-level optimization with VLA architectural characteristics.

Experiments and Results

Real-World Benchmark (GM-100, 25 robots, 100 tasks)

Method Avg SR (%) Avg PS (%)
WALL-OSS 4.05 10.35
GR00T N1.6 7.59 15.99
π_0.5 13.02 27.65
Ours w/o depth 15.74 33.69
Ours w/ depth 17.30 35.41
  • Depth distillation yields average SR +4.28% and PS +7.76% improvements (relative to π_0.5).
  • Largest improvement on Galaxea R1Pro (SR +6.88%), with PS improvement reaching +5.54% on AgileX.

Simulation Benchmark (RoboTwin 2.0, 50 tasks)

Setting π_0.5 SR (%) Ours w/o depth SR (%) Ours w/ depth SR (%)
Clean scenes 82.74 86.50 88.56
Randomized scenes 76.76 85.34 86.68
  • Absolute SR improvement in randomized scenes reaches 9.92% (w/ depth), far exceeding the 5.82% in clean scenes, indicating depth information significantly enhances robustness to visual perturbations.

Scaling and Data Efficiency

  • Increasing pretraining data from 3,000 to 20,000 hours yields continuous downstream success rate improvements with no saturation observed.
  • Under a limited budget of only 80 demonstrations per task, LingBot-VLA outperforms π_0.5 trained on the full 130 demonstrations in both SR and PS, indicating higher data efficiency.

Training Throughput

  • On 8-GPU setups, 261 samples per GPU per second, a 1.5–2.8× speedup over existing VLA codebases.

Boundaries and Limitations

  • Limited platform coverage: Only bimanual, fixed-base/waist configurations were evaluated; single-arm or mobile robot platforms were not covered, nor were non-desktop scenarios.
  • Marginal gains from depth distillation are unstable on some tasks: In the appendix, for tasks like Click Bell, Ours w/ depth achieves only 36% SR in randomized scenes, lower than π_0.5's 66%, indicating depth information is not beneficial across all tasks.
  • Data annotation relies on manual segmentation: Video segmentation is performed by human annotators. Although instruction annotation is automated, atomic action segmentation still involves subjectivity, potentially affecting cross-platform consistency.
  • Inference frequency not reported: The paper does not specify the control frequency on real robots, which directly affects deployment feasibility assessment.
  • Scaling experiments do not explore beyond 20,000 hours: Although no saturation is observed, behavior at more extreme data scales cannot be inferred.

Engineering Insights

  • Prioritize reproducing the data pipeline: The annotation pipeline for 20,000 hours of data (manual segmentation + Qwen3-VL instruction annotation) is the foundation of performance. It is recommended to first reproduce the data format and action chunk definition (T=50) before focusing on the model architecture.
  • Depth distillation is a low-cost, high-reward module: Only a pretrained depth model (LingBot-Depth) and a projection layer are needed to achieve an average 4.28% SR improvement. Downstream teams can prioritize integrating this module, especially for visually randomized scenarios.
  • Distributed training configuration is a key bottleneck: The HSDP shard group design depends on the expert partitioning of the MoT architecture. If the VLM or action expert structure is replaced, the sharding strategy needs to be re-tuned. It is recommended to first validate throughput at small scale (8 GPUs) before scaling to multiple nodes.
  • Data efficiency advantage is worth exploiting: With limited demonstration budgets (e.g., 80 per task), LingBot-VLA still outperforms π_0.5 trained on full data, indicating its architecture utilizes data more effectively. This characteristic can significantly reduce collection costs in downstream scenarios with scarce data.
  • Watch for task-level performance fluctuations: Depth distillation may yield negative returns on certain tasks (e.g., Click Bell). Deployment decisions should be evaluated per task type rather than relying solely on average metrics.
