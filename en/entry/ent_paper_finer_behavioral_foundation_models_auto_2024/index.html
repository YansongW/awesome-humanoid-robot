Summary

The forward-backward representation (FB) is a recently proposed framework (Touati et al., 2023; Touati & Ollivier, 2021) to train behavior foundation models (BFMs) that aim at providing zero-shot efficient policies for any new task specified in a given reinforcement learning (RL) environment, without training for each new task.

Sources

  • arXiv:2412.04368 Finer Behavioral Foundation Models via Auto-Regressive Features and Advantage Weighting
  • 智元、众擎都在卷的人形机器人运控基座：41篇论文看懂BFM

Overview

This paper proposes FB-AWARE, a reward-free behavior foundation model (BFM) that combines autoregressive features with advantage weighting, aiming to address the dual shortcomings of the vanilla FB framework: the linear limitation of task encoding and poor policy optimization on offline datasets. Through theoretical proofs and benchmark experiments on D4RL, DMC, Jaco, etc., the authors demonstrate that this model matches or even surpasses single-task offline agents on zero-shot downstream tasks, and systematically analyze the modest but consistent improvements brought by autoregressive features.

What It Changes

The core assumption of the FB framework (Touati & Ollivier 2021) is a linear relationship between task encoding z and features B, which severely limits its expressive power for complex reward functions—all methods based on successor features are subject to this constraint. More critically, vanilla FB performs poorly in policy optimization on offline datasets composed of a small number of near-optimal examples, which is exactly the distribution shift problem encountered when online RL algorithms are directly applied to offline settings. What this paper truly changes is: advancing FB from the narrow applicability of "linear task encoding + online exploration data" to the general scenario of "nonlinear task manifolds + mixed offline data". The authors prove that autoregressive features can expand the optimizable reward set from a linear subspace to a nonlinear manifold, while introducing advantage weighting enables FB to train stably on low-quality, mixed-behavior datasets. This is not merely an algorithmic patch but a substantive expansion of the expressive boundaries of the FB theoretical framework—although experiments show the gains on standard benchmarks are "positive but modest," it clears theoretical obstacles for BFM applications on more complex tasks.

Method Breakdown

Autoregressive Feature Decomposition

  • Decompose task encoding z and features B into K blocks: z=(z₁,…,z_K), B=(B₁,…,B_K)
  • Compute block by block: z₁=𝔼[r(s)B₁(s)], z₂=𝔼[r(s)B₂(s,z₁)], z₃=𝔼[r(s)B₃(s,z₁,z₂)], and so on
  • Definition 3.1 (Autoregressive feature map): The i-th component of B(s,z) depends only on (z₁,…,z_{i-1}), not on subsequent components
  • Network implementation: By applying masks on fully connected layers, B(s,z) can be computed in a single forward pass, with each layer accessing previous layers of all preceding blocks

Theoretical Guarantees

  • Theorem 3.2: If B is autoregressive, then the fixed point z_r=𝔼[r(s)B(s,z_r)] always exists and can be computed component-wise directly, with π_{z_r} being the optimal policy
  • Theorem A.3: Two-block autoregressive FB suffices to represent any continuous task encoding r↦z_r, whereas vanilla FB is limited to linear encodings

Advantage-Weighted Policy Optimization

  • Objective: argmax_θ 𝔼[Σᵢ w(sᵢ,aᵢ)logπ_θ(aᵢ|sᵢ)], with weights w∝exp(A(s,a)/β)
  • Key design 1: Sample zᵢ independently for each sample (rather than a single z per minibatch), empirically leading to faster learning
  • Key design 2: Use improved weighted importance sampling (IWIS), reducing bias from O(n⁻¹) to O(n⁻²)
  • Key design 3: Train two parallel forward networks F₁, F₂, using the average rather than the min for the Bellman target (for successor measures, the conservative interpretation of min is not direct)

Training Details

  • z sampling: 50% from Gaussian distribution, 50% from data-state B representations, normalized to norm √d
  • Autoregressive FB computes B representations every 32 steps (vanilla FB every step), using a single z for the entire batch (k=1)
  • Orthogonal regularization loss ensures B approximates an orthogonal matrix
  • Residual autoregressive normalization strategy f_ar: the first component is self-normalized, subsequent components are residual-normalized, preserving relative magnitudes across groups

Key Innovations

  1. Autoregressive features break the linear task encoding limitation: This is a fundamental extension of the FB theoretical framework. In vanilla FB, all policies share the same feature B(s), and task encodings are restricted to the linear subspace spanned by the features; autoregressive features make B(s,z) depend on z, turning the task encoding map into a nonlinear manifold that can theoretically represent any continuous task encoding (Theorem A.3). This innovation elevates FB from "only handling linearly decomposable rewards" to "handling arbitrary continuous reward functions"—a qualitative leap in expressive power.

  2. Advantage weighting adapts FB to offline mixed data: The core reason vanilla FB fails in policy optimization on offline datasets is distribution shift—the mismatch between training and behavior policies distorts Q-value estimates. With the introduction of advantage-weighted regression (combining IWIS and dual-network ensembles), FB-AW jumps from a total score of 22.3 to 696.2 on D4RL locomotion, approaching the single-task SOTA XQL (752.9). This resolves the key engineering obstacle preventing FB from moving from "toy problems" to "real offline benchmarks."

  3. Balance between theoretical completeness and engineering feasibility: The authors not only prove the expressive power of autoregressive FB (Theorem A.3) but also address practical training difficulties—z sampling variance, B normalization, reward scale invariance, etc. In particular, the residual autoregressive normalization strategy maintains scale invariance without requiring the full z vector, which is a critical design enabling autoregressive FB to be trained in practice.

Experiments and Results

Jaco Arm (RND dataset, d=128)

  • FB average: 33.3±12.6; FB-AW average: 72.2±11.6; FB-AWARE(4) average: 82.5±12.6; FB-AWARE(8) average: 71.8±11.4
  • Autoregressive features yield approximately 15% relative improvement (82.5 vs 72.2)

DMC Locomotion (MOOD dataset)

  • On Humanoid, vanilla FB performance is near zero (6.5±5.7), FB-AW improves to 596.7±16.9, FB-AWARE(4) reaches 594.8±17.6
  • The autoregressive component provides slight improvements on out-of-dataset tasks (e.g., quadruped skip: FB-AW 654.6 vs FB-AWARE(4) 835.2)

Key Conclusions

  • Advantage weighting is necessary to obtain any decent performance ("In many setups, the offline techniques are needed to get any decent performance at all")
  • The impact of autoregressive features is "positive but moderate," concentrated on tasks requiring spatial precision and task generalization beyond training-set behavior
  • FB-AW and FB-AWARE overall performance matches task-specific SOTA methods such as XQL and IQL

Boundaries and Limitations

  • Limited autoregressive gains: The authors explicitly acknowledge that improvements from autoregressive features are "positive but moderate," not significant across all tasks, possibly indicating that BFM expressive power is not the key limiting factor on these benchmark tasks
  • Environment restrictions: All experiments are in noise-free, Markovian (no history dependence) continuous control environments, without partial observability or stochastic rewards
  • Dataset dependence: On low-quality RND datasets, advantage weighting actually hurts performance (Table 5); the best algorithm depends on the quality of available data
  • Theoretical cost: Autoregressive FB provides Q-function information only at z=z_r, whereas non-autoregressive FB provides Q-functions for all z—the additional expressive power comes at the cost of obtaining less information about other Q-functions
  • Computational overhead: Computing the fixed point z_r at test time requires multiple forward passes through the B network, though the authors claim the overhead is small compared to vanilla FB; during training, single-z-per-batch leads to increased variance and longer training time
  • Not addressed: Performance on larger-scale environments, hyperparameter sensitivity analysis, quantitative comparison of specific computational costs

Engineering Insights

  • Check data quality first: FB-AW performs excellently on MOOD (mixed-goal) datasets but may hurt performance on RND (low-quality exploration) datasets. When reproducing, always assess the exploration sufficiency and behavioral diversity of the dataset before deciding whether to enable advantage weighting
  • Choosing the number of autoregressive blocks: In Jaco experiments, 4 blocks outperform 8 blocks (82.5 vs 71.8), while D4RL uses 8 blocks. It is recommended to start with 4 blocks and adjust based on the spatial precision requirements of the task; autoregressive features yield the greatest gains on tasks requiring precise goal-reaching
  • Most common pitfalls: The z sampling strategy (50% Gaussian + 50% state B representations) and residual autoregressive normalization are critical for training stability; omitting them leads to training divergence or performance plateaus. Using a single z for the entire batch (k=1) increases variance but yields better convergence performance—do not casually switch to k>1
  • Use the average rather than the min for dual-network ensembles: For successor measures, the conservative interpretation of min is not direct (taking min over rewards r may encode min, but for -r it encodes max)—this is a key difference from Q-function ensembles
  • Evaluation protocol: Sample 100,000 states per task to compute the task encoding z_r, average cumulative reward over 100 episodes, repeated across 5 random seeds—strictly follow this protocol when reproducing to ensure comparability
  • Humanoid is the litmus test: Vanilla FB performance is near zero on Humanoid; if your method cannot surpass this baseline on Humanoid, the offline training techniques are not correctly implemented
