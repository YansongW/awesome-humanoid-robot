Summary

This paper presents an effective approach for learning novel 4D embodied world models, which predict the dynamic evolution of 3D scenes over time in response to an embodied agent's actions, providing both spatial and temporal consistency.

Overview

TesserAct is a 4D world model for embodied intelligence that characterizes dynamic scenes by jointly predicting RGB, depth, and normal map sequences, and achieves temporally consistent 4D point cloud reconstruction using normal integration and optical flow constraints. The method is fine-tuned on the CogVideoX architecture, trained on approximately 285k mixed synthetic/real videos, and significantly outperforms existing video generation baselines in depth/normal accuracy and 4D reconstruction quality, while directly serving downstream action planning.

What It Changes

Existing video world models (such as OpenSora, CogVideoX) operate in 2D pixel space, and their outputs lack geometric consistency—object sizes and shapes drift over time, failing to provide reliable spatial priors for tasks requiring precise 6-DoF poses such as grasping and navigation. What TesserAct truly changes is the fundamental question of "what the world model outputs": shifting from pure visual appearance prediction to joint "appearance + geometry" prediction, using depth and normals as explicit intermediate representations, thereby constraining the generation process with 3D geometry rather than pixel statistics alone.

The deeper significance of this shift is that it upgrades the world model from a "video generator" to an "actionable scene simulator." Downstream policies can directly consume 4D point clouds instead of 2D frames, and inverse dynamics models can learn action mappings in geometric space rather than pixel space. Meanwhile, by reusing the generative capabilities of existing video diffusion models (CogVideoX), the enormous computational cost of training a 4D generative model from scratch is avoided—this is the key engineering judgment that makes the work feasible.

Method Breakdown

Core Representation: RGB-DN Four-Channel Video

  • Predicts RGB, depth (relative map, [0,1]), and normal map sequences, with dimensionality lower than explicit voxels/point clouds and naturally compatible with video model input formats.
  • Depth is an affine-invariant relative map, requiring subsequent optimization to recover scale.

Model Architecture (Fine-tuned on CogVideoX)

  1. Encoding: A 3D VAE separately encodes RGB, depth, and normal videos (VAE frozen, not fine-tuned), with three independent InputProj projectors extracting embeddings for each modality.
  2. Fusion: The DiT receives the sum of the three embeddings, conditioned on text 𝒯 (action instruction + robot arm name) and denoising step t.
  3. Decoding: RGB output follows the original scheme; a new Conv3D layer concatenates the input latent variable with the predicted RGB denoising output, then combines with the DiT hidden state, and outputs depth and normal denoising predictions via DNProj.
  4. Initialization: All new modules are zero-initialized, ensuring that RGB output matches the pre-trained CogVideoX in the early training phase, stabilizing training.

Training Objective

  • Joint noise prediction loss: L = 𝔼[||[ε_v, ε_d, ε_n] − ε_θ(x_t, t, x^0, 𝒯)||²]

4D Scene Reconstruction (Inference Post-processing)

  • Normal Integration: Uses predicted normal maps to optimize depth, minimizing the spatial consistency loss ℒ_s.
  • Optical Flow Segmentation: RAFT computes inter-frame optical flow, dividing regions into static/dynamic/background based on magnitude threshold c.
  • Two New Losses:
  • Temporal consistency loss ℒ_c: Applies cross-frame depth consistency to dynamic and background regions.
  • Regularization loss ℒ_r: Constrains the optimized depth to be similar to the generated depth (weighted by dynamic/background masks).
  • Total loss: arg min_D̃ L_s + L_c + L_r, iteratively optimized from the first frame to the i-th frame.

Data Pipeline

  • Synthetic: RLBench 20 high-difficulty tasks × 1000 instances × 4 viewpoints = 80k videos, depth from simulator, normals estimated by DSINE depth2normal.
  • Real: RT1 Fractal (80k), Bridge (25k), SomethingSomethingV2 (100k), depth from RollingDepth, normals from Temporal-Consistent Marigold-LCM-normal.
  • Scene randomization (Colosseum pipeline): varying backgrounds, tabletop textures, and lighting.

Key Innovations

  1. RGB-DN Joint Generation Paradigm: For the first time, depth and normals are treated as generation targets equal to RGB within a video diffusion model, rather than post-hoc estimates. This allows geometric information to participate in constraints during generation, rather than being attached as post-processing—this is the essential difference from all baselines (OpenSora/CogVideoX post-estimation).

  2. Zero-Initialized Multimodal Fusion: By zero-initializing all parameters of the newly added depth/normal branches, the model's behavior in the early training phase is ensured to be completely consistent with the pre-trained video model, avoiding the catastrophic forgetting and training instability commonly seen in multimodal joint training. This design decision is simple yet extremely effective, and is a key engineering innovation.

  3. Optical Flow-Guided Depth Optimization Loss: Uses optical flow magnitude to distinguish dynamic/static/background regions, applying temporal consistency constraints separately. Compared to global consistency constraints, this avoids erroneous depth penalties caused by moving objects (such as robot arms), keeping the reconstructed 4D point cloud geometrically faithful in motion regions.

Experiments and Results

Experiments are conducted on two domain types: real domain (RT1 Fractal and Bridge datasets, 400 unseen samples) and synthetic domain (RLBench dataset, 200 unseen samples). Baselines include OpenSora, CogVideoX (both video diffusion models, without depth/normal annotations, estimated post-hoc with Rolling Depth and Marigold) and 4D Point-E (direct point cloud output). All metrics are averaged over 10 runs. Key results are shown in the table below (numbers taken verbatim from Table 2 of the paper):

  • Depth and Normal Accuracy Leads Across the Board: On the real domain, TesserAct's AbsRel is 22.07, outperforming CogVideoX's 26.17 and OpenSora's 31.41; δ₁ and δ₂ reach 66.80 and 82.60 respectively, both optimal. The advantage is even more pronounced on the synthetic domain, with δ₁ at 69.26, δ₂ at 93.03, and 11.25° accuracy at 36.85, significantly higher than baselines (calculated from table values 26.04→36.85). This demonstrates that explicit RGB-DN four-modality modeling outperforms post-hoc depth/normal estimation.
  • Best 4D Point Cloud Reconstruction Accuracy: TesserAct achieves Chamfer L₁ of 0.2030 on the real domain and 0.0811 on the synthetic domain, both lower than all baselines (synthetic domain reduced by approximately 72% compared to CogVideoX's 0.2884, calculated from table values 0.2884→0.0811). 4D Point-E outperforms video diffusion baselines on the synthetic domain (0.1086) but still lags behind this method, indicating that RGB-DN video representation is more efficient than direct point cloud generation, especially in capturing fine-grained details of dynamic scenes.
  • RGB Quality Trade-off: On the synthetic domain, TesserAct's FVD (40.01) and SSIM (77.59) outperform all baselines; on the real domain, FVD (21.59) and SSIM (75.86) are slightly inferior to CogVideoX (20.64 and 79.38), but the authors note this is a trade-off that "does not significantly harm RGB"—achieving substantial gains in depth, normal, and point cloud accuracy at minimal RGB cost.

Boundaries and Limitations

  • Single-Surface Representation: RGB-DN only captures a single surface of the world, unable to handle occlusion and multi-view information. The authors explicitly acknowledge that future work needs to generate multi-view RGB-DN to build complete 4D models.
  • Depth Scale Ambiguity: Depth is a relative map ([0,1]) and cannot directly recover metric scale; reconstruction relies on an optimization process that may introduce cumulative errors.
  • Real Data Annotations Depend on Estimators: RollingDepth and Marigold-LCM-normal are both estimated results with noise, potentially limiting the model's upper bound in real scenes.
  • Limited Task Coverage: Action planning is only validated on 9 RLBench tasks, without real robot deployment; 4D information provides no gain on tasks such as open microwave.
  • Missing Ablation Details: The paper does not provide quantitative comparisons of individually removing each loss term (only qualitative descriptions), nor does it report inference latency and memory usage.
  • Training Compute Cost Not Disclosed: The total training time for 40k iterations, batch size 16, and 49-frame outputs is not mentioned.

Engineering Insights

  • Reproduction Priority: First verify the data pipeline—the depth/normal estimator versions (RollingDepth, Marigold-LCM-normal, DSINE) for synthetic data (RLBench 20 tasks × 4 viewpoints) and real data (RT1/Bridge/SSv2) must be consistent; otherwise, differences in geometric supervision signal quality will directly affect the depth AbsRel metric.
  • Most Likely Pitfall: The zero-initialization strategy is key to training stability. If the newly added Conv3D and DNProj are not zero-initialized, early RGB output will deviate from the pre-trained distribution, causing FVD collapse. It is recommended to strictly follow the "zero-initialize all new modules" setting.
  • Hyperparameter Sensitivity: Loss weights λ_d=20 and λ_b=200 are universal across real/synthetic domains, but λ_g1/λ_g2 need domain-specific adjustment (20 for real, 2 for synthetic)—synthetic data has cleaner normals, so regularization weights should be reduced to avoid over-constraining generation diversity.
  • Downstream Integration: Inverse dynamics (ID) model training requires 500 samples per task, with input of 8192 point sampling + 20% Gaussian noise augmentation. If downstream tasks involve dynamic occlusion, it is recommended to first validate the sensitivity of the optical flow segmentation threshold c, as this parameter directly affects the effectiveness of ℒ_c.
  • Hardware Requirements: Not disclosed in the paper, but DiT training with 49 frames × 512×512 × 3 modalities, combined with gradient checkpointing, is estimated to require at least 8×A100 level; if resources are limited, one can first try reducing the frame count (e.g., 25 frames) to verify whether the geometric supervision gains are maintained.
