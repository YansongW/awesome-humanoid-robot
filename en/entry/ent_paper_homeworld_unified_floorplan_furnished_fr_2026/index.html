Summary

Indoor scene generation is crucial for robot simulation and modern interior design. However, complex layouts together with scarce 3D scene data make learning-based generation challenging. Overview

HomeWorld is a unified hierarchical framework for generating controllable, dense, interactive whole-house scenes from natural language prompts, proposed by the research team. Its core contribution lies in decomposing whole-house generation into four stages—floor plan synthesis, hierarchical roaming arrangement, recursive refinement, and manipulable object placement—combining LLMs trained on large-scale real floor plan data with explicit 3D anchoring to address shortcomings in existing methods regarding global coherence, physical feasibility, and simulation readiness.

What It Changes

Whole-house scene generation has long been fragmented into isolated subtasks such as floor plan synthesis, single-room layout, and object placement, resulting in generated scenes lacking global topological consistency and cross-room semantic coherence. Existing methods either rely on handcrafted rules (e.g., ProcTHOR) producing repetitive and biased layouts, or directly learn in 3D but are limited by scarce training data and computational costs, or employ 2D lifting (e.g., Text2Room) but lack strong 3D prior anchoring, leading to geometric fragmentation when scaling to whole houses. What HomeWorld truly changes is redefining "generation" as a "controllable hierarchical instantiation" process: first, an LLM synthesizes topologically correct floor plans on structured representations; then, furniture is progressively filled via 2D inpainting under explicit 3D shell constraints; finally, a VLM recursively corrects physical violations. This paradigm transforms the data-scarce 3D generation problem into a relatively data-sufficient 2D generation and 3D verification problem, simultaneously improving topological validity, object density, and physical plausibility of generated results.

Method Breakdown

Stage 1: Floor Plan Generation (LLM + K-D Tree)

  • Data construction: Collected 1.084 million floor plan images from online real estate repositories, trained a detection model on 2K manually annotated images to identify doors, windows, walls, and dimension chains; detection boxes were converted to centerlines and merged with neighboring endpoints to reconstruct vectorized structures; OCR extracted room labels and dimensions, yielding 314K validated floor plans after quality filtering.
  • Representation design: Encoded floor plans as binary K-D trees, with internal nodes storing split axes and coordinates, leaf nodes corresponding to spatial units with assigned room semantics; attachments such as doors and windows are separately represented in JSON. Compared to directly predicting polygon coordinates, the K-D tree output space is more structured, deterministically avoiding polygon overlaps and holes.
  • Training: Used Qwen3-4B-Instruct with LoRA supervised fine-tuning, with data split of 281K/15K/15K.

Stage 2: Hierarchical Roaming Arrangement (2D Lifting + 3D Anchoring)

  • Shell instantiation: Created empty unfurnished shells in Blender with basic lighting and materials, serving as explicit 3D constraints for roaming.
  • Global layout (top-down view): Rendered top-down views, annotated doors, windows, and rooms adjacent to doors, used an image inpainting model (Gemini 3.0 Image Pro Preview) to synthesize furnished top-down views, SAM-3 extracted instance masks and 2D boxes, and a VLM performed open-vocabulary category recognition.
  • Detail refinement (egocentric view): A heatmap-based viewpoint selection strategy discretized the floor into a grid, greedily selecting viewpoints covering the least observed regions; for each viewpoint, egocentric images were rendered and inpainted to synthesize secondary objects, SAM-3D reconstructed 3D geometry, and a geometry alignment module integrated objects into the room.
  • Roaming order: Large functional furniture (beds, sofas) placed first via top-down views, then small items progressively enriched via egocentric views.

Stage 3: Recursive Layout Refinement (VLM Sequential Decision)

  • Formulated refinement as a sequential decision process: a VLM refiner observes the current layout state and predicts structured corrective actions (translation, rotation, or combined transformations), actions are deterministically applied to 3D bounding boxes, re-rendered for verification, and the loop continues until validation passes or iteration limits are reached.
  • Fine-tuning data construction: Corrupted layout construction (injecting perturbations such as boundary violations, collisions, doorway blockages), oracle-labeled repair actions (scoring ground truth by residual error, newly introduced violations, movement cost, and semantic plausibility), and model-in-the-loop samples (initial refiner rollouts with intermediate states re-labeled by the oracle).

Stage 4: Manipulable Object Placement

  • Identified furniture capable of supporting small objects based on semantic categories and geometric properties (presence of horizontal surfaces and sufficient usable area).
  • Constructed local surface canvases for each furniture piece, using inpainting models to synthesize surface-level arrangements.
  • Used PhysX-Anything to infer physical properties (density, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio), estimating mass from density and mesh volume.
  • A VLM parsed synthesized surface images into manipulable object sets (semantic categories, scales, relative positions, and orientations), transformed into the global coordinate system.
  • Applied lightweight physical filtering before insertion: removed objects exceeding support boundaries, severely colliding, floating, or violating category-level support constraints, preserving explicit support relationships.

Key Innovations

  1. K-D Tree Floor Plan Representation: Transformed floor plan generation from free polygon prediction into structured recursive split prediction, fundamentally eliminating topological errors such as overlaps, holes, and disconnections. This is a reconstruction of the output space rather than a simple constraint, making LLM generation structurally valid by design and deterministically convertible back to traditional representations.
  2. Hierarchical Roaming: Combining top-down global layout first with egocentric view detail refinement, coupled with explicit 3D shell constraints, resolves geometric inconsistencies of pure 2D lifting methods at whole-house scales. This design effectively integrates 2D generation priors with 3D anchoring, avoiding the data bottleneck of direct 3D learning.
  3. VLM Recursive Refiner: Models layout correction as a sequential decision process, training a VLM to output executable 3D bounding box editing actions through a three-stage data construction of corrupted layout construction + oracle labeling + model-in-the-loop. This makes the refinement process verifiable and iterative, rather than one-shot generation.

Boundaries and Limitations

The paper does not explicitly discuss limitations, but inferences from factual points include: shell texture generation adopts simple strategies (reusing 3D-FRONT assets or synthesizing via 2D generation models) without introducing a dedicated material pipeline; lighting uses rule-based generation rather than learned methods. No mention is made of physical simulation validation results for generated scenes, generalization tests against real-world scenes, specific numbers for generation time/computational cost, or detailed statistical results from the user study. No mention of specific iteration limits for the VLM refiner, specific parameters for viewpoint selection (grid resolution, field of view, safety distance), or specific statistics on manipulable object counts (beyond >15 in Table 1). Additionally, LayoutGPT only supports living rooms and bedrooms, so it was not evaluated in kitchen and bathroom comparisons, potentially affecting the completeness of baseline comparisons.

Engineering Insights

When reproducing or adopting HomeWorld, prioritize verifying the following: Correct implementation of the K-D tree representation is the cornerstone of floor plan generation quality; ablation shows that canonical representation leads to 9.2% overlaps, 2.0% holes, and 16.2% disconnections, while K-D trees reduce disconnections to 12.3% but not to zero, requiring attention to constraints on leaf node semantic assignment. Data construction for the VLM refiner is the most error-prone area—the types and magnitudes of injected perturbations in corrupted layouts directly affect oracle labeling quality; it is recommended to strictly follow the paper's six perturbation categories (boundary violations, collisions, doorway blockages, rotation, scale, category-level errors) and retain model-in-the-loop samples to cover intermediate states. Viewpoint selection in hierarchical roaming significantly impacts final object density (FOD drops from 4.16 to 2.6 when removed); the grid resolution of the heatmap coverage strategy needs tuning based on room scale. Manipulable object placement relies on PhysX-Anything's physical property inference; it is advisable to first validate the accuracy of density and mesh volume estimation, otherwise mass estimation biases can cause physical filtering to erroneously remove objects. Finally, dataset release (300K floor plans + 5K scenes) is a key dependency for reproduction; if unavailable, one can initially train the LLM on the 314K validated floor plan subset, but distribution differences must be noted.
