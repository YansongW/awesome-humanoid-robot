Summary

World models, which are predictive representations of how environments evolve under actions, have become a central component of robot learning. They support policy learning, planning, simulation, evaluation, data generation, and have advanced rapidly with the rise of foundation models and large-scale video generation. Institutions per source list: NTU、UC Berkeley、Stanford、东京大学、Oxford、Microsoft、ETH、Princeton、Harvard.

Overview

This survey, jointly authored by Nanyang Technological University, UC Berkeley, Stanford, Microsoft, and other institutions, systematically reviews the evolving role of world models in robot learning—from decoupled video generation plus inverse dynamics pipelines, to single-backbone unified policies, MoE/MoT expert architectures, unified VLAs, and latent-space world modeling. The core contribution lies in proposing a unified probabilistic perspective (viewing policies, passive/controllable world models, and inverse dynamics models as different conditional queries of the same joint distribution) and clarifying that world models should provide three core capabilities: foresight, imagination-driven planning, and data amplification.

What It Changes

What this survey truly changes is the cognitive framework for the "functional positioning" of world models in robot learning. Previous literature either treated world models as low-cost simulators for RL or as extensions of video prediction, resulting in significant fragmentation. The authors redefine world models as a unified carrier of four roles—policy learning, evaluation, simulation, and data generation—and explicitly state that the value of world models lies not in generating photorealistic future frames, but in generating futures that are causally aligned with actions, physically self-consistent over long horizons, and sufficient to support policy evaluation and action selection. This judgment directly challenges the implicit assumption that "visual realism ≈ control utility"—the authors repeatedly emphasize that visually plausible rollouts that violate dynamics will directly undermine closed-loop control and the evaluation signals themselves.

Another key change is at the methodological level: the authors use the unified probability distribution \(p(o_{t+1:t+k}, a_{t+1:t+k} | o_t, l)\) to unify seemingly disparate architectural routes (decoupled IDM, single-backbone, MoE/MoT, unified VLA, latent-space) as different marginalization/conditioning approaches to the same objective. This transforms comparisons between methods from a zero-sum competition of "which is better" into a design-space exploration of "under what conditions which conditioning approach is superior." Additionally, the authors explicitly distinguish between two paradigms—"world model as policy" and "world model as simulator"—and note that the latter is evolving from rollout-based validation tools into learning simulators for policy reinforcement learning, post-training, and co-optimization—a trend judgment with direct implications for research resource allocation.

Method Decomposition

Unified Probabilistic Perspective

  • Core formalization: ideal joint distribution is \(p(o_{t+1:t+k}, a_{t+1:t+k} | o_t, l)\)
  • Policy model = conditioned action marginal: \(p(a_{t+1:t+k} | o_t, l)\)
  • Passive world model = conditioned observation marginal: \(p(o_{t+1:t+k} | o_t, l)\)
  • Controllable world model = action-conditioned observation: \(p(o_{t+1:t+k} | o_t, a_{t:t+k-1}, l)\)
  • Inverse dynamics model = observation-conditioned action: \(p(a_{t+1:t+k} | o_t, o_{t+1:t+k}, l)\)

Five Architectural Evolution Routes

  1. Decoupled IDM pipeline: The world model first generates future observations, then an independent policy module infers actions. Formula: \(\pi(a_{t+1,t+H'} | o_t, l) = P(a_t | E_{img}(o_t), E_{text}(l), \Phi(\hat{o}_{t+1:t+H}))\). Representatives: UniPi, Dreamitate, RoboDreamer, IRASim, EVA
  2. Single-backbone unified policy: Observation and action tokens are processed within the same generative backbone. Unified objective: \(\hat{y} = f_\theta(\tilde{x}_\tau, o_t, l, \tau)\), where \(x = [z^v; z^a]\) is the concatenation of visual and action representations. Representatives: UVA, UWA, VideoVLA, Cosmos Policy, DreamZero, GigaWorld-Policy
  3. MoE/MoT expert architectures: Retain separate expert streams with interaction via shared attention/cross-attention. Inter-layer interaction: \((h^v_{\ell+1}, h^a_{\ell+1}) = F^{mix}_\ell(h^v_\ell, h^a_\ell; o_t, l)\). Three modes: parallel expert coupling (GE-Act), deep interaction (Motus, LingBot-VA, BagelVLA), latent-space expertization (LDA-1B, FRAPPE)
  4. Unified VLA: Future modeling is internalized within the architecture. Three subcategories: explicit future state prediction (GR-1, UP-VLA, WorldVLA), implicit latent future modeling (DreamVLA, UniVLA, CoWVLA), multi-expert unification (F1, InternVLA-A1, HALO, TriVLA)
  5. Latent-space world modeling: Fully internalizes prediction in representation space without relying on pixel generation. Representatives: FLARE, VLA-JEPA, JEPA-VLA, WoG, DIAL

World Model as Simulator

  • RL formalization: \((\hat{o}_{t+1}, \hat{r}_t, \hat{d}_t) \sim p_\phi(\cdot|o_{\leq t}, a_{\leq t}, l)\), with policy optimization using GRPO-style objectives (including importance ratios and clipping terms)
  • First-layer paradigm: RL in learned simulators (UniSim, World-Env, VLA-RFT, World-Gymnast, RehearseVLA)
  • Second-layer paradigm: co-evolution of simulator and policy (World-VLA-Loop, VLAW, WoVR), formula: \(\phi^{k+1} \leftarrow \text{UpdateWM}(\phi^k, D_{real} \cup D_{policy}(\pi_{\theta^k}))\), \(\theta^{k+1} \leftarrow \text{UpdatePolicy}(\theta^k, \hat{D}(\phi^{k+1}))\)

World Model as Evaluator

  • Rollout-based candidate evaluation: GPC, IRASim, World-in-World, DreamPlan
  • World model as policy evaluator: WorldEval, WorldArena
  • Explicit feedback heads: World-Env, VLA-RFT, RISE

Key Innovations

  1. Unified probabilistic perspective dissolves paradigm debates: Unifies policies, passive/controllable world models, and inverse dynamics models as different conditional queries of the same joint distribution. This formalization provides, for the first time, a comparable mathematical basis for debates such as "decoupled vs. unified" and "pixel vs. latent-space"—they are essentially implementation choices of the same objective under different constraints, not mutually exclusive research directions.

  2. Explicit proposal of "world model as evaluator": The authors elevate evaluation from a subsidiary function of RL to one of the core downstream uses of world models, noting that evaluators are only useful if imagined futures preserve the causal consequences of candidate actions—hallucinations and long-horizon errors not only degrade visual quality but directly corrupt the evaluation signal itself. This judgment shifts evaluation criteria from "visual realism" to "action fidelity + controllable interaction + closed-loop decision utility."

  3. Cautious stance on "video-pretrained backbones outperform VLM backbones": The authors explicitly state this remains an open empirical question, with current results being "evidence of promising inductive biases" rather than definitive conclusions. They also point out that the key difference is not whether full future videos are rendered online, but how much activity the visual branch maintains during control—an insight that directly informs designs like Fast-WAM that "use world models during training, eliminate them at inference."

Result Interpretation: MoE/MoT and latent-space methods perform prominently on LIBERO (LingBot-VA and VLA-JEPA both achieve 97+ average success rates), while among decoupled methods, UniPi scores 0.0 on the Long task, indicating that pure decoupled pipelines have severe limitations on long-horizon tasks. However, the authors also note insufficient cross-benchmark generalization—strong performance on one benchmark does not necessarily transfer to another.

Boundaries and Limitations

  • The authors explicitly do not enforce a single narrow formal definition, adopting a robot-learning-centric perspective, which means some theoretical unification is sacrificed
  • Whether pixel-level prediction is the optimal control abstraction remains an open question; the dominance of video instantiation reflects literature prominence rather than an optimality assumption
  • Which prediction substrate (video-pretrained backbones, VLMs, latent, structured, or symbolic) is most effective for control remains an open empirical question
  • Architectural evolution trends should be understood as dominant directions rather than strict chronological replacement relationships
  • The authors do not discuss specific quantification of computational costs, systematic fair comparisons across methods, or actual deployment latency data
  • Open-loop benchmark results should be interpreted cautiously—they are easy to standardize but may not reflect closed-loop control utility
  • Low-dimensional signals such as tactile feedback are easily diluted by high-dimensional visual features in joint latent optimization; multimodal integration remains an unsolved challenge
  • Integration of classical control methods like MPC faces dual bottlenecks of computational overhead and lack of formal guarantees

Engineering Implications

  • Verify evaluation protocols first: Results across LIBERO, RoboTwin, CALVIN, and SIMPLER are not directly comparable, with significant cross-benchmark generalization differences. When reproducing, explicitly report evaluation settings, otherwise numbers are meaningless
  • Use decoupled pipelines cautiously for long-horizon tasks: UniPi scores 0.0 on LIBERO Long, indicating severe cumulative error in pure decoupled IDM over long horizons. If choosing this route, introduce closed-loop chunked denoising (e.g., DreamZero) or explicit error control mechanisms
  • MoE/MoT and latent-space methods offer high cost-effectiveness: LingBot-VA and VLA-JEPA both achieve 97+ average success rates on LIBERO, and latent-space methods (e.g., LeWorldModel) can significantly reduce training and inference costs. If resources are limited, prioritize these two routes
  • "Use world models during training, eliminate at inference" is a practical strategy: Fast-WAM's findings suggest the main gains may come from video co-training during training rather than explicit future imagination at inference. Prioritize such designs for deployment
  • When evaluating world models, focus on action fidelity rather than visual realism: Visually plausible but dynamics-violating rollouts directly corrupt evaluation signals. Use action-conditioned consistency and executability diagnostics as core metrics
  • Pay attention to attribute matching when selecting datasets: Tables 3/4 show that general-purpose datasets like OXE and DROID are mostly ✗ on Human Prior and Contact/Physics dimensions; tactile-related tasks require specialized datasets like RH20T-P and Hoi!
  • The maintained GitHub repository (https://github.com/NTUMARS/Awesome-World-Model-for-Robotics-Policy) is a practical resource for tracking field progress; consider subscribing
