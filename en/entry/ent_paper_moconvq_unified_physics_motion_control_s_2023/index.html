Summary

In this work, we present MoConVQ, a novel unified framework for physics-based motion control leveraging scalable discrete representations. Building upon vector quantized variational autoencoders (VQ-VAE) and model-based reinforcement learning, our approach effectively learns motion embeddings from a large, unstructured dataset spanning tens of hours of motion examples.

Overview

MoConVQ is a unified physics-based character motion control framework based on Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoders (VQ-VAE) and model-based reinforcement learning, proposed by the research team. It aims to enable physics-based characters to acquire diverse skills from tens of hours of large-scale example motion data, and provides a unified interface for interactive control, physics-based pose estimation, text-driven motion generation, and large language model integration. Its core contribution lies in introducing Residual VQ-VAE (RVQ) to build a scalable discrete motion representation, coupled with a differentiable world model for efficient training, ultimately validating the robustness and generalization capability of this representation on tasks such as unseen motion tracking and text-driven motion generation.

What It Changes

For a long time, physics-based character animation has been constrained by two problems: first, dataset scales remained at the minute level, resulting in extremely limited skill libraries; second, latent motion representations lacked semantics, preventing users from intuitively selecting or combining desired actions from the latent space, often requiring task-specific reward function redesign and high-level policy training. What MoConVQ truly changes is replacing continuous latent variables with a discrete codebook for "motion representation," making this codebook the unified interface for all downstream tasks. The deeper significance of this shift is that discrete codes are naturally composable (index tuples can be directly consumed by language models or high-level policies), and the quantization process itself filters out high-frequency noise and physical inconsistencies in raw motion, preventing the physics simulator from being misled by data artifacts during tracking. Furthermore, the authors explicitly abandon the "puppet-like" mechanism that relies on residual root forces for balance maintenance in previous methods, allowing characters to remain stable under purely physical actuation, providing a more credible physical foundation for subsequent tasks such as pose estimation and text-driven generation.

Method Breakdown

Overall Architecture

MoConVQ consists of three components: an encoder ℰ (1D convolutional network), a decoder 𝒟 (1D deconvolutional network + control policy 𝝅 + simulation module), and a discrete codebook ℬ. Input motion segments 𝑴 (T=24 frames, 1.2 seconds) are encoded by ℰ into a sequence of latent vectors 𝑽, quantized into code sequences 𝒁 via nearest-neighbor search, and the decoder reconstructs motion 𝑴̃ in the simulation environment.

Residual VQ-VAE (RVQ)

  • Uses N=8 quantization layers, each with codebook size |ℬ|=512 and code dimension 768.
  • Quantization proceeds layer by layer: the first layer quantizes the original latent vector, and subsequent layers quantize the residual from previous layers r_d = 𝒗 − ∑_{j<d} 𝒛_j.
  • The final code is the sum of codes from all layers 𝒛^k = ∑_{d=0}^{N} 𝒛_d^k, equivalent to the index tuple I^k = (I_0^k, …, I_N^k).
  • Key design rationale: compared to directly enlarging the codebook, RVQ enables model capacity to grow exponentially with the number of layers while establishing a coarse-to-fine hierarchical representation—coarse codes capture the principal components of motion, and fine codes supplement details.

Differentiable World Model Training

  • Treats the simulator as a black box and trains a four-layer MLP (512 units) world model 𝒲 to approximate the simulator, making the decoding process fully differentiable.
  • Training procedure: each round alternates between updating the world model and the MoConVQ components. The world model generates simulated sequences by having the character track random reference motions, then replays them with 𝒲 to produce synthetic sequences, optimizing the loss ℒ_𝒲 = ‖𝑴̃_sim − 𝑴̃_𝒲‖.
  • MoConVQ components optimize the loss ℒ = ‖𝑴 − 𝑴̃_𝒲‖ + β₁‖ℰ(𝑴) − sg(𝒁)‖ + β₂‖sg(ℰ(𝑴)) − 𝒁‖ + β₃ℒ_reg, where the first three terms are standard VQ-VAE losses (reconstruction, commitment, VQ objective), and ℒ_reg is action regularization.

Action Regularization and Codebook Maintenance

  • Action regularization: ℒ_reg = Σ_t w₁‖𝒂̂^t − 𝒂̄_t‖ + w₂‖𝒂̂^t‖, where 𝒂̄_t is the exponential moving average (EMA) of actions, β=0.8. The first term encourages smooth action sequences, and the second term constrains action magnitude.
  • Codebook EMA update: ℬ ← (1−γ)ℬ + γℬ̂, γ=0.99; inactive codes are also reset to mitigate codebook collapse.
  • Quantizer dropout: during training, a random span of leading RVQ layers is selected (randomly chosen from [1, N]) to ensure motion can be represented by any prefix of layers, supporting runtime trade-offs between representation complexity and reconstruction quality.

Downstream Task Interfaces

  • General tracking control: Encoder-decoder configuration; quantized embeddings filter noise and can imitate unseen motions.
  • Interactive control: Decoder-only configuration; a high-level policy 𝝅_steering autoregressively predicts latent codes, trained via scheduled sampling (initial p=0.8 gradually decaying to 0).
  • MoConGPT: Index-based GPT with a dual-Transformer architecture (temporal Transformer N_T=12 layers, depth Transformer N_D=5 layers), trained with negative log-likelihood loss ℒ_GPT.
  • LLM integration: Index-only decoder configuration; uses in-context learning (ICL) to provide Claude-2 with 1600 text-index pair examples, requiring no fine-tuning.

Key Innovations

  1. Residual VQ-VAE as a physics-based motion representation: This is the first introduction of RVQ to physics-based character control. Compared to continuous latent variables, the discrete codebook is not only capacity-scalable (8 layers × 512 codes) but also inherently noise-robust—ablation experiments show that adding Gaussian noise causes a significant drop in tracking accuracy for continuous VAE, while MoConVQ's performance remains essentially unchanged. This property is crucial for handling artifacts in real motion capture data.
  2. Unified interface covering multiple tasks: The same discrete representation simultaneously supports interactive control, physics-based pose estimation, text-driven motion generation, and LLM integration. In particular, the LLM integration uses in-context learning to let Claude-2 directly output index sequences, generating composite actions such as "walk then sit" without any fine-tuning, and this action combination is not present in the training data—demonstrating the semantic composability of discrete representations as a "motion language."
  3. Fully physics-driven without residual forces: MoConVQ does not use any residual root forces in the physics-based pose estimation task. Compared to SimPoE's no-root-force version (MPJPE=115.2 mm), MoConVQ achieves 125.6 mm—slightly lower accuracy but significantly better motion smoothness (e_smooth=3.4 vs 23.5) and greater physical realism.

Experiments and Results

Unseen Motion Tracking (HDM05 test set, 2 hours)

  • MoConVQ achieves an MPBPE of 6.3 cm, demonstrating that the discrete representation generalizes effectively to unseen actions.

MoConVQ's accuracy is lower than non-physical baselines, but its motion smoothness (e_smooth=3.4) and acceleration (Accel=5.1) significantly outperform all baselines, proving that physical constraints effectively filter out jitter artifacts from the pose estimator.

MoConVQ's R-precision is lower than T2MGPT, but its FID is close, and it is the only physically accurate generation method. The authors note that retargeting strategy affects metrics and that the model has not fully converged (still room for improvement after 40k training steps).

Ablation Experiments (HDM05 + Gaussian noise ε∼N(0,0.1))

  • Continuous VAE achieves higher accuracy on clean data but degrades significantly under noise; MoConVQ's performance remains essentially unchanged under noise.
  • Residual layers significantly improve tracking accuracy while maintaining quantization robustness.

Boundaries and Limitations

Unspecified aspects of the paper include: quantitative evaluation metrics for MoConGPT and T2M-MoConGPT (only qualitative examples provided); systematic evaluation of LLM integration on more complex tasks. Acknowledged limitations include: the model struggles to learn sparse and isolated actions in the dataset (e.g., backflips); the convolutional decoder requires multiple future motion codes, potentially introducing real-time latency; the motion representation does not distinguish body parts, making it difficult to control individual limbs; the framework only supports single-person actions and does not extend to environmental interaction or multi-agent scenarios. Additionally, global position control is not implemented in the physics-based pose estimation task, resulting in higher unaligned MPJPE; the R-precision of the text generation task is significantly affected by the retargeting strategy. Artifacts in training data (e.g., foot sliding from treadmill capture) still degrade model performance, and while the EMA soft constraint mitigates this, it cannot fully eliminate it.

Engineering Insights

When reproducing MoConVQ, prioritize verifying the following aspects: Data preprocessing—training data must be downsampled to 20 FPS and retargeted to the simulated character (19 joints, 20 rigid bodies); retargeting quality directly affects text generation metrics; Alternating training of world model and MoConVQ—replay buffer size 50000, replacing 1024 frames per iteration, training takes approximately 6 days (RTX 3090); ensure the world model converges to sufficient accuracy first, otherwise decoder gradient signals become unreliable; Action regularization—the EMA smoothing factor β=0.8 is an empirical value; too small causes action jitter, too large over-smooths and loses details; Quantizer dropout—randomly select the number of RVQ layers during training; if this technique is omitted, the model may rely on all 8 layers and cannot flexibly adjust representation complexity at runtime. The most common pitfall is data artifact handling: high-frequency noise in motion capture data can be mistaken by VQ as motion details; it is recommended to apply mild smoothing to raw data before training, but avoid hard EMA constraints (which eliminate subtle motions). For LLM integration, note Claude-2's 100k context limit; example texts should be kept within an average of 167 characters, and the LLM may occasionally decompose actions too finely or directly output semantically similar complete examples, requiring additional prompting guidance.
