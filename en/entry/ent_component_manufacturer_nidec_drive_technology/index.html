Overview

Nidec Drive Technology is a precision transmission subsidiary of Nidec Corporation, producing FLEXWAVE strain wave gear reducers and motion control products for the humanoid robot market. Its core value lies in achieving high positioning accuracy, zero backlash, and low vibration through strain wave transmission technology, meeting the stringent requirements of humanoid robot joints for smooth motion and precise position control.

Why It Exists: Pain Points and Historical Positioning

In the development history of humanoid robot joint actuation, reducers have always been one of the key bottlenecks determining overall machine performance. Humanoid robot joints need to output high torque in limited space, achieve high positioning accuracy, maintain zero backlash, and exhibit low vibration characteristics—because any minute tooth clearance or elastic deformation is amplified into noticeable positioning errors and vibrations at the end effector.

Before FLEXWAVE emerged, the precision reducer market was long dominated by Harmonic Drive Systems (harmonic reducers) and Nabtesco (RV reducers). Harmonic reducers are known for single-stage high reduction ratios, zero backlash, and high torque density, but the flexspline has inherent limitations in fatigue life and impact resistance; RV reducers offer high rigidity and load capacity but are larger in size and weight with a limited reduction ratio range. Humanoid robots impose more demanding requirements on joint reducers than industrial robots—they need to simultaneously achieve lightweight design, high precision, high torque density, and long service life, creating an entry opportunity for new strain wave transmission solutions.

Nidec Drive Technology's strategic positioning is precisely to enter this market gap with the FLEXWAVE strain wave gear reducer. What it truly changes is not the fundamental principle of reducers—the physical basis of strain wave transmission is the same as harmonic drives—but rather the engineering implementation path of manufacturing processes and tooth profile design. Through differentiated flexspline structural design and precision machining capabilities, FLEXWAVE aims to improve flexspline fatigue life and dynamic performance while maintaining the inherent advantages of strain wave transmission, thereby establishing competitive barriers in the emerging humanoid robot application scenario.

Principle Breakdown

① Basic Principle of Strain Wave Transmission

The working principle of the FLEXWAVE strain wave gear reducer is identical to that of harmonic drives, consisting of three core components:

  • Wave generator: An elliptical cam + thin-wall bearing that serves as the input, converting rotational motion into periodic elastic deformation of the flexspline;
  • Flexspline: A thin-walled cup-shaped external gear ring that undergoes periodic elastic deformation following the wave generator, typically serving as the output;
  • Circular spline: A rigid internal gear ring with more teeth than the flexspline (typically 2 more teeth), usually fixed.

The transmission ratio is determined by the tooth count difference. Let the flexspline tooth count be \(Z_f\) and the circular spline tooth count be \(Z_c\), then the single-stage reduction ratio is:

\[ i = \frac{Z_f}{Z_c - Z_f} \]

Since the tooth count difference between the circular spline and flexspline is typically 2, a large reduction ratio can be achieved in a single stage. For each revolution of the wave generator, the meshing position between the flexspline and circular spline advances by 2 tooth pitches around the circumference, achieving reduction output.

② FLEXWAVE's Differentiated Technical Approach

The core difference between FLEXWAVE and Harmonic Drive lies in tooth profile design and flexspline structure. Traditional harmonic reducers use involute tooth profiles, while FLEXWAVE has made targeted designs in tooth profile optimization and flexspline wall thickness distribution to improve stress distribution and enhance fatigue life. Additionally, FLEXWAVE has independent technical accumulation in material selection and heat treatment processes.

③ Matching Logic with Humanoid Robot Joints

Humanoid robot joints require the combined characteristics of "high precision + zero backlash + low vibration." Strain wave transmission naturally possesses zero backlash—because the elastic deformation of the flexspline ensures constant tooth surface contact, eliminating the side clearance found in traditional gear pairs. Low vibration stems from the continuity of the meshing process—multiple teeth mesh simultaneously with uniform load distribution. These characteristics precisely correspond to the demands of humanoid robots for smooth motion and precise positioning.

Limitations and Boundaries

① Inherent Challenges of Flexspline Fatigue Life

Strain wave transmission relies on the periodic elastic deformation of the flexspline to transmit torque. The flexspline material is subjected to alternating stress over long periods, making fatigue life an inherent bottleneck. FLEXWAVE attempts to mitigate this shortcoming through tooth profile optimization and material process improvements, but the physical principle dictates that load capacity and service life remain constrained by the flexspline's fatigue characteristics.

② Limited Impact Resistance

Compared with RV reducers, strain wave transmission has relatively lower rigidity and limited impact load resistance. Humanoid robots may encounter impact loads in scenarios such as dynamic walking and fall protection, imposing additional requirements on the structural strength of reducers.

③ Cost and Production Capacity

The manufacturing of precision reducers involves high-precision machining and strict quality control, resulting in high costs. As part of the Nidec Group, Nidec Drive Technology possesses scaled manufacturing capabilities, but for the humanoid robot market—an emerging sector still in early industrialization—production capacity planning and cost control require continued investment.

Common Misconceptions

  1. "FLEXWAVE is a reducer based on an entirely new principle"—Incorrect. FLEXWAVE and harmonic drives share the same physical principle, both belonging to strain wave gearing. The differentiation lies in tooth profile design, flexspline structure, and manufacturing processes, not in a fundamental principle breakthrough.

  2. "Nidec Drive Technology is an independent company"—Inaccurate. It is a business unit under Nidec Corporation, not an independently listed entity. Its market strategy, production capacity planning, and financial decisions are constrained by the group's overall strategy.

  3. "Strain wave reducers are only used in humanoid robots"—Incorrect. Strain wave reducers are widely applied in industrial robots, collaborative robots, precision rotary tables, aerospace, and other fields. Humanoid robots are just one of their emerging application scenarios.

  4. "Zero backlash equals zero elastic deformation"—Incorrect. Zero backlash means there is no side clearance between tooth surfaces, but the elastic deformation of the flexspline itself still exists. Under load, torsional deformation of the flexspline causes some transmission error, which is an inherent characteristic of strain wave transmission.

  5. "Nidec only makes reducers"—Incorrect. Nidec Drive Technology offers multiple precision transmission product lines for the North American market, including planetary reducers, right-angle worm gear reducers, cycloidal reducers, AGV drive systems, and more. Strain wave reducers are only part of its product portfolio.
