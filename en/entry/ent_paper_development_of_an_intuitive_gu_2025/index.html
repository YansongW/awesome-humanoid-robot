Overview

This research focuses on the field of humanoid robot teleoperation, noting that many existing systems lack the development of graphical user interfaces (GUIs) centered on non-expert users. To address this, the authors propose developing a scalable GUI designed to be simple and intuitive, enabling non-professional operators to easily control a robot through the obstacle course specified by FIRA. The study draws on common practices in user interface (UI) development and integrates human-robot interaction (HRI) and related concepts, aiming to build a novel teleoperation system for non-experts.

Content

Research Background and Motivation

Humanoid robot operation is a significant research area with numerous practical applications and competitive value. However, many existing systems do not invest sufficient effort in developing graphical user interfaces (GUIs) centered on non-expert users. The core motivation of this study is to fill this gap by providing more accessible control tools for non-professional operators.

Core Contributions and Methods

  • Objective: To develop a scalable GUI whose design emphasizes simplicity and intuitiveness, enabling non-expert operators to control a robot through the obstacle course specified by FIRA (Federation of International Robot-soccer Association).
  • Design Foundation: The study draws on common practices in user interface (UI) development and a deep understanding of human-robot interaction (HRI) and related concepts to guide the design of the new interface.
  • Key Features: The GUI aims to lower the operational barrier, requiring no professional training to use, thereby expanding the potential application of humanoid robots in broader scenarios.

Experiments and Conclusions

The study validates the effectiveness of the GUI using the FIRA obstacle course as a test scenario. Experimental results show that non-expert operators can successfully complete teleoperation tasks with the interface, demonstrating its intuitiveness and ease of use. This work provides a feasible solution for the popularization of humanoid robot teleoperation systems.
