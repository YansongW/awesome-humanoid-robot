Summary

The advancement of robot learning is currently hindered by the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality datasets. While established data collection methods such as teleoperation and universal manipulation interfaces dominate current datasets, they suffer from inherent limitations in scalability and real-world deployability. Institutions per source list: Joy Future Academy、JD.

Overview

EgoLive is a large-scale egocentric dataset constructed by JD.com and other institutions, containing 1,680 hours, 65,866 episodes, and 346 real-world tasks of high-fidelity stereo video, accompanied by depth, hand motion tracking, and fine-grained language instruction annotations. Its core contribution lies in achieving, for the first time, large-scale data collection in real service scenarios with multi-view, motion tracking, language annotation, and depth information through a custom head-mounted device, JoyEgoCam, and a fully automated annotation pipeline, providing an in-the-wild training foundation for general-purpose robot manipulation models.

What It Changes

The robot learning community has long been plagued by a dilemma: teleoperation data is high-quality but expensive to collect and poorly scalable, while internet videos are abundant but lack the action, depth, and interaction structure required for robots. What EgoLive truly changes is the "cost-quality" trade-off curve of data collection—it replaces robotic arm teleoperation and specialized interfaces like UMI with first-person head-mounted cameras, returning data collection to the most primitive and scalable form: "humans naturally performing tasks." This is not merely a replacement of collection methods; it means the data distribution shifts from constrained laboratory scenarios to real service environments such as homes, retail stores, and pharmacies, elevating environmental diversity, object categories, and task complexity by an order of magnitude.

Another key change lies in the "degree of automation" in annotation. Previous datasets like EgoDex had motion tracking but lacked language annotations, and Ego4D had language but no depth or hand tracking. EgoLive is the first to simultaneously provide stereo RGB, depth, 6D hand trajectories, and fine-grained language instructions at the thousand-hour scale, all generated through an automated pipeline. This allows downstream researchers to skip extensive manual data cleaning and annotation efforts and focus directly on policy learning. From an engineering perspective, this is equivalent to upgrading the "data factory" from a manual workshop to an automated production line.

Method Breakdown

Hardware Layer: JoyEgoCam Head-Mounted Device

  • Stereo RGB cameras, resolution 2160×2160 @ 60 FPS, FOV 130°×130°, simulating human binocular disparity
  • Integrated IMU, sampled at 200 Hz, for camera ego-motion estimation and visual-inertial fusion
  • Design principle: minimally invasive, does not occlude the face or interfere with hand movements, ensuring natural operator behavior

Automated Annotation Pipeline (Three Stages)

  1. Motion Tracking:
  2. Hands: a two-stage method—first estimating MANO parameters from monocular video using HaMeR, then performing stereo optimization to obtain consistent 3D hand keypoints satisfying binocular 2D projection constraints
  3. Camera pose: ORB-SLAM3 fusing stereo RGB + IMU, initialized from the first frame of the left camera
  4. Semantic Understanding:
  5. Hand-object detection using the [Shan20] model, tracking with BoT-SORT, and segmentation masks with SAM2
  6. Each episode is divided into subtasks (1-20 seconds) based on hand-object detection and tracking results
  7. Instruction generation: fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-32B, employing a multi-stage inference strategy that explicitly models three elements—hand, manipulated object, and action—rather than free-form descriptions
  8. 3D Reconstruction:
  9. Depth: FoundationStereo reconstructs from calibrated stereo video at 1152×1152 resolution
  10. Depth evaluation: ChArUco calibration board + staircase array, distances 0.5-3.5 m, BFGS joint optimization of global reprojection error

Quality Assessment

  • LLM-as-a-Judge protocol, scoring across four dimensions: hand consistency, object consistency, action consistency, and global consistency

Key Innovations

  1. "Real tasks" rather than "demonstration tasks": EgoLive's data comes from tasks naturally performed by operators in real service scenarios (homes, retail, pharmacies), not standard actions designed in laboratories. This means the data captures the long-tail distribution of the real world—cluttered tables, lighting variations, occlusions, and non-standard object manipulation methods—diversity that teleoperation data struggles to achieve.

  2. The five-modality combination of stereo + IMU + depth + hand tracking + language annotation: No previous dataset has simultaneously possessed all five modalities at the thousand-hour scale. Ego4D has language but no depth, EgoDex has tracking but no language, and Xperience-10M has all but at only 512×512 @ 20 FPS. EgoLive fills this gap with flagship specifications of 2160×2160 @ 60 FPS, and depth accuracy with average error below 5.4 mm within 0.9 m, meeting fine manipulation requirements.

  3. Engineering of the fully automated annotation pipeline: The concatenation of hand tracking (HaMeR + stereo optimization), semantic segmentation (SAM2), and language generation (fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-32B) is not a simple assembly but is specifically designed for the first-person perspective—for example, 3D hand optimization solves the wrist drift problem of monocular estimation, and the subtask segmentation mechanism aligns language descriptions with action segments. This enables continuous low-cost dataset growth rather than a one-off project.

Boundaries and Limitations

The paper does not explicitly list a limitations statement, but the following boundaries can be inferred from factual points: - No robot policy training or sim-to-real transfer experiments: The dataset's value remains at the "data supply" level, and it has not yet been proven to directly improve robot manipulation success rates. - Depth accuracy degrades significantly with distance: Mean error exceeds 8.7 mm beyond 1.5 m and 14 mm beyond 2.5 m, which may be insufficient for long-distance manipulation or large-scale scene perception. - Language annotation relies on LLM generation: Although evaluated via LLM-as-a-Judge, specific scores are not disclosed, and the quality ceiling of free-form descriptions is limited by Qwen3-VL-32B's capabilities. - Limited scenario coverage: Although 346 tasks is substantial, they are concentrated in service-oriented scenarios (homes, retail, pharmacies), not covering industrial manufacturing, outdoor operations, and other robot application domains. - Data collectors are recruited operators: Their behavior patterns may deviate from real users, affecting the ecological validity of the data distribution.

Engineering Insights

  1. First verify whether depth accuracy meets your task requirements: For close-range desktop manipulation (<0.9 m), EgoLive's depth error (<5.4 mm) is fully sufficient; but for mobile manipulation or long-range perception, consider the risk of policy degradation due to depth error.

  2. Hand tracking data can be directly used for imitation learning: EgoLive's 3D hand keypoints undergo stereo optimization, have no wrist drift, and are consistent with 2D projections. Downstream teams can directly use them as action labels without additional cleaning. However, note that MANO parameters represent a hand model, not a robot end-effector, so a retargeting strategy must be designed for human-to-robot mapping.

  3. The granularity design of language instructions is worth borrowing: Explicitly modeling the three elements of "hand-object-action" rather than free-form descriptions aligns instructions more tightly with subtasks (1-20 seconds). When reproducing, it is recommended to preserve this structure rather than directly using LLM-generated free text.

  4. The most likely pitfall is the resolution of depth reconstruction: FoundationStereo runs at 1152×1152, while the original video is 2160×2160, meaning the depth map is downsampled. If pixel-level aligned RGB-D is needed downstream, you must handle upsampling or recomputation yourself.

  5. Data access channels are open: Available via https://robotdata-market.jdcloud.com/console/market. However, training configurations, inference frequency, and other engineering details are not mentioned in the paper, so reproducing the annotation pipeline will require self-exploration.
