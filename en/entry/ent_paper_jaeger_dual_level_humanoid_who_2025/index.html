Summary

JAEGER: Dual-Level Humanoid Whole-Body Controller is a 2025 work on loco-manipulation and whole-body-control for humanoid robots.

Sources

  • JAEGER: Dual-Level Humanoid Whole-Body Controller (arXiv)

Overview

JAEGER is a whole-body controller for adult-sized humanoid robots (Unitree H1/H1-2), proposed by the author team. The core contribution lies in decoupling whole-body control into two independent policies for the upper and lower body (Dec-POMDP framework), accompanied by a real-time retargeting network and supervised-initialization curriculum learning. The method significantly outperforms open-source SOTA methods such as HumanPlus, ExBody, and OmniH2O in both root velocity tracking and pose tracking tasks, with joint angle tracking errors reduced to 30-60% of other methods.

What It Changes

The real change in this work is breaking the default assumption of "a single policy controlling the whole body." Previous approaches such as OmniH2O and HumanPlus attempted to use one policy to handle both balance and pose imitation simultaneously. However, morphological differences between humanoid robots and humans mean that "fully imitating human poses" actually undermines balance, and interactions between the upper and lower body (e.g., large upper-body movements increasing lower-body stability difficulty) often cause training to fall into local optima, requiring tedious hyperparameter tuning and curriculum design. JAEGER redefines the problem as collaboration between two independent agents, letting the lower body focus on balance and velocity tracking while the upper body focuses on pose following, making the effective combination of "coarse-grained root control" and "fine-grained joint tracking" feasible for the first time. Additionally, its retargeting network replaces traditional optimization methods with a three-layer MLP, reducing per-frame processing time from seconds to sub-milliseconds (0.00064 s/frame) with smoother outputs, clearing the computational bottleneck for real-time teleoperation and online retargeting.

Method Breakdown

Overall Framework

JAEGER consists of three components: a retargeting network, a dual-level controller, and a curriculum learning pipeline. It supports two command modes: root-based mode (root velocity commands + upper-body reference joint angles) and pose-based mode (full-body reference joint angles).

Retargeting Network

  • Two-stage optimization (similar to H2O) processes the AMASS dataset to generate human-robot pose pairs, followed by a lightweight three-layer MLP to learn the mapping.
  • Key design: The MLP acts as a continuous function approximator, robust to input noise and eliminating inter-frame jitter; manually removes non-continuous frames caused by unstable IK solutions.
  • Performance: 1 kHz execution frequency, 0.00064 s/frame on RTX 4060, error 0.13567 m/frame (better than H2O's 9.05 s/frame, 0.14476 m/frame).

Dual-Level Controller (Dec-POMDP)

  • Two agents: π^lower (lower body) and π^upper (upper body), each receiving n-step observation history x_{t,n}, with outputs concatenated as PD controller targets.
  • Lower body: Gated Transformer-XL (2 transformer blocks, 6 attention heads, hidden_size=128, memory_length=15). Since WBC is a long-horizon task, Transformers outperform MLP/LSTM in complex terrain balancing; gating mechanism (GRU) replaces residual connections to improve RL stability.
  • Upper body: 3-layer MLP with 3-step observations, as only pose following is needed without complex balancing.
  • Observations include: scaled base angular velocity, projected gravity, command information, joint position/velocity errors, previous actions, reference pose angles; the critic additionally receives linear velocity.

Curriculum Learning

  1. Base policy: Fix the upper body and independently train the lower body to obtain π^base.
  2. Supervised initialization: The upper body directly imitates reference poses via MSE (L_SL); the lower body adds KL regularization (L_KL) to prevent deviation from π^base; pose amplitude is halved in this stage.
  3. RL training: PPO optimizes cumulative reward, restoring the original pose amplitude.

Reward Function (Key Terms)

  • Upper-body DoF position: exp(−4.0*|q_ref−q|), weight 20
  • Lower-body DoF position: exp(−4.0*|q_ref−q|), weight 100
  • Foot height: exp(−10.0*|h_target−h|), weight 100
  • Linear/angular velocity: exp(−4.0*|v_cmd−v|), weight 20
  • Additional stability and sim-to-real supplementary rewards (torque limits, action rate, foot contact time, etc.).

Key Innovations

  1. Decoupled upper/lower body Dec-POMDP framework: This is the first explicit modeling of humanoid whole-body control as a dual-agent collaboration problem. Its value lies in splitting the 21-DoF high-dimensional action space into two lower-dimensional subproblems, avoiding convergence difficulties from the curse of dimensionality in a single policy, while allowing heterogeneous architectures (Transformer + MLP) for the upper and lower body to allocate computational resources by task complexity.
  2. Supervised initialization + KL-regularized curriculum learning: Directly addresses the RL cold-start problem. First, independently train the lower-body base policy, then "teach" the upper body to imitate poses via supervised learning, while using KL constraints to prevent the lower body from forgetting balance skills. The progressive strategy of halving pose amplitude significantly reduces early exploration difficulty, which is key to convergence speed and final performance.
  3. MLP retargeting network: Compared to H2O's optimization method (9.05 s/frame), JAEGER's MLP achieves real-time processing at 0.00064 s/frame, with angular acceleration RMS reduced by approximately 50% (e.g., left shoulder pitch from 0.0143 to 0.0069 rad/s²) and lower error. This enables online retargeting and low-latency teleoperation.

Key conclusions: JAEGER leads comprehensively in joint angle tracking (errors 30-60% of other methods); angular velocity tracking error is only 5°/s in root-based mode; ablations confirm that supervised initialization (especially for the H1-2 upper body) and the decoupled architecture (JAEGER Single shows significant upper-body error degradation) are both indispensable.

Boundaries and Limitations

The paper does not explicitly list a limitations section, but inferences can be drawn from the results: 1) H1-2 has generally higher velocity tracking errors than H1 due to a heavier upper body (arms and computing modules), indicating the framework is sensitive to mass distribution; 2) It does not support broader command types such as keypoint positions (OmniH2O has an advantage in root tracking because it directly optimizes global keypoints); 3) Complex poses (single-foot long-duration contact, large-amplitude movements) cannot be deployed to real robots even if feasible in simulation; 4) The authors acknowledge the need for extensive reward engineering (Table 12 includes 10+ penalty/regularization terms) to prevent undesirable behaviors and address sim-to-real challenges.

Engineering Insights

When reproducing or drawing on JAEGER, prioritize checking the following key points: 1) Supervised initialization is the performance cornerstone—ablations show that removing Init degrades H1-2 upper-body error from 0.1192 to 0.4088 rad; be sure to retain the progressive "halved pose amplitude" strategy; 2) Upper and lower body architectures are not interchangeable—a lightweight MLP suffices for the upper body, but the lower body must use a gated Transformer-XL (memory_length=15); the single-network scheme (JAEGER Single) significantly degrades joint tracking; 3) The retargeting network is a prerequisite dependency—all baselines use JAEGER's retargeting preprocessing; replacing it with the H2O optimization method would reduce real-time performance by 14,000×; 4) Reward engineering is not optional—the foot contact time (weight 10000) and orientation penalty (weight 200) in Table 12 are critical for balance; directly removing them may cause training divergence; 5) Domain randomization parameters (friction U(0.2,1.1), control delay U(20,60)ms, external push 1 m/s) are key to sim-to-real transfer; when deploying on real H1-2, maintain the LCM communication (50 Hz policy / 500 Hz low-level) configuration.
