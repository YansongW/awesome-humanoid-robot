Overview

Rhythm is the first unified framework to achieve real-world physical interaction deployment of dual humanoid robots, consisting of three components: IAMR (Interaction-Aware Motion Retargeting), IGRL (Interaction-Guided Reinforcement Learning), and a real-world deployment module. The core contribution lies in resolving kinematic conflicts through an optimization strategy that decouples self-motion from interaction geometry, and achieving robust transfer from simulation to Unitree G1 hardware via graph-based rewards and curriculum training.

What It Changes

Research on dual humanoid robot interaction has long remained in the simulation stage, with the core obstacle being not a single algorithmic flaw but two overlooked coupled problems throughout the entire pipeline: the fundamental contradiction between self-motion fidelity and interaction geometry accuracy during retargeting, and the lack of coupled dynamics modeling caused by treating agents as isolated entities during training. Existing methods such as PAIR and Harmanoid adopt coupled formulations, often sacrificing natural poses to strictly satisfy interaction constraints when kinematic conflicts arise, which produces unusable distorted motions in real deployment.

What Rhythm truly changes is elevating "interaction" from a posterior constraint to prior knowledge. It no longer treats the two agents as two independent entities or a unified mesh, but explicitly distinguishes intra-agent edges (self-motion) from inter-agent edges (interaction geometry) via topological partitioning, and designs decoupled optimization objectives and reward functions accordingly. This perspective shift enables retargeting results to precisely maintain interaction geometry while preserving individual motion styles, and allows reinforcement learning policies to explicitly learn coupled dynamics rather than implicitly guessing, ultimately advancing dual humanoid interaction from simulation to real hardware.

Method Breakdown

IAMR: Interaction-Aware Motion Retargeting

  • Dual-manifold reference construction: Maps raw 3D keypoint sequences onto an individual manifold M_ind (scaled by individual body proportions) and a unified manifold M_uni (global average scaling), where the former ensures kinematic feasibility and the latter maintains interaction geometry consistency.
  • Topological partitioning and decoupled optimization: Partitions interaction mesh edges into intra-agent edges E_self and inter-agent edges E_inter, minimizing the hybrid energy q* = argmin_q (E_self(q) + E_inter(q)) with constraints q ∈ C_phy (joint limits, collision avoidance, zero foot velocity).
  • Variable-stiffness interaction constraints: E_inter employs distance-dependent spring potential energy with stiffness ω_ij = ω_max · e^(-γ d_ij), hardening at close range to prevent penetration and softening at distance to allow free motion, avoiding pose distortion from hard constraints.
  • Optimization implementation: Frame-by-frame SQP with weights w_self=2.0, w_inter=10.0, w_reg=0.1, λ_rot=0.1, solved via CVXPY/OSQP.

IGRL: Interaction-Guided Reinforcement Learning

  • Multi-agent MDP with CTDE: Uses the MAPPO algorithm, with observation space comprising proprioception o_prop, peer perception o_peer (including relative pose P_rel = R_ego^T(P_peer - P_ego)), and reference motion o_ref.
  • Hierarchical policy network: Dual 1D-CNN temporal encoders (Conv1d(60→40, k=6, s=2) + Conv1d(40→20, k=4, s=2)) process historical and future windows, with output embeddings concatenated with current observations and decoded into actions via MLP [512, 256, 128].
  • Graph-based rewards: Interaction graph reward r_inter = exp(-(1/σ_inter) Σ ω_ij ||d_ij^sim - d_ij^ref||²) inherits IAMR's distance-aware dynamic weights; contact graph reward includes active/inactive contact consistency and force regularization.
  • Curriculum adaptive sampling: Smooth sampling based on multi-objective error vector [e_fail, e_track, e_inter], with curriculum weights transitioning from stability phase [0.8, 0.1, 0.1] to precision phase [0.05, 0.30, 0.65] (thresholded by L̄_max 350/500).
  • Dual-agent domain randomization: Applies 20-60ms random delays to peer observations to simulate wireless communication, and ±5cm/±0.2rad perturbations to initial states to force learning of recovery behaviors.

Real-World Deployment

  • Hierarchical localization: POINT-LIO provides 10Hz high-frequency odometry, GICP global registration corrects drift, and Kalman filtering fuses outputs to produce drift-free global poses.
  • Asynchronous synchronization mechanism: Global poses broadcast via LCM, with motion phase φ as a continuous synchronization variable, φ_dot_ego = 1.0 + k(φ_peer - φ_ego) compensating for clock drift.
  • Control architecture: High-level policy outputs desired joint positions at 50Hz, low-level PD controller executes at 500Hz, with ONNX Runtime inference latency <3ms.

Key Innovations

  1. Decoupled retargeting optimization: First to explicitly separate interaction geometry from self-motion fidelity as tunable-weight optimization objectives, achieving "hard at close range, soft at distance" interaction constraints via variable-stiffness spring potential energy, avoiding the defect of sacrificing natural poses under kinematic conflicts in existing methods. This design yields a 43% improvement in F1-Strict over the strong baseline DOR on the Inter-X dataset (with significant anthropometric mismatches).

  2. Topology-prior-driven reward design: Directly converts interaction graphs and contact graphs extracted by IAMR into reinforcement learning rewards, enabling policies to explicitly learn coupled dynamics rather than implicitly guessing. The distance-aware dynamic weights of the interaction graph reward ensure differentiated learning of precise close-range contact and free distant motion, while the force regularization of the contact graph reward constrains physical plausibility.

  3. Robust training strategy for real deployment: Curriculum adaptive sampling gradually transitions from "survival first" to "interaction precision first," combined with dual-agent domain randomization (communication delays, initial perturbations), enabling policies to actively recover interaction topology under asynchronous observations and physical misalignment on real hardware. In real experiments, Rhythm improves over the Single Agent baseline by more than 60%.

Boundaries and Limitations

The paper does not explicitly discuss limitations, but boundaries can be inferred from method details. Current state estimation relies on pre-built maps, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation and plan to eliminate in future work. The paper does not address extensions beyond two agents, non-humanoid interaction forms, nor does it report training time, computational resource consumption, or detailed failure case analysis. Real experiments are validated only on the Unitree G1 platform, without testing other hardware forms. The curriculum sampling thresholds (350/500) may depend on specific task distributions and require re-tuning when transferring to other interaction categories.

Engineering Insights

When reproducing Rhythm, prioritize verifying IAMR's weight settings (w_self=2.0, w_inter=10.0)—these directly determine the balance between self-motion fidelity and interaction precision in retargeting results, and the 43% F1 improvement on Inter-X stems precisely from this. The most error-prone aspect in reinforcement learning training is the threshold switching in curriculum sampling; the L̄_max 350/500 thresholds are strongly correlated with episode length definitions and must be consistent with the paper. Communication delays (20-60ms) and initial perturbations (±5cm/±0.2rad) in dual-agent domain randomization are critical to Sim-to-Real success and cannot be omitted. During real deployment, the phase synchronization gain k in LCM communication needs adjustment based on actual clock drift rates; it is recommended to conduct offline synchronization tests first. The Kalman filter fusion frequency for state estimation (POINT-LIO 10Hz + low-frequency GICP) must match the 50Hz policy control loop; otherwise, pose jumps may occur.
