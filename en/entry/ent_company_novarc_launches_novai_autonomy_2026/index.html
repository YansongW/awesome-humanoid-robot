Overview

Novarc Technologies is a company focused on industrial physical AI, dedicated to revolutionizing welding automation in the global manufacturing industry. At the Automate 2026 exhibition (June 22-25, 2026, Chicago), the company released NovAI™ Autonomy—an advanced solution that brings machine vision and real-time adaptive capabilities to robotic welding—and expanded its enterprise-grade welding intelligence platform, NovHub™, to ABB and Yaskawa robots. Its core contribution lies in addressing industry pain points such as skilled welder shortages and high part variability through physical AI technology, improving welding quality and production efficiency.

Content

What It Is: Precise Definitions of NovAI Autonomy and NovHub

NovAI™ Autonomy is an advanced welding automation solution released by Novarc Technologies at the Automate 2026 exhibition. It uses machine vision and real-time adaptive technology to enable robots to automatically assess root openings and gaps, optimizing starting parameters before arc initiation to ensure weld quality. The solution can be retrofitted onto manufacturers' existing robotic cells, adding real-time vision, adaptive control, and welding intelligence, thereby reducing manual rework and improving return on investment.

NovHub™ is Novarc's enterprise-grade welding intelligence platform that transforms welding data into real-time insights, centrally managing welding videos, process parameters, part traceability, and production sequencing. It helps manufacturers review weld histories, investigate quality issues, monitor production trends, and optimize production decisions.

Together, they form the NovAI Suite—Novarc's core technology portfolio evolving from pipe welding automation toward broader geometries, robots, and industrial manufacturing applications.

Why It Exists: Pain Points and Historical Positioning

The core contradiction in welding automation is: traditional automation excels at repetitive tasks but cannot handle real-world part variability. Novarc CEO Soroush Karimzadeh noted that reindustrialization and reshoring manufacturing require a step-change in manufacturing capability, and industries such as shipbuilding, data centers, energy infrastructure, and heavy equipment face common challenges:

  • Skilled welder shortage: Global manufacturing faces an "ominous shortage of skilled welders," with a gap in the transmission of traditional welding expertise;
  • Shortage of robot programmers: Even with automation equipment, engineers capable of writing and debugging welding programs are equally scarce;
  • High part variability: Welded workpieces exhibit significant differences in gaps, misalignment, and tack welds, which traditional automation cannot adapt to, leading to overwelding, costly grinding, and scrap.

Novarc's historical positioning lies in evolving from a leader in pipe welding automation to an AI-driven welding automation solution provider covering broader geometries, robots, and industrial manufacturing applications. The launch of NovAI marks a milestone in this evolution.

Principle Breakdown

① Machine Vision: Enabling Robots to "See" the World Before Welding

The core of NovAI Autonomy lies in introducing machine vision into the welding process. Traditional welding robots execute tasks along preset trajectories, "blind" to variations in workpiece gaps and misalignment. NovAI uses cameras and vision algorithms to autonomously assess root openings and gaps before arc initiation, building a digital model of the workpiece to provide input for subsequent adaptive control.

② Real-Time Adaptive Control: Letting Welding Parameters Respond to Conditions

Based on the workpiece state obtained from the vision system, NovAI Autonomy adjusts welding parameters in real time to handle variations such as gaps, misalignment, and tack welds. The key aspects of this adaptive capability are:

  • Pre-arc optimization: Setting starting parameters based on visual assessment before welding begins, rather than relying on manual presets;
  • Real-time adjustment during welding: Continuously monitoring and adjusting during the welding process to ensure consistent weld quality.

③ Engineering Essence of Physical AI

NovAI falls under the category of Physical AI—embedding AI capabilities into physical systems so they can perceive, decide, and act on the real world. Unlike pure software AI, physical AI must handle sensor noise, actuator latency, and physical constraints. Novarc's engineering implementation path is to integrate visual perception, adaptive control, and welding intelligence into a retrofittable module, rather than requiring manufacturers to replace entire robotic cells.

Who Uses It: Application Cases

ABB Robot Integration: NovAI Autonomy will be demonstrated on ABB's IRC5 robots, delivering AI-driven real-time vision and adaptive control. ABB Global Product Manager Joshua Williamson stated that this technology can reduce overwelding, costly grinding, and scrap—precisely the main pain points of traditional welding automation when part variability is high.

Yaskawa Robot Integration: NovAI Autonomy will be demonstrated on Yaskawa YRC1000 robots, showcasing how physical AI technology autonomously assesses root openings and gaps before arc initiation. Novarc and Yaskawa have signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) to accelerate the deployment of vision-based welding cells in both new and retrofit applications.

Miller Copilot Builder with Blue iQ: Developed in collaboration with Miller and powered by NovAI, this solution brings adaptive intelligence to the most challenging welding scenarios, automatically adapting to part-to-part variations, assembly challenges, and tack weld consumption.

Target Industries: Shipbuilding, data centers, energy infrastructure, and heavy equipment—industries facing the common challenges of skilled welder shortages, robot programmer shortages, and high part variability.

Limitations and Boundaries

  1. Physical limitations of vision systems: Machine vision may be interfered with by strong arc light, smoke, spatter, and other welding environments, requiring engineering safeguards and algorithm optimization (engineering judgment).
  2. Performance boundaries of retrofit solutions: NovAI retrofits onto existing robotic cells, and its performance is constrained by the original robot's motion accuracy, payload capacity, and controller performance. For older equipment, the improvement potential of adaptive control may be limited (engineering judgment).
  3. Reduction of programming dependence does not mean elimination: Although NovAI reduces dependence on robot programmers, initial configuration, vision calibration, and exception handling still require professional personnel.
  4. Expansion of covered geometries is ongoing: Novarc started with pipe welding, and its expansion toward broader geometries is a gradual process; adaptability to complex workpieces requires continued validation (engineering judgment).

Common Misconceptions

  1. "NovAI means unmanned welding"—Wrong. It addresses part variability and quality consistency, reducing dependence on skilled welders, but welding cells still require human supervision, maintenance, and exception handling. What it truly changes is not "whether people are present" but "what people do"—shifting from manual operation to supervision and decision-making.
  2. "Retrofitting NovAI means replacing the robot"—Wrong. Its core selling point is precisely the retrofit, leveraging manufacturers' existing robot assets and adding vision and adaptive capabilities, rather than starting from scratch.
  3. "Machine vision is not feasible in welding"—This is a stereotype of traditional vision technology. NovAI's engineering innovation lies in combining vision with adaptive control and optimizing it for welding scenarios, rather than simply applying generic vision solutions.
  4. "Physical AI is just a marketing concept"—NovAI demonstrates the concrete implementation of physical AI in industrial settings: a closed loop of perception (vision) → decision (parameter optimization) → execution (real-time adjustment), rather than remaining at the demonstration stage.
