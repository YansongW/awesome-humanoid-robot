Summary

BiGym: A Demo-Driven Mobile Bi-Manual Manipulation Benchmark is a 2024 work on simulation benchmark for humanoid robots, with open-source code available.

Sources

  • BiGym: A Demo-Driven Mobile Bi-Manual Manipulation Benchmark (arXiv)
  • BiGym: A Demo-Driven Mobile Bi-Manual Manipulation Benchmark project page

Overview

BiGym is a MuJoCo-based mobile bimanual manipulation benchmark comprising 40 tasks, 50 human VR demonstrations, and sparse rewards, proposed by Chernyadev et al. in 2024. It aims to address the lack of mobile manipulation, dual-arm robots, and natural human demonstrations in existing benchmarks, providing a more challenging evaluation platform for imitation learning (IL) and demonstration-driven reinforcement learning (RL).

What It Changes

Existing robot benchmarks (e.g., MetaWorld, RLBench) are either limited to single-arm fixed-base setups or rely on dense shaped rewards and narrow-distribution demonstrations generated by motion planners, which severely mismatch the noise and multimodality of real-world human manipulation. More critically, progress on benchmarks like RLBench has stagnated, especially for 3D next-best pose agents relying on heuristic keyframe extraction, whose methods cannot transfer to mobile bimanual morphologies. BiGym truly changes this landscape: it is the first to combine "mobility" with "bimanual arms" and provides natural human demonstrations collected via VR, forcing algorithms to confront the compound challenges of long horizons (1000-7000 steps), sparse rewards, and POMDP state estimation, rather than scoring under the simplified assumption of a single-arm fixed base.

Method Breakdown

Benchmark Architecture

  • Built on MuJoCo and dm_control, featuring the Unitree H1 humanoid robot with Robotiq 2F-85 grippers and actuated wrist joints on each arm.
  • Observation space 𝒪 = {ℐ_head, ℐ_left, ℐ_right, 𝒟_head, 𝒟_left, 𝒟_right, s_proprio}, with default image resolution of 84×84.

Action Modes

  • Full-body mode: Action space A_wb ∈ ℝ^23 = {A_arms ∈ ℝ^10, A_legs ∈ ℝ^10, A_torso ∈ ℝ^1, A_grip ∈ ℝ^2}, providing full control over all joints.
  • Bimanual mode: Action space A_bm ∈ ℝ^16 = {A_arms ∈ ℝ^10, A_base ∈ ℝ^4, A_grip ∈ ℝ^2}, where the lower body is treated as a floating base via a classical controller, focusing on upper-body manipulation.

Demonstration Collection

  • Teleoperation using Valve Index VR equipment, with the headset controlling body pose and the 6 DoF controller poses mapped to arms via inverse kinematics.
  • Demonstrations are collected at a 500 Hz physics frequency and can be downsampled to 20-500 Hz; only lightweight control signals are saved, with observations (RGB/depth) generated during playback.

Tasks and Rewards

  • 40 tasks across 4 categories: goal reaching (3), tabletop manipulation (5), dishwasher (13), and kitchen countertop (19).
  • All tasks provide only sparse rewards: a success detector gives 1, otherwise 0; most tasks use joint angle or distance tolerances (default 0.1) to determine success.

Algorithm Baselines

  • IL: BC, ACT (ResNet-18 + Transformer, action sequence length 16), Diffusion Policy (UNet-1D and Transformer backbones).
  • RL: DrQV2, AWAC, IQL, CQN, all using CNN encoders (3 convolutional layers, 32 channels, SiLU activation) and 2-layer 512-dimensional MLP heads.
  • All methods use common hyperparameters: frame stacking 4, Adam learning rate 0.0001, batch size 256; IL trains for 150K steps, RL for 100K steps.

Key Innovations

  1. Natural human demonstrations via VR: Unlike RLBench's motion-planner-generated trajectories, BiGym's demonstrations contain multimodal noise and variability from real manipulation, imposing fundamentally different requirements on IL algorithms' out-of-distribution generalization.
  2. Mobile bimanual task design: The 40 tasks span from simple goal reaching (reach_target_single) to long-horizon dishwasher operations (dishwasher_unload_cups_long), with task horizons ranging 1000-7000 steps, far exceeding existing benchmarks, and explicitly distinguishing full-body and bimanual control modes to facilitate studying the coupling of mobility and manipulation.
  3. Sparse rewards + demonstration-driven dual-track evaluation: Simultaneously supports IL and RL algorithms, with RL baselines (DrQV2, AWAC, IQL, CQN) systematically failing on complex tasks, providing the community with a clear list of "unsolved problems" rather than merely showcasing algorithmic strengths.

Simulation speed is approximately 400-1400 FPS (depending on the number of cameras), measured on an NVIDIA L4 GPU.

Boundaries and Limitations

The authors explicitly did not replace the parallel grippers with other dexterous manipulators, nor did they benchmark 3D next-best pose agents (due to their reliance on heuristic keyframe extraction, applicable only to single fixed arms). All algorithms use common hyperparameters without per-task tuning, which may underestimate the potential of RL methods. All algorithms fail on long-horizon tasks (e.g., dishwasher_unload_cups_long), indicating a lack of task-level reasoning and hierarchical methods. The paper does not specify demonstration diversity metrics or fidelity validation between human demonstrations and simulation physics.

Engineering Insights

When reproducing BiGym, first verify the demonstration download and caching mechanism (pulled from Google Cloud) and ensure sufficient local storage. The most common pitfall is action mode selection: the full-body mode (A_wb ∈ ℝ^23) and bimanual mode (A_bm ∈ ℝ^16) have different observation space dimensions (s_proprio_fb ∈ ℝ^76 vs s_proprio_bm ∈ ℝ^64), requiring careful alignment when switching. For downstream teams, ACT is recommended as the IL baseline due to its highest average success rate across 40 tasks (46.3%); however, if the goal is RL research, note that CQN's advantages in fixed single-arm settings completely vanish in mobile bimanual scenarios, suggesting the POMDP exploration problem introduced by the mobile base is more severe than expected. Training with frame stacking 4, batch size 256, and learning rate 0.0001 reproduces the paper's results, but IL requires 150K steps and RL requires 100K steps, incurring significant computational costs.
