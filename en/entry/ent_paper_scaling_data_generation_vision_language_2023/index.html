Summary

Recent research in language-guided visual navigation has demonstrated a significant demand for the diversity of traversable environments and the quantity of supervision for training generalizable agents. Institutions per source list: 澳大利亚国立大学、上海 AI Lab.

Overview

This paper proposes ScaleVLN, a fully automated and reproducible large-scale Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) data generation and training paradigm. The authors leverage 1200+ real environments from HM3D and Gibson, construct high-quality navigation graphs, repair rendered images, generate 4.9 million instruction-trajectory pairs, and achieve new state-of-the-art results on four benchmarks—R2R, REVERIE, CVDN, and R2R-CE—through a two-stage pretraining-finetuning pipeline. The core contribution lies in demonstrating that "scene count matters more than sample count" and "navigation graph quality and image quality have a decisive impact on pretraining effectiveness."

What It Changes

The VLN field has long been constrained by data scarcity. R2R has only 22k human annotations, and PREVALENT augmented data amounts to just 178k pairs, all derived from 61 scenes in MP3D. Previous mainstream solutions either relied on large VLMs pretrained on web image-text data, or used sparse, collision-prone navigation graphs like AutoVLN, or depended on non-public Marky and MURAL as in MARVAL—approaches that are neither reproducible nor simplified.

What ScaleVLN truly changes is the underlying assumption of "where data comes from." It demonstrates that rather than racking one's brains over model architecture or pretraining tasks, effort is better spent on "generating high-quality in-domain data using traversable environments." The authors decompose data generation into four sub-problems—navigation graph construction, image inpainting, trajectory sampling, and instruction generation—each solved with off-the-shelf tools, ultimately producing 352 times more data than R2R. The significance of this paradigm is that it gives the VLN community, for the first time, a reproducible, scalable data generation pipeline that significantly outperforms all previous augmentation methods—not through mysterious models, but through engineering that assembles existing components like the Habitat simulator, Co-Mod GAN, and EnvDrop Speaker.

Method Breakdown

Data Generation Pipeline (Four Steps)

  1. Navigation Graph Construction
  2. Sample viewpoints using Habitat, with geodesic distance between any two viewpoints > 0.4 m.
  3. Merge adjacent viewpoints via agglomerative clustering (threshold 1.0 m), automatically positioning nodes near open-space centers.
  4. Randomly connect viewpoints within 5.0 m, with a maximum of 5 edges per node, then apply graph refinement to ensure full connectivity and complete traversability.

  5. Final graph: average edge length 1.41 m, average node degree 4.55, collision rate 0.00% (compared to AutoVLN's 29.35%).

  6. Image Inpainting

  7. Treat corrupted renders as image-to-image translation, training a Co-Modulated GAN on Matterport3D render-camera image pairs.

  8. Crop 4 images of 512×512 from 512×1024 panoramas (center angles 0°/90°/180°/270°), inpaint each, then extract 36 single-view images (12 viewpoints × 3 elevations).

  9. Trajectory Sampling

  10. Following PREVALENT, collect all shortest paths connecting any two nodes via 3-5 intermediate nodes.

  11. HM3D yields 2,890,267 paths; Gibson yields 2,051,443; totaling 4,941,710 instruction-trajectory pairs.

  12. Instruction Generation

  13. Use the off-the-shelf EnvDrop Speaker (LSTM, trained on R2R) to generate instructions for each trajectory.
  14. The authors explicitly compare GPT-2: EnvDrop achieves higher Bleu-4 (27.66 vs 24.36) and similar improvements for R2R agents, but with lower diversity.

Training Strategy

  • Pretraining: DUET or HAMT, 20k iterations, batch size 256, lr 5×10⁻⁵, 2×A100, approximately 72 GPU hours.
  • Auxiliary tasks: MLM (mask 15% tokens), MRM (mask 15% views), SAP (predict next action), sampled uniformly per iteration.
  • Finetuning: DA GGER (imitation learning), 200k iterations, batch size 16, single GPU, approximately 48 GPU hours.
  • Key decisions:
  • Pretraining uses R2R+PREV+HM-E as complementary; finetuning retains HM-E but removes PREV (PREV causes overfitting to MP3D).
  • Adding Gib-E during finetuning is unhelpful (domain gap introduces noise).
  • Combining MRM with MLM+SAP slightly degrades performance (large-scale data already provides rich semantics; masked-region prediction introduces noise).

Key Innovations

  1. Empirical finding that "scene count > sample count": Table 2 shows that 800 scenes/700k samples (Val-Unseen SR 76.25) significantly outperforms 200 scenes/700k samples (SR 74.84), while 800 scenes/2890k samples (SR 76.59) differs little from 800 scenes/700k samples (SR 76.25). This directly overturns the intuition of "stacking data volume" and points the way for future data generation work—collecting more environments is more cost-effective than sampling more trajectories.

  2. Reproducible construction method for high-quality navigation graphs: AutoVLN's graph has sparse nodes (density 0.36 nodes/m²) and a 29.35% collision rate; MARVAL uses trained models to predict navigable directions (complex and error-prone). ScaleVLN uses Habitat sampling + agglomerative clustering + graph refinement to achieve a graph with density 1.16 nodes/m² and 0.00% collision rate, fully reproducible. This case of "simple engineering beating complex learning" is highly persuasive in the VLN data generation domain.

  3. Decisive impact of image inpainting on pretraining: Table 4 shows that HM-E (R) + Gib-E (F) (61% more environments but corrupted images) performs worse than HM-E (R) alone (Val-Unseen SR 76.33 vs 76.59). This demonstrates that "visual quality > environment count"—using Co-Mod GAN for image inpainting is not a nice-to-have but a necessary component of the data generation pipeline.

Key Ablation Findings

  • Data volume: 800 scenes/700k samples (SR 76.25) ≈ 800 scenes/2890k samples (SR 76.59), but far better than 200 scenes/700k (SR 74.84)—scene count dominates.
  • Pretraining data combination: R2R+PREV+HM-E pretraining + R2R+HM-E finetuning (method 6) achieves Val-Unseen SR 79.10, with seen/unseen gap < 1% (80.02 vs 79.10).
  • Pretraining tasks: MLM+SAP improves SPL by 16.49% over no pretraining; adding MRM slightly degrades (Val-Unseen SR 75.82 vs 76.59).
  • Vision encoder: CLIP ViT-H/14 improves Val-Unseen SPL by 1.4 (calculated from in-table values 11.0÷8.0) over B/16 (69.71 vs 68.31).
  • Depth modality: Without HM3D augmentation, RGBD significantly boosts SR (74.12 vs 72.92); with HM3D augmentation, RGBD's SR is slightly lower than RGB-only (76.46 vs 76.59).
  • Cross-model generalization: Seq2Seq, EnvDrop, and HAMT improve SR by 12.2%, 3.8%, and 5.5%, respectively, with ScaleVLN compared to PREVALENT.

Boundaries and Limitations

The authors do not explicitly list a limitations section, but inferences can be drawn from the experiments:

  • Instruction quality ceiling: EnvDrop Speaker's Bleu-4 is only 27.66; the authors acknowledge low quality and poor diversity of generated instructions, and only tried two simple generation models. Better instruction generation (e.g., GPT-4-level) is a clearly unexplored direction.
  • Gibson data ineffective during finetuning: The authors speculate domain gap introduces noise but provide no in-depth analysis (e.g., domain adaptation or data filtering).
  • Stopping problem unresolved: The authors note that the remaining 6% SR gap to humans is similar to the OSR-SR gap (6-7%), implying the policy network needs improvement, but no solution is proposed.
  • No real-world validation: No sim-to-real transfer experiments are mentioned.
  • Depth modality conclusion questionable: The authors suspect depth information becomes unnecessary as data scales up, but this is unverified.

Engineering Insights

When reproducing or borrowing from ScaleVLN, prioritize checking the following three points:

  1. Navigation graph quality is the foundation: First check the graph's collision rate and node density. AutoVLN's 29.35% collision rate directly contaminates pretraining data. Using Habitat sampling + agglomerative clustering (threshold 1.0 m) + 5.0 m connections + max 5 edges per node, an average edge length of 1.41 m and node degree of 4.55 are reasonable targets.

  2. Image inpainting cannot be skipped: Corrupted renders from HM3D/Gibson are a real problem. Table 4 proves that "61% more environments but bad images" is worse than "fewer environments but good images." Co-Mod GAN trained on Matterport3D suffices, but note the vertical seams at panorama stitching points (caused by independent inpainting of 0° and 180°)—the authors say these do not appear in the final data; verify the cropping logic when reproducing.

  3. Finetuning data combination is the biggest pitfall: Pretraining uses R2R+PREV+HM-E as complementary, but finetuning must remove PREV (otherwise overfitting to MP3D drops Val-Unseen SR from 79.10 to 75.78). Gibson data is used only for pretraining; adding it during finetuning is harmful. This "asymmetric pretraining and finetuning data" conclusion is the easiest to trip over—directly copying the PREVALENT-era practice of "using the same augmentation for both pretraining and finetuning" will cause significant performance drops.

Additionally, choosing CLIP ViT-H/14 as the vision encoder stably improves SPL (+1.4), but doubles training cost; if compute is limited, ViT-B/16's SR gap is small (78.08 vs 78.20), so B/16 can be prioritized for ablations.
