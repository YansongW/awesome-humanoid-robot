Overview

This paper, jointly authored by Fudan University, Shanghai Innovation Institute, and the National University of Singapore, is the first to systematically propose and define World Action Models (WAMs), a new paradigm in embodied intelligence—namely, embodied foundation models that unify predictive state modeling and action generation. The paper provides a formal definition of WAMs, two core criteria, a taxonomy of two major architectural categories (Cascaded and Joint), and a comprehensive review of the data ecosystem, evaluation protocols, and representative works, aiming to offer a unified conceptual framework for this fragmented research area.

What It Changes

What this survey truly changes is the conceptual positioning of embodied intelligence models. For a long time, VLA models have been viewed as reactive mappers from "observations to actions," while world models have been treated as external simulators or data augmentation tools, with the two being disconnected in architecture, objectives, and application scenarios. The authors reposition world models from external tools (supervisors) to the predictive core within the policy architecture, establishing a new paradigm where "World" (predicting physics) and "Action" (motor control) are equal components. The significance of this conceptual reframing lies in the fact that it provides, for the first time, a unified classification coordinate system for the scattered "prediction + control" works, enabling researchers to clearly identify where different works sit within the architectural spectrum, rather than fighting in fragmented terminology.

More importantly, the authors clarify long-standing terminological confusion by distinguishing WAMs from adjacent concepts such as Video Action Models (VAMs), Video Policies, and Action World Models (AWMs). WAM is a modality-agnostic superset, where video is merely one proxy for modeling the world; Video Policies only need to inherit pretrained spatiotemporal representations to map observations to actions, whereas WAMs must have an explicit predictive commitment—the synthesis of the next state must be an explicit component of the model's reasoning and output. This delineation of conceptual boundaries provides clear admission criteria for subsequent research.

Method Decomposition

Formal Definition of WAM

The WAM target distribution is the joint distribution \(p(o', a | o, l)\), with the objective function: $\(\mathcal{L}_{WAM} = \mathbb{E}_{(o,l,o',a)\sim\mathcal{D}}[-\log p(o', a | o, l)]\)$

Two Core Criteria

  1. Forward predictive modeling: Must generate or utilize a quantifiable representation of the future state \(o'\) (explicit visual prediction such as pixel-level video frames, dense optical flow, or implicit physical representations such as physics-grounded latent spaces)
  2. Coupled action generation: The action \(a\) must be strictly aligned with the expected future state \(o'\) (joint probability output or policy conditioning in cascaded/unified latent architectures)

Two Architectural Categories

  • Cascaded WAM: Explicitly decomposes the objective \(p(o', a|o, l) = p(a|o', o, l)p(o'|o, l)\), first synthesizing future state representations and then deriving actions
  • Explicit (pixel space): Learned action extraction (UniPi's IDM, π0.7's VLA-replacing IDM), geometric action extraction (AVDC's optical flow + SE(3) analytical derivation, 3DFlowAction's 3D flow fields)
  • Implicit (latent representations): VPP's VAE encoding + single-step diffusion prediction, S-VAM's self-distillation compression, MWM's future semantic masks replacing RGB prediction
  • Joint WAM: Directly models the joint distribution, with state prediction and action generation co-optimized in a shared representation space
  • Autoregressive: GR-1/GR-2's explicit decoupled representations, CoT-VLA's hybrid attention routing, WorldVLA's modality-specific causal masks
  • Diffusion-based: Unified Stream (UWM's independent noise level control, Cosmos Policy's latent frame injection) and Multi-Stream (DUST's independent flow-matching losses, DiT4DiT's paired Video DiT–Action DiT architecture)

Key Design Decisions

  • Cascaded decomposition provides a natural inductive bias: the world model need not reason about robot kinematics, and the action model need not solve long-horizon scene prediction
  • Implicit latent planning (VPP, S-VAM) aims to bypass the computational bottleneck of pixel-level video synthesis, achieving inference speeds compatible with real-time control
  • Autoregressive joint prediction faces a tension between catastrophic error propagation and the computational bottleneck of sequential decoding, driving the development of diffusion and parallel decoding paths

Key Innovations

1. First formal definition and taxonomy of WAMs: Previously, works integrating world models and action generation were fragmented in architecture, learning objectives, and application scenarios, lacking a unified conceptual framework. The authors' two core criteria (forward predictive modeling + coupled action generation) and the Cascaded/Joint dichotomy provide an actionable classification coordinate system for the entire field, enabling researchers to precisely locate their own contributions and differences from adjacent works.

2. Clarifying the boundaries between WAMs and adjacent concepts: The modality-agnostic superset relationship with VAMs, the "explicit predictive commitment" distinction from Video Policies, and the repositioning of AWMs as a "major agent category"—these conceptual distinctions are not mere terminological games but directly determine the minimum requirements for model design: merely inheriting pretrained spatiotemporal representations to map observations to actions is insufficient to qualify as a WAM; explicit next-state synthesis must be a component of reasoning and output.

3. Systematic review of implicit latent planning paths: The authors explicitly identify the computational overhead of pixel-level video synthesis as the primary bottleneck for real-time deployment and systematically organize three technical routes that bypass pixel reconstruction—RSSM/TSSM state-space models, JEPA-paradigm latent embedding prediction, and lightweight latent prediction in works such as VPP/S-VAM/MWM. This review provides a clear technology selection map for real-time deployment-oriented research.

Boundaries and Limitations

Limitations explicitly acknowledged by the authors include: autoregressive video world models suffer from severe error accumulation and struggle to model highly multimodal distributions; diffusion video world models are computationally intensive, with pixel-level diffusion denoising being costly in RGB space; and current evaluation protocols have gaps in assessing WAM-related capabilities. Additionally, the authors note that traditional simulators (IsaacGym, MuJoCo) struggle to capture the complexity and variability of real environments, leading to significant sim-to-real gaps.

The paper does not mention a systematic analysis of the limitations of its own survey methodology or specific recommendations for future work directions (Sec. 7 discusses key obstacles and future directions but does not elaborate). Training configurations, data volumes, inference frequencies, and hardware details are not mentioned, which limits readers' ability to directly judge the practical deployment feasibility of the models discussed. Notably, many models in the paper are validated only in simulation environments (e.g., RoboEnvision, This&That, TesserAct, CoT-VLA, WorldVLA), and their real-world generalization capabilities remain unverified.

Engineering Implications

Direct guidance for engineering reproduction and model selection:

First, verify three things: First, determine whether your task requires an "explicit predictive commitment"—if reactive mapping suffices, a standard VLA is adequate without introducing the additional complexity of WAMs; second, assess your real-time budget—pixel-level video synthesis (explicit Cascaded path) incurs extremely high computational costs for denoising in RGB space; if closed-loop high-frequency control is needed, prioritize implicit latent planning (VPP, S-VAM routes) or Joint WAM with diffusion parallel decoding; third, consider data conditions—standard VLAs strictly require paired \((o_t, a_t)\) trajectories; if your data is predominantly action-free video, UWM's independent noise level control or PAD's token sequence expansion strategy can incorporate action-free internet video data.

Most common pitfalls: First, catastrophic error propagation in autoregressive joint prediction—early visual hallucination cascades lead to subsequent action failures; pay attention to mitigation mechanisms such as CoT-VLA's hybrid attention routing or AIM's intent-causal attention masks; second, the "pixel-matching shortcut"—models waste capacity on reconstructing irrelevant background changes rather than learning actionable transition physics; MWM's geometric information bottleneck or LDA-1B's DINO latent-space prediction are worthwhile strategies to avoid this; third, pure discrete control exhibits high variance; if discrete action tokens are adopted, accompanying variance mitigation measures are needed; fourth, two-stage error accumulation in cascaded architectures—UniPi's single long-horizon generation has been found to suffer from semantic drift and compound errors; VLP's semantic-level intervention (limiting error accumulation within a single segment) or Veo-Act's online contact detector (handing control to a reactive VLA upon detecting imminent contact) are effective engineering mitigations.
