Summary

In humanoid motion control, model predictive control (MPC) offers physically grounded prediction and constraint handling, while reinforcement learning (RL) enables robust whole-body skills through large-scale simulation. Institutions per source list: 加州理工学院、JHU.

Overview

This paper proposes the MPC-RL framework, which converts the predicted trajectories generated by centroidal dynamics MPC (CD-MPC) during training into landmark rewards to guide PPO training for humanoid locomotion and manipulation policies, requiring no MPC at deployment. The core contribution is the πⁿ MPC solver, which achieves construction-free batched solving with parallel-in-horizon via velocity-form and a novel variable splitting, significantly reducing training overhead. It is validated on the Themis humanoid across tasks including pushing a 290 kg cart (829% of body weight).

What It Changes

The combination of MPC and RL has long faced the dilemma of "expensive training" or "slow construction": residual policy methods rely on real-time MPC solving at test time, while offline trajectory libraries limit exploration scope. What this paper truly changes is shifting MPC from a "computational burden at deployment" to a "data generator during training"—once the policy is trained, MPC is entirely offline, fundamentally bypassing test-time solving and state estimation dependencies. Additionally, it redesigns the solver to address the structural bottleneck of GPU batched MPC solvers in cross-horizon parallelism, making long-horizon MPC guidance feasible across 4096 parallel environments, rather than merely elegant in theory.

Another dimension changed is the engineering cost of problem construction. Traditional sparse QP construction grows explosively in VRAM and symbolic compilation with prediction horizon N (e.g., CusADi requires 150 GB RAM and 1.5 hours of compilation at N=10), nearly condemning long-horizon MPC guidance in resource-constrained settings. By leveraging closed-form parallel-in-horizon updates and precomputed caches, this paper liberates problem construction from per-iteration symbolic operations, turning "MPC-guided RL" from a lab toy into an engineering-reproducible pipeline.

Method Breakdown

CD-MPC Model and Reward Generation

  • Centroidal state ξ = [c⊤, l_G⊤, k_G⊤]⊤ ∈ ℝ⁹, with discrete dynamics stepping at Δt, and contact forces constrained by CWC and friction cones.
  • Extract N_L landmarks from MPC predicted trajectories, with time indices δ_ℓ = n_ℓ·Δt, n_ℓ = 1 + ⌊s_ℓ(N−1)⌋, s_ℓ ∈ [0,1].
  • Reward r_t^{mpc,c} = exp(−Σ_{ℓ=0}^{N_L−1} α_ℓ·‖e_c^ℓ(t)‖²_{Q_c}/σ_c²), where e_c^ℓ(t) = c_t − ĉ^{t−δ_ℓ}(δ_ℓ).
  • Confidence weighting α_0 > α_1 > ⋯ > α_{N_L−1} > 0, with α = {0.5, 0.25, 0.15, 0.07, 0.03} in experiments; α_0=1 degenerates to strict next-step supervision, α_ℓ=1/N_L degenerates to time-averaged tracking.

πⁿ MPC Solver

  • Based on π MPC's parallel-in-horizon ADMM, extended to time-varying dynamics matrices, supporting PyTorch/JAX.
  • Key design one: velocity-form MPC, optimizing input increments Δu_k rather than absolute inputs.
  • Key design two: novel variable splitting—introducing auxiliary variables z_{k+1} = ξ̄{k+1} and v_k = B̄_k·Δu_k, duplicating v_k instead of Δu_k, and incorporating z into the dynamics equations.
  • This splitting guarantees J_k = (B̄kᵀB̄_k)⁻¹B̄_kᵀ can be precomputed and cached (since B̄ᵀB̄{t,k} = B + I ≻ 0), turning ADMM updates into closed-form parallel-in-horizon operations.}ᵀB_{t,k
  • Inherits Nesterov acceleration and restart options.

Training Configuration

  • PPO (rsl-rl) + mjlab, N_env=4096, physics at 200 Hz, control at 50 Hz, episode length 20 s.
  • Action a_t ∈ ℝ²⁹ as joint position increments, tracked by PD; actor observes only proprioception and joystick commands, critic additionally receives noise-free state and MPC references.
  • Effective inertia PD gains: K_{p,i} = J_{eff,i}·ω_{n,i}², K_{d,i} = 2ζ_i·J_{eff,i}·ω_{n,i}, with J_{eff,i} ≈ M_{ii}(q⋆) + A_i, and ω_{n,i} chosen at 1-2 Hz (adapted to Themis's high-inertia, low-gear-ratio actuators).

Key Innovations

  1. Confidence weighting in landmark-guided rewards: Rather than simply treating MPC trajectories as tracking targets, the decreasing α_ℓ explicitly models the physical intuition that "near-horizon predictions are more trustworthy." This prevents model mismatch and integration errors in long-horizon MPC predictions from being blindly amplified by the RL policy, representing an adaptive compromise between MPC priors and RL exploration.

  2. Variable splitting that "duplicates v_k instead of Δu_k": This seemingly minor choice is the key to πⁿ MPC's construction-free and parallel-in-horizon capabilities. It enables precomputation of the inverse of B̄_kᵀB̄_k, transforming ADMM updates from sequential symbolic operations into closed-form batched operations, directly bypassing the VRAM/compilation bottlenecks of traditional QP construction.

  3. MPC information injection in asymmetric actor-critic: The critic exclusively receives MPC-predicted CoM and angular momentum references, while the actor relies only on proprioception. This ensures hardware deployability of the policy at test time (no MPC or state estimator required), while allowing the value function to obtain more accurate long-term return estimates during training—a natural extension of the "privileged information at training" paradigm under MPC guidance.

Boundaries and Limitations

The paper does not explicitly list limitations. Inferred from the fact points, MPC-RL validation is concentrated on the Themis-specific hardware platform (high-inertia, low-gear-ratio actuators), and its PD gain choices (1-2 Hz natural frequency) may not transfer to high-bandwidth, low-inertia humanoids. Additionally, all hardware experiments are conducted on treadmills or controlled push/pull scenarios, without unstructured terrain or dynamic unknown environments. Although MPC-guided loco-manipulation is mentioned, the "manipulation" in hardware validation is limited to cart pushing, excluding fine grasping or bimanual coordination tasks. The specific trade-off values between MPC horizon N and update rate during training are not given in the fact points, making it impossible to assess robustness under varying computational resources.

Engineering Insights

When reproducing, first verify the variable splitting implementation of the MPC solver—ensure that v_k = B̄k·Δu_k is duplicated rather than Δu_k, otherwise the J_k precomputed cache fails and the parallel-in-horizon advantage is lost. Second, the landmark confidence weighting α schedule (0.5→0.03) is central to reward design; using uniform weights or only next-step supervision may significantly degrade training stability and final performance. The most common engineering pitfall is PD gain selection: Themis's low-gear-ratio actuators require ω at 1-2 Hz; copying high-bandwidth servo parameters may cause policy divergence due to actuator saturation. Finally, actor observation noise injection (e.g., body angular velocity ±0.2 rad/s) is critical for sim-to-real transfer; omitting it leads to severe performance drops at hardware deployment. It is recommended that downstream teams first reproduce the 4096-environment training configuration, then gradually adjust MPC horizon N and update rate to balance GPU memory and training time.
