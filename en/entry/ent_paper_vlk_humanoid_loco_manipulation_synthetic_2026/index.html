Summary

Perception-based humanoid loco-manipulation requires connecting egocentric observations and task instructions to whole-body motion. Learning this mapping requires synchronized egocentric images, language commands, and robot-compatible kinematic trajectories, yet no existing data source provides this complete tuple at scale. Institutions per source list: UC Berkeley、CMU、Shanghai AI Lab.

Overview

This paper proposes the VLK system, which synthesizes large-scale vision-language-kinematics triplet data in 3DGS-reconstructed scenes through a real-to-sim-to-real pipeline to train perception-locomotion-manipulation policies for the Unitree G1 humanoid robot. The core contribution is connecting 3DGS scene reconstruction with whole-body motion tracking into a complete data generation system, enabling end-to-end language-instruction-driven manipulation without human demonstrations, validated across 120 real-world trials.

What It Changes

Humanoid robot locomotion-manipulation learning has long been constrained by data scarcity: real teleoperation is costly and difficult to scale, human motion capture lacks robot egocentric observations, and egocentric videos lack robot-compatible kinematics. What the authors truly change is the data generation paradigm—no longer relying on human demonstrations or real data collection, but instead reconstructing real scenes via 3DGS, synthesizing interaction trajectories, and rendering egocentric observations, integrating three fragmented approaches (scene reconstruction, motion synthesis, visual rendering) into a closed-loop data factory. This shift enables on-demand, infinitely scalable training data with inherent vision-language-kinematics alignment, resolving the fundamental contradiction where previous data sources could not simultaneously provide all three modalities.

Method Breakdown

Three-Stage Data Generation Pipeline

  1. Scene Reconstruction and Annotation: iPhone 14 Pro Polycam scanning (RGB + LiDAR depth) reconstructs metric-scale 3DGS scenes; point clouds are extracted and semantic 3D bounding boxes (object categories such as chair/table/box) and walkable region polygons are manually annotated using the viser tool.
  2. Interaction Synthesis: The navigation model is trained with G1 motions from BONES-SEED; the interaction model is trained with OMOMO sequences retargeted to G1 via OmniRetarget. Interaction synthesis builds on CHOIS/HiHi conditional diffusion formulations, with additional conditions including initial object state, object geometry (BPS representation + MLP projection), desired relative wrist pose (defined in the object coordinate frame), and wrist contact labels.
  3. Egocentric Rendering: Synthesized G1 trajectories are replayed in Isaac Sim with the 3DGS scene loaded, and a virtual ZED 2i camera is mounted on the head (extrinsics calibrated to match the real camera); domain randomization is applied (lighting variation, camera extrinsics and focal length perturbation, image-space augmentation).

Motion Representation and Policy Architecture

  • Kinematic State: x_τ = [Δx_τ, Δy_τ, Δψ_τ, h_τ, R^root_τ, sin(q_τ), cos(q_τ), c_τ], where c_τ denotes left/right wrist-object contact labels (∈{0,1}), predicted autoregressively at deployment rather than directly observed.
  • Object Trajectory: X_obj = [o, R_obj], where o is the global object position and R_obj is the 6D rotation relative to the input object geometry.
  • VLK Policy: Inputs are the current egocentric RGB observation o_t, language instruction ℓ, and current kinematic state x_t; outputs H=30 frames of future trajectory (1 second at 30Hz). Initialized from pretrained π_0.5 and fine-tuned across the full model; flow matching with x_0 target prediction, reconstruction loss L_traj = ||x̂ − x||²₂.
  • Auxiliary Losses: Foot-ground contact prediction (focal loss), cumulative root trajectory consistency, forward kinematics accuracy (ankle/wrist, weight 1.0), and foot sliding regularization (weight 0.05).

Post-Processing and Deployment

  • Foot Slip Correction: Predicted foot contact labels identify contact phases, and inverse kinematics constrains feet to fixed contact positions within each phase.
  • Hand-Object Contact Optimization: Adapted from the EgoAllo optimization method, driving wrist positions and rotations to the input wrist poses in the object local coordinate frame.
  • Deployment System: Triton inference backend, VLK on RTX 5090 (31ms end-to-end), whole-body tracking on RTX 5000 Ada (50Hz, 4.3ms per tick); adjacent action chunks overlap by 10 frames; sharpness-based frame selection using Laplacian variance addresses motion blur.

Key Innovations

  1. Synthetic Data Closed Loop: First to connect 3DGS scene reconstruction with whole-body motion tracking into a complete data generation system, achieving a real-to-sim-to-real closed loop. Compared to prior fragmented approaches, this system generates arbitrarily scaled paired data on demand with inherent vision-language-kinematics alignment.
  2. Autoregressive Contact Label Prediction: At deployment, wrist-object contact is not directly observed but predicted autoregressively by the policy, eliminating additional sensor dependencies while maintaining stable object interaction control.
  3. Differentiable Forward Kinematics Layer: A differentiable FK layer in the training loss imposes geometric constraints on global body and end-effector positions, significantly improving the physical plausibility of generated trajectories and wrist placement accuracy—something difficult to achieve with pure joint-angle-space training.

Key Ablations

  • Contact Label Necessity: Without contact labels, Pick (Floor) achieved 0/5 success in both real-world scenes, demonstrating that contact labels are critical for manipulation stability.
  • Data Scale Impact: Pick (Surface) improved from 0% success with 10% data to 46% success with the full dataset, showing the decisive role of data scale for complex tasks.
  • Domain Randomization: Walking mode success improved from 41% without randomization to 90% with full randomization, with lighting randomization alone contributing the most (87%, per original paper Table values).

Boundaries and Limitations

Interaction synthesis is limited by OMOMO coverage, which includes only a finite set of large-object interactions, generating behaviors better suited for two-handed box carrying rather than grasping cups or tools. The contact-aware tracker stabilizes large-object manipulation but does not handle precise object grasping. Surface-level interactions (Pick/Put Surface) show lower reliability, partly due to limited retargeted interaction data for box manipulation across different support surface heights. The paper does not explicitly discuss real-world failure case categorization, generalization to other robot platforms, generalization to unseen object categories or scene layouts, or long-duration deployment stability.

Engineering Insights

When reproducing, prioritize three aspects: first, scene reconstruction quality—whether 3DGS optimization converges and semantic annotations are accurate, as this directly affects synthetic data quality; second, domain randomization configuration—lighting randomization contributes the most to success rate improvement (41%→87%) and must be fully implemented; third, contact label generation and prediction—this is key to manipulation stability, as Pick (Floor) real-world success drops to zero without contact labels. Common pitfalls include: OMOMO retargeting quality to G1 (recommend validating physical plausibility of retargeted trajectories first), motion blur handling at deployment (sharpness frame selection window of 0.3s must match inference frequency), and temporal synchronization of asynchronous chunk merging (merging contributes 3.4ms and must not block the 50Hz tracking loop). Data generation costs approximately 600 GPU hours per environment; it is recommended to validate pipeline correctness at small scale before full-scale generation.
