Overview

CubeMars is a global robotics power system supplier with 17 years of experience in motor R&D and manufacturing, specializing in the design and mass production of compact integrated joint motors and actuators. What it truly changes is not the motor itself, but the high-level integration of motor, reducer, driver, and encoder, moving humanoid robot joint modules from customization toward standardization and scalable supply.

Content

What It Is: Precise Definition

CubeMars falls under the category of component manufacturer, positioned at the upstream level of the value chain. Its core products are joint motors and integrated actuators designed for humanoid robots, quadruped robots, exoskeletons, gimbal systems, and underwater thrusters. Its product portfolio covers frameless inner-rotor torque motors, planetary gear joint modules (AK series), gimbal motors, and underwater thrusters, offering end-to-end services from custom prototyping to mass production.

CubeMars' flagship AK series integrates motor, reducer, driver, and encoder into a single compact unit, representing a typical integrated joint solution. These modules directly output torque and angle feedback, eliminating the need for downstream robot manufacturers to match motors and reducers themselves, significantly shortening the joint integration cycle.

Why It Exists: Pain Points and Historical Positioning

Before the industrialization of humanoid robots, robot joint design faced a fundamental contradiction: the high-speed, low-torque characteristics of motors did not match the low-speed, high-torque requirements of joints. Traditional industrial solutions used high-ratio reducers (typically greater than 50:1) to amplify torque, but at the cost of increased friction, difficult backdrivability, external shock loads being amplified by the reduction ratio and potentially damaging the motor side, and the inability to directly sense output torque.

CubeMars' positioning sits precisely at the compromise point of this contradiction. Its AK series employs a moderate reduction ratio (engineering judgment, based on the "compact, precise" product positioning in the source material), retaining some torque amplification capability while maintaining backdrivable transparency, giving joints the compliance needed for force control and human-robot interaction. Its participation in the MIT Mini Cheetah project in 2017 helped define the standard for high-performance robot joints, marking a critical transition from industrial motors to high-dynamic robot actuators.

Principle Breakdown

① Trade-off Between Torque Amplification and Reflected Inertia

The relationship between joint output torque, motor torque, and reduction ratio is:

\[ \tau_{\text{out}} = G \, \tau_m - J_{\text{eq}} \ddot{\theta}_m \]

where \(G\) is the reduction ratio, \(\tau_m\) is the motor output torque, and \(J_{\text{eq}}\) is the equivalent reflected inertia. The larger the reduction ratio, the higher the output torque, but the inertia reflected from the motor side to the output end also scales by \(G^2\), reducing the joint's dynamic response and transparency. CubeMars' AK series balances this contradiction within a compact volume—providing sufficient peak torque for dynamic motions while retaining partial backdrivability to support force control.

② Engineering Value of Integrated Design

Traditional joints require separately sourcing motors, reducers, encoders, and drivers, then assembling them, facing multiple engineering challenges such as interface matching, thermal design, and electromagnetic compatibility. CubeMars integrates these components into a single mechanical unit, eliminating interface mismatch risks at the source and unifying thermal management and cable routing. This modular joint design is a key approach for improving maintainability and scalability in modern humanoid robots.

③ Mass Production Capability

In 2025, CubeMars' annual output of planetary gear joints exceeded 100,000 units. This production capacity enables it to support humanoid robots transitioning from prototypes to mass production. The cost amortization and yield improvements brought by scale production are key considerations for downstream robot manufacturers when selecting suppliers.

Who Uses It: Application Cases

  • MIT Mini Cheetah (2017): CubeMars supported the development of MIT's Mini Cheetah quadruped robot, helping define high-performance robot joint standards. Mini Cheetah is known for its highly dynamic locomotion capabilities, demanding extreme power density and response speed from actuators.
  • Global exoskeleton companies (2023): CubeMars collaborated with leading global exoskeleton companies, providing high-precision, high-reliability drive systems. Exoskeletons require actuators to deliver sufficient assistive torque within compact volumes while maintaining compliance to ensure wearer safety.
  • Humanoid robot joint modules (2024): CubeMars achieved full-stack development and mass production capability for humanoid robot joint modules, covering the complete chain from motor design and reducer matching to driver and encoder integration.
  • Quadruped robots (2016): Supported US quadruped robot projects, overcoming challenges in high-dynamic motor response.
  • Gimbal systems and ROV underwater thrusters: Product line extends to gimbal motors and underwater thrusters—the former requiring lightweight design and low cogging torque, the latter requiring corrosion resistance and pressure tolerance.

Limitations and Boundaries

  1. Mid-range product positioning: CubeMars' AK series strikes a compromise between torque density and transparency. For lower-limb large joints demanding extreme torque density (e.g., knee joint peak requirements of 200 N·m), larger modules or custom solutions may be needed (engineering judgment).
  2. Geographically concentrated supply chain: As a Chinese manufacturer, its production capacity and delivery are influenced by the domestic supply chain, and its global footprint remains to be observed.
  3. Risk of technology generation gaps: Humanoid robot actuator technology iterates rapidly, with harmonic reducers, planetary roller screws, quasi-direct drive, and other approaches coexisting. CubeMars must continue investing to remain competitive.
  4. No coverage of hydraulic actuator routes: For the hydraulic approach used by Boston Dynamics' early Atlas, CubeMars' electric product line cannot cover it, though electrification is the current mainstream trend.

Common Misconceptions

  1. "CubeMars is just a motor manufacturer"—Inaccurate. Its AK series is an integrated module of motor, reducer, driver, and encoder, making it essentially an integrated actuator supplier rather than a pure motor vendor.
  2. "17 years of experience equals 17 years since founding"—Incorrect. CubeMars was formally established in 2018; the 17 years refers to the core team's technical accumulation in the motor field.
  3. "100,000 units annual output equals all joint modules"—Note that this figure refers specifically to planetary gear joint output; production volumes for other categories (such as gimbal motors and underwater thrusters) are not disclosed.
  4. "Participation in MIT Mini Cheetah equals official MIT collaboration"—The source material states "contributed to," meaning support and contribution were provided, but the specific form of collaboration is not disclosed and should not be over-interpreted.
