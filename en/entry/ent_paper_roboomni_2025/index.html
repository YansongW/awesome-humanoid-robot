Overview

RoboOmni is an end-to-end omni-modal LLM framework designed to enable robots to proactively infer user intent from cross-modal contexts of speech, environmental audio, and vision, rather than passively executing explicit instructions. The authors constructed the OmniAction dataset (141,162 multimodal episodes) and the OmniAction-LIBERO benchmark, achieving an average success rate of 85.6% across six contextual instruction types, far surpassing the strongest baseline NORA (25.9%), while reducing inference latency to approximately half that of cascaded approaches.

What It Changes

Existing VLA systems remain fundamentally in the "instruction-execution" paradigm: they either wait for text commands or degrade speech to ASR text, discarding paralinguistic cues such as intonation, emotion, and speaker identity, while completely ignoring environmental sounds. This causes robots in real household scenarios to blindly guess or refuse to act when faced with ambiguous dialogue (e.g., "put that thing over there"). What RoboOmni truly changes is elevating "intent inference" from the periphery of research to the core of the system—it no longer assumes intent is given, but instead treats intent discovery itself as a first-class task of the model, resolving ambiguity through multimodal cues (who is speaking, how they speak, what is happening in the environment), and proactively clarifying when necessary. This shift moves human-robot interaction from "tool use" toward "collaborative understanding," representing a paradigm-level displacement.

Method Breakdown

Architecture: Perceiver–Thinker–Talker–Executor

  • Perceiver: Visual observations I_t are embedded via f_v to obtain v_t, audio segments x_t are embedded via f_s to obtain s_t, and concatenated with text context c_t into a unified input X_t = [v_t; s_t; c_t], following the multimodal processing pipeline of Qwen2.5-Omni.
  • Thinker: The LLM backbone autoregressively generates text tokens, speech representations, and action tokens over the joint vocabulary space V ∪ A, enabling omni-modal reasoning.
  • Talker: Converts the Thinker's high-level semantic representations into speech waveforms.
  • Executor: Extends the vocabulary with 2048 discrete action tokens (FAST+ tokenizer), encoding continuous 7-DoF action vectors a_t ∈ R^7 into short discrete sequences r_t ⊂ A.

Unified Training Objective

Dialogue and action supervision are unified under autoregressive maximum likelihood: L(θ) = L_chat(θ) + L_act(θ) = -E ∑{k=1}^{K} log p_θ(z_k|X_t, z), z_k ∈ V ∪ A The two loss types are combined via batch interleaving, with actions predicted in chunks (size 6).

OmniAction Dataset Construction (Three Stages)

  1. Text Scripting: Sampled 74,645 trajectories from Open-X Embodiment, using GPT-4o to rewrite atomic instructions into multi-turn household dialogues across six contextual instruction types (Sentiment, Overlapping, Non-Verbal, Identity, Dyadic, Triadic), including filtering, dialogue synthesis, interaction expansion, and intent consistency verification.
  2. Auditory Implementation: Three neural TTS systems (MOSS-TTSD, CosyVoice, Gemini-TTS) synthesized speech with 5,096 voice timbres (categorized into six classes by age/gender); multi-speaker simulation used CTC to control overlap offsets; 2,482 non-verbal events were inserted; 640 environmental backgrounds were overlaid (with controlled SNR).
  3. Verification: Human evaluation of sampled dialogues confirmed 98.7% consistency in task intent recoverability.

Key Innovations

  1. First end-to-end framework incorporating environmental audio into VLA action decisions: Prior work either ignored audio or used only ASR-transcribed text. RoboOmni allows raw audio (including paralinguistic cues and environmental sounds) to directly participate in intent inference, achieving 82% average success on Non-Verbal instructions (e.g., "do A when you hear the doorbell, do B when you hear thunder"), where cascaded baselines nearly fail.
  2. Unified token-space joint generation of dialogue and actions: By discretizing continuous actions via FAST+, chat and manipulation share the same autoregressive objective without requiring specialized diffusion heads or action experts (contrast π0), simplifying the architecture and improving training efficiency—pretraining + SFT reaches nearly 90% accuracy within 2k steps, whereas SFT from scratch reaches only ~30% at 20k steps.
  3. Large-scale omni-modal manipulation dataset OmniAction: 141,162 episodes, 112 skills, 748 objects, 5,096 voice timbres, 2,482 sound events, and 640 background soundscapes, filling the gap in "audio + inferential instruction" data and providing a public resource for subsequent research.

Among end-to-end models, dyadic and overlapping tasks are easier (~88%), while non-verbal is the hardest (~82%). Under ground-truth textual prompts, OpenVLA achieves only 2.6%, NORA 16.3%, and π0 3.0%, indicating that baselines already perform poorly with explicit instructions and nearly collapse when faced with contextual instructions.

Intent Recognition and Efficiency

  • Intent recognition accuracy: RoboOmni 88.9% vs ASR+GPT-4o 55.6% vs Qwen2.5-Omni-7B 50.0%.
  • Inference latency (ASR+OpenVLA as 1.0×): RoboOmni 0.49×, ASR+π0 0.96×—end-to-end eliminates ASR and cascaded communication overhead.

Boundaries and Limitations

The paper does not explicitly declare limitations. Inferred from factual points: real-world experiments were validated only on the WidowX 250S single-arm platform, without dual-arm, mobile manipulation, or dynamic environments; the data focuses on household pick-and-place tasks (112 skills), with no fine manipulation (e.g., assembly, suturing) or non-rigid object manipulation; the six instruction types cover common ambiguous scenarios, but real-world intent inference complexity far exceeds this scope (e.g., sarcasm, metaphor, cross-turn long-term memory). Training costs are high (64×A100×10 days pretraining), potentially limiting reproduction by small teams. No failure case analysis or generalization boundary tests were reported.

Engineering Insights

When reproducing, prioritize verifying three points: FAST+ tokenizer discretization parameters (the codebook quality of 2048 tokens directly determines action precision; validate reconstruction error on a small dataset first); batch interleaving ratio (imbalanced dialogue-to-action loss ratios may bias the model toward chatting over manipulation; the paper does not provide specific weights, requiring self-tuning); audio preprocessing pipeline (16kHz sampling rate, 224×224 image resolution, action chunk 6 are fixed configurations; changes may affect alignment with pretrained weights). The most likely pitfall is prompt engineering in OmniAction data generation—each of the six instruction types has strict constraints (e.g., sentiment cues prohibit imperative negations, identity cues must not directly describe actions at the end), and directly applying generic dialogue templates will break intent recoverability. For downstream SFT, start from the pretrained checkpoint rather than training from scratch; otherwise, accuracy reaches only 30% at 20k steps and degrades at 30k steps. Inference deployment on a single RTX 4090 achieves 0.49× latency, but note that Talker's speech synthesis quality may degrade in noisy environments; consider adding denoising preprocessing in real-world scenarios.
